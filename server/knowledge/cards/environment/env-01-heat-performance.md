## Summary
Heat reduces endurance performance even in well-hydrated riders. Blood is diverted to the skin for cooling, heart rate climbs, stroke volume falls, core temperature rises, and perceived effort increases, so sustainable power drops. Humidity makes it worse because sweat cannot evaporate. Riders who pace by their cool-weather power in the heat overreach early; pacing by heart rate and effort, cooling and acclimation recover much of the loss.

## Key numbers
- Sustainable power in events over about 30 min: typically 5–15% lower at 30–35 °C than at 15–20 °C in unacclimatised riders; more in high humidity or still air.
- Heart rate at a given power: +10–20 beats/min.
- Sweat rate: 1–2.5 L/h.
- Short efforts under about 2–3 min and sprints are not impaired and may improve with warm muscles.
- Outdoor cycling has strong convective cooling from airflow; climbing at low speed and indoor riding lose that benefit.
- Warning signs of heat illness: chills or goosebumps in the heat, dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, stopping sweating, stumbling. Confusion or collapse is an emergency: stop, shade, aggressive cooling, call for help.

## In practice
- Pacing: reduce power targets by about 3–5% at 25–28 °C and 5–10%+ above 30 °C if not acclimatised; cap heart rate at usual values for the effort and let power fall.
- Start hydrated; drink to a plan based on sweat rate; add sodium on long rides (see `nut-11-sodium`).
- Train early or late in the day; choose shaded or breezy routes; light, ventilated clothing; sunscreen.
- Expect lower numbers in files from hot days; they do not indicate lost fitness.
- Long steep climbs at low speed in direct sun are the highest-risk situation.

## Limits
Individual heat tolerance varies widely with fitness, body size, acclimation and medication. People with heart conditions or on medicines affecting sweating or blood pressure need medical advice.

## Coach guidance
Always check temperature before judging a ride. Put safety first: any description of confusion, collapse or stopped sweating gets an immediate stop-and-cool-and-seek-help message.
