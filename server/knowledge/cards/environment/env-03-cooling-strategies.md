## Summary
Cooling before and during exercise in the heat lowers starting core and skin temperature, increases the margin before critical temperatures are reached and reduces perceived effort. Meta-analyses show meaningful average improvements in endurance performance in hot conditions. The most practical methods for cyclists are cold drinks or ice slurry, ice in clothing, and water poured over the body, combined with the airflow that riding provides.

## Key numbers
- Average effect in the heat: roughly 4–7% improvement in endurance performance for pre-cooling and for cooling during exercise in pooled studies (larger in lab time-to-exhaustion tests than in real races).
- Ice slurry: about 7 g/kg (roughly 500 ml for 72 kg) over the 30 min before the start; lowers core temperature by about 0.3–0.6 °C.
- Ice vest or cold towels during warm-up and waiting: 10–30 min.
- During: cold fluids (bottles frozen half-full or insulated), water over head, neck, forearms and thighs; ice socks (ice in a stocking down the back of the jersey) at feed points.
- Menthol (mouth rinse or in drinks) reduces the sensation of heat without lowering temperature; modest performance effects reported.
- Warm-up in heat: shorten to 10–15 min, in shade, with cooling.

## In practice
- Indoor training: two fans directed at torso and face, a cool room, cold drinks; the biggest single upgrade for indoor power.
- Combine methods; they add up.
- Wetting clothing helps only when air is dry enough to evaporate; in very humid, still conditions prioritise cold drinks and ice.
- Practise ice slurry and cold intake in training: some riders get stomach cramps or headaches.
- Cooling the feeling without cooling the body (menthol) should not be used to override warning signs of heat illness.
- After the finish: shade, cold drinks, wet towels; cool down before a hot shower.

## Limits
Benefits are smaller outdoors at speed than in still-air lab studies; logistics often limit what is possible mid-event.

## Coach guidance
For hot events, add a cooling plan to the race plan: what before the start, what at each feed point.
