## Summary
Body temperature, alertness and muscle function follow a daily rhythm, and for most people physical performance is somewhat better in the late afternoon and early evening than soon after waking. The difference is modest and can be largely trained away: regularly training at a given time of day improves performance at that time. This matters for early-morning events and after crossing time zones, when the body clock and the start time disagree.

## Key numbers
- Typical time-of-day effect on maximal and high-intensity performance: about 3–10% lower in the early morning than late afternoon in lab studies; smaller for long steady endurance.
- Core temperature is lowest about 2–3 h before habitual wake time and peaks in the early evening.
- Training at the event's time of day for 2–4 weeks reduces or removes the morning deficit.
- A longer warm-up and caffeine partly offset early-morning deficits.
- Chronotype matters: "morning types" lose less in the morning; "evening types" lose more.
- After travel, performance follows the home clock until adapted: an event at 08:00 local time after flying 6 zones east is at 02:00 body time on arrival. Adaptation takes roughly 1 day per zone eastward, less westward (see `rec-10-travel-jet-lag`).

## In practice
- For early starts: do key sessions and some long rides in the morning in the final 3–4 weeks; rehearse the early breakfast.
- Wake at least 3 h before the start where possible; bright light, movement and a full warm-up.
- The best time to train is the time that can be kept consistently; consistency outweighs circadian optimisation.
- Hard evening sessions can delay sleep in some riders; finish 2–3 h before bed or shift them earlier.
- After westward travel, evening events feel worst; after eastward travel, morning events do. Schedule arrival accordingly.
- Shift workers: anchor training to the most stable part of the schedule and protect sleep first.

## Limits
Lab findings on time-of-day effects are larger than what is seen in real competition; individual variation is large.

## Coach guidance
Plan sessions around the rider's life first. For a fixed early or late event, add time-of-day rehearsal to the last weeks.
