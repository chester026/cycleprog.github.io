## Summary
Cool air is good for endurance performance; real cold is not. When muscles and hands get cold, power, coordination and braking control fall, and wet clothing in wind strips heat many times faster than dry cold. The main risks in cycling are long descents after sweaty climbs, rain at low temperatures, and stops. Clothing strategy, layers that manage sweat and block wind, is the decisive factor.

## Key numbers
- Best endurance performance occurs at about 10–15 °C air temperature.
- Below about 5 °C with wind, or in rain under about 10 °C, expect lower power and greater energy use; maximal and sprint power fall as muscle temperature drops.
- Wind chill at 35 km/h makes 5 °C feel like about −1 °C; on a 60 km/h descent, colder still.
- Wet clothing increases heat loss several-fold.
- Carbohydrate use rises with shivering and cold stress: keep intake at 60+ g/h on long cold rides; drink even though thirst is low.
- Hypothermia signs: uncontrollable shivering, clumsy hands, slurred speech, apathy, confusion. Stop, get shelter and dry layers, warm drinks; confusion is an emergency.
- Cold air can trigger airway narrowing in susceptible riders, especially at high intensity.

## In practice
- Layers: wicking base, insulating mid, wind- and waterproof outer; cover hands, feet, ears and knees (knees below about 12–15 °C).
- Start slightly cool; carry a packable jacket and put it on before descents and stops.
- Warm up longer; keep intensity steady rather than stop-start to avoid sweat-then-chill.
- Wet roads: lower tyre pressure slightly, brake earlier, mudguards and lights; avoid painted lines and metal covers.
- Hard intervals are often better indoors in winter; use outdoor rides for LOW volume.
- Change out of wet kit immediately after riding.
- Ice risk: do not ride on the road when surfaces may be frozen.

## Limits
Research on cycling performance in cold is limited; tolerance depends on body size and composition, with smaller, leaner riders cooling faster. Raynaud's phenomenon and cold-induced asthma need individual medical advice.

## Coach guidance
On cold or wet days, interpret lower power neutrally and prioritise safety: suggest indoor alternatives without making outdoor riding sound like a failure of resolve.
