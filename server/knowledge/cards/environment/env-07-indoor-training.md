## Summary
Indoor training removes traffic, weather, coasting and terrain, which makes it the most time-efficient way to execute structured intervals precisely. Its weaknesses are heat build-up, monotony, a fixed position and the absence of skills practice. With good cooling and sensible session choice it can carry a large share of quality work; durability, handling and long-ride fuelling still need the road.

## Key numbers
- No coasting: 60 min indoors is roughly comparable in pedalling time to 70–80 min outdoors on rolling terrain.
- Cooling: without fans, heart rate drifts 10–20 beats and power drops within 20–30 min; two strong fans and a room under about 18–20 °C largely prevent it.
- Sweat rates of 1–1.5 L/h are common: drink 500–1000 ml/h with sodium on longer sessions.
- Session length: most riders tolerate 45–90 min well; long indoor rides (2–3 h) are possible with entertainment and breaks from the saddle every 10–15 min.
- ERG mode holds power regardless of cadence: ideal for steady MID and HIGH intervals of 2 min+; poor for sprints and short on/off efforts (trainer lag 2–5 s) and can cause a "spiral" when cadence drops.
- Resistance or slope mode teaches pacing and suits sprints, short intervals and tests.

## In practice
- Best indoor sessions: sweet spot, threshold, VO2max and short-interval sets, recovery spins, cadence drills.
- Vary cadence and stand periodically; a rocker plate or riser reduces saddle pressure.
- Virtual racing and group rides provide hard, variable efforts but tend to become unplanned high intensity; count them as quality days.
- Use the same power source as outdoors where possible (see `zt-13-indoor-vs-outdoor-power`).
- Keep at least one outdoor ride a week when conditions allow, for skills, position and enjoyment.
- A towel, a mat and wiping down the bike protect equipment from sweat corrosion.

## Limits
Little research compares long-term indoor versus outdoor training outcomes; recommendations are practical.

## Coach guidance
Treat indoor rides as fully valid training. When indoor heart rate drifts or power fades, ask about fans and room temperature before changing targets.
