## Summary
Because the rider's body creates most of the drag, what covers it matters. Loose, flapping fabric is the largest avoidable aerodynamic cost in everyday riding; a close-fitting jersey or a skinsuit removes it. Helmets differ by a smaller but real margin. These gains apply at any level, grow with speed, and cost far less than frames or wheels. Thermal comfort still comes first: an overheated rider loses more than any fabric saves.

## Key numbers (at about 40 km/h; roughly half at 30 km/h)
- Flapping jacket or loose jersey versus fitted jersey: 15–40 W.
- Fitted jersey and shorts versus a good skinsuit: 5–15 W.
- Standard vented road helmet versus aero road helmet: 3–10 W; versus a time-trial helmet in a time-trial position: 8–20 W (only if the head is held steady and the tail sits flat).
- Aero socks and shoe covers: 2–6 W.
- Gloves, zips, pinned numbers, cables and stuffed pockets: each 1–3 W; a flapping race number 5+ W.
- Shaved legs: small and variable, a few watts in some tests.
- Unzipped jersey on a descent or flat: 10–20 W.

## In practice
- Fit matters more than brand: no wrinkles on shoulders and arms in the riding position.
- Size down gilets and jackets for racing; stow them fully when not worn.
- Choose helmet by temperature and course: aero for flat and fast, ventilated for long hot climbs.
- Pin or stick numbers flat.
- For hot events prioritise ventilation, light colours and sun protection.
- In cold or wet conditions, staying warm preserves power; hands and core first.

## Limits
Published savings depend on rider, position, speed and test method; individual testing often gives different rankings. Fabric rules apply in sanctioned races.

## Coach guidance
Suggest fitted clothing as an early, inexpensive improvement for riders interested in speed. Never trade safety equipment or thermal safety for drag.
