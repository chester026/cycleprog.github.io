## Summary
Riding behind another cyclist reduces air resistance dramatically, and because air resistance is most of the cost on flat roads, the power saving is large. The effect grows with speed and with the size of the group and falls quickly as the gap opens. It is the reason group rides cover ground so much faster than solo rides for the same effort, and the foundation of all road-race tactics.

## Key numbers
- Behind one rider at a wheel gap of about 0.2–0.5 m: drag reduced by roughly 30–45%; power saved about 25–35% at 35–40 km/h.
- Third and fourth positions in a line: slightly more saving than second.
- Inside a large peloton: simulations and wind-tunnel tests report drag as low as 5–10% of a solo rider in the best positions; realistic power savings of 50–70% in the heart of a big bunch.
- Gap sensitivity: most of the benefit is gone beyond about 2–3 m; at 1 m it is still substantial.
- The lead rider gains a small benefit (about 2–3%) from a rider close behind.
- Climbing: at 15–20 km/h the saving is about 5–10%; meaningful into a headwind.
- Crosswind: shelter moves to the downwind side; overlap wheels diagonally only in an organised echelon.
- Typical effect on a ride: 2–4 km/h faster average at the same normalized power in a cooperative group.

## In practice
- Hold a steady gap by soft-pedalling and moving slightly out of the draft rather than braking.
- Look past the wheel in front, not at it; keep hands near the brakes.
- In a rotating paceline, keep the pace constant when taking the front; pull off into the wind; turns of 20–60 s.
- In long events, time spent in groups on flat sections is the cheapest way to save energy for climbs.
- Group riding requires predictable behaviour: no sudden braking, signal hazards, do not overlap wheels.

## Limits
Savings depend on rider sizes, speed, wind and group discipline; solo training and non-drafting events (time trials, many triathlons) get none.

## Coach guidance
When comparing rides, account for drafting before judging speed or effort. Encourage skills practice with experienced groups.
