## Summary
Speed can be bought, but the price per watt varies a hundredfold. The best returns come from things that cost nothing or little: position, tyre choice and pressure, fitted clothing, a clean drivetrain. Frames and wheels come last. Training, pacing and fuelling outweigh all of it for most amateurs. The ranking below is an approximate synthesis for flat-to-rolling riding at 35–40 km/h; exact values depend on rider and conditions.

## Approximate ranking (watts saved at about 40 km/h)
| Change | Saving | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Lower, narrower position on existing bike | 20–40 W | free (practice) |
| Fitted jersey instead of loose | 10–30 W | low |
| Fast tyres + latex/TPU tubes or tubeless, correct pressure | 10–20 W | low |
| Clean, well-lubricated chain | 3–8 W | very low |
| Aero road helmet | 3–10 W | medium |
| Skinsuit, aero socks | 5–15 W | medium |
| Clip-on aero bars (where allowed) | 30–60 W | low–medium |
| Deep-section wheels | 5–15 W | high |
| Aero frame | 5–15 W | very high |
| 1 kg lighter bike | 0–1 W on flat; about 1% on steep climbs | high |

## Non-equipment gains for comparison
- Even pacing instead of a too-fast start: often minutes in a long event.
- Fuelling at 80–90 g/h instead of 30–40 g/h in events over 3 h: the difference between finishing strong and fading 10–20%.
- A season of consistent training: 5–10% of threshold power for a developing rider (15–30 W).

## In practice
- Start at the top of the table and stop when the cost per watt stops making sense for the rider.
- For mountainous events, re-rank: gearing, tyres, carried weight, then aerodynamics.
- Comfort and reliability first: a puncture or a position that cannot be held erases any gain.
- Buy for the bike and riding the rider actually does.

## Limits
Figures are rounded from published tests and practice; savings are not simply additive and shrink at lower speeds.

## Coach guidance
Answer upgrade questions by pointing first to free and cheap items and to training. Do not pressure anyone toward purchases; riding well on modest equipment is fully valid.
