## Summary
CdA, drag coefficient times frontal area, is the single number that describes how much air a rider has to push. The rider's body accounts for roughly 70–80% of total drag, so position changes outweigh any equipment purchase. Lowering the torso, narrowing the arms and keeping the head low can save tens of watts at ordinary riding speeds, provided the position can be held and power is not lost.

## Key numbers (typical CdA, m²)
- Upright on the tops: 0.38–0.45
- Hoods, arms straight: 0.33–0.38
- Drops: 0.30–0.34
- Hoods with forearms horizontal ("aero hoods"): 0.27–0.31
- Time-trial position: 0.20–0.26 (elite 0.17–0.21)
- Power saved: at 35 km/h each 0.01 m² of CdA is worth about 5–6 W; at 40 km/h about 8 W; at 45 km/h about 11–12 W.
- Moving from straight-arm hoods to aero hoods: roughly 20–40 W at 40 km/h.
- Narrower hand position (within what is safe and legal) and a level, tucked head each save several watts.

## In practice
- Bent elbows with a flat back on the hoods is usually more aerodynamic than the drops with straight arms.
- Build tolerance gradually: 5–10 min blocks in the aero position during endurance rides, then at tempo.
- Check that power holds: a position that costs 15 W of output to save 10 W of drag is slower.
- Hip angle is the usual limiter; saddle position, shorter cranks and flexibility work can help (see `bike-08-bike-fit`).
- Use the low position where speed is high (flats, descents, headwinds); sit up on steep climbs for comfort and breathing.
- Field testing (repeated loops at constant power, or virtual-elevation methods) can rank positions if conditions are calm.

## Limits
Individual results vary; the same change can help one rider and not another. Published values depend on measurement method. Some extreme positions are banned in competition or unsafe on open roads.

## Coach guidance
Recommend position practice before equipment. Tie the expected gain to the rider's actual speeds and event.
