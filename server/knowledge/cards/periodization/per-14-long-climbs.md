## Summary
On long climbs speed is governed by power relative to total mass, and by how well that power survives repeated climbs and altitude. Climbs of 30–90 minutes are sustained MID efforts, so the core preparation is long sweet-spot and threshold work, long rides with accumulated climbing, sensible gearing and pacing below the red line.

## Key numbers
- Climbing speed: on gradients above about 6–7%, roughly 80–90% of power goes against gravity; each 1% change in total system mass (rider + bike + kit) changes climbing time by about 1% at the same power.
- Vertical speed (VAM) rough guide on 7–9% gradients: VAM (m/h) ≈ W/kg × 280–300 (e.g. 3.0 W/kg ≈ 840–900 m/h).
- Pacing a single long climb: 85–95% FTP for 30–60 min when it is the only one; 75–85% when several follow.
- Altitude: sustainable power falls about 1% per 100 m above roughly 1000–1500 m for unacclimatised riders (about 5–8% at 2000 m, 10–15% at 2500–3000 m).
- Gearing: plan for 70–85 rpm at target power on the steepest long sections; for many amateurs that means 34 × 32–34 or lower.
- Training blocks: 3 × 15 → 2 × 30 → 1 × 45–60 min at sweet spot; weekly long ride with 60–80% of event elevation.

## In practice
- No long climbs nearby: sustained efforts on a trainer with the front wheel raised, into headwinds, or low-cadence tempo to mimic the muscular demand.
- Stay seated for most of the climb; stand briefly every few minutes to relieve muscles.
- Start each climb at the low end of the range; overpacing the first climb is the classic error.
- Descending skill and clothing for cold descents matter for both time and safety.
- Weight loss: only gradual and only where there is something sensible to lose; power loss from under-fuelling outweighs the mass saved.

## Limits
VAM and altitude figures are approximations affected by wind, gradient and drafting.

## Coach guidance
Convert goals into required W/kg for the actual climbs and compare with fatigued power. Never push weight loss; raise it only if the rider does, and steer to gradual, well-fuelled approaches.
