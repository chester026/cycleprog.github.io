## Summary
Missed sessions are a normal part of training for anyone with a job and a family. A few missed days cost no measurable fitness, while trying to "make up" by cramming creates the fatigue and injury risk that actually derail progress. The rule is to move forward: keep the most important sessions of the week, drop the rest, and adjust the plan when a gap is longer.

## Key numbers
- 1–3 days missed: no loss; resume the plan as written.
- 4–7 days: negligible loss; resume, with the first quality session at about 90% of planned work time.
- 1–2 weeks: repeat the last completed week before progressing.
- 2–4 weeks: lower FTP about 5–8%, restart at 50–70% of prior load, rebuild over 2–4 weeks.
- Priority order within a week: (1) the key quality session, (2) the long ride, (3) the second quality session, (4) easy volume.
- Consistency target: completing about 80–90% of planned sessions over months is excellent.

## In practice
- Never stack two hard sessions on consecutive days just to catch up, and never add missed hours to the next week.
- If time is short on the day, shorten rather than skip: 30–40 min with the core of the intervals keeps the stimulus.
- If the reason is fatigue or illness, the missed session was the right training decision.
- Repeatedly missing the same session means the plan does not fit the rider's life; change the plan.
- Before an event, a missed final week matters little; fitness is already banked.

## Limits
Guidance is practice-based, consistent with detraining time courses.

## Coach guidance
Respond to missed training without any hint of blame. State what was not lost, and give the next concrete session.
