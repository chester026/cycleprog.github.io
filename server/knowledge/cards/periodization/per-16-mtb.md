## Summary
Cross-country Olympic (XCO) racing lasts about 80–100 minutes at very high average intensity with constant changes: steep climbs above threshold, technical descents with little pedalling but high whole-body effort, and a start that is close to maximal. Marathon events add duration. Fitness requirements combine a high VO2max and threshold relative to body mass with repeatability and technical skill; average heart rate is higher than power alone would suggest.

## Key numbers
- XCO: average heart rate about 88–92% of maximum; a large share of time above threshold power alternating with 20–30% of time not pedalling.
- Start: first 1–3 min well above VO2max power to gain position before singletrack.
- Climbs: typically 30 s–5 min, at 100–150% FTP.
- Power variability is extreme (variability index 1.3–1.5+); heart rate and RPE describe the load better than average power.
- Marathon (3–6 h): IF around 0.75–0.85 with repeated surges.
- Training week in build: 1 short-interval or VO2max session, 1 threshold or over-under session, 1 skills/race-simulation ride off-road, 1 long ride.

## In practice
- Train starts: 2–3 × (90 s all-out then 5–8 min at threshold).
- Do some intervals off-road on race-like terrain; standing, low-cadence, high-torque efforts are specific.
- Upper-body and core strength matter more than on the road.
- Skills sessions when fresh; skill under fatigue at the end of rides.
- Tyre pressure and suspension set-up change both speed and fatigue.
- Fuel in marathon events is harder to take on rough ground; plan feeding points and use drink carbohydrate.

## Limits
Power meters capture pedalling work only; descending cost and isometric effort are invisible in the file. Courses differ greatly.

## Coach guidance
Read MTB rides with heart rate and RPE first, power second. Count technical practice as real training.
