## Summary
Most cycling seasons are organised into three phases. Base builds aerobic volume and general strength. Build adds progressively harder and more event-specific intensity. Peak (with taper) reduces volume to shed fatigue while keeping intensity, so the rider arrives fresh. The structure is a convention that works; controlled comparisons in trained cyclists have not shown one periodization scheme to be clearly superior over 8–12 weeks.

## Key numbers
- Base: 8–16 weeks. Mostly LOW volume, rising 5–10% per week; 1 MID session (tempo/sweet spot); sprints; gym strength 2×/week.
- Build: 6–12 weeks in 3–4 week blocks. 2 quality sessions per week (threshold, VO2max, event-specific), long ride maintained, volume steady or slightly lower.
- Peak/taper: 1–2 weeks before the main event; volume −40–60%, intensity kept.
- Race period: for multi-event seasons, 1 quality session plus races, short rebuilds between targets.
- Transition: 1–3 weeks easy or off after the season.
- Count back from the event: a rider with 16 weeks might do 6 base, 8 build, 2 taper.

## In practice
- Base is not "easy only": a little intensity year-round maintains top-end ability and makes the build less of a shock.
- Riders with limited hours shorten base and rely more on MID work early.
- Fitness markers by phase: base → lower heart rate at endurance power, longer rides; build → rising 5–20 min power; peak → freshness, top-end sharpness.
- Riders without a target event can cycle 6–8 week emphases (endurance, threshold, VO2max) with an easy week between.

## Limits
Phase lengths are tradition-based. Seasonal weather, work and family schedules often dictate the plan more than theory.

## Coach guidance
Ask for the main event date and available weeks first; then say which phase the rider is in and what that phase is for.
