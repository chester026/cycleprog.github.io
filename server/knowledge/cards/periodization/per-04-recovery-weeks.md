## Summary
A recovery week is a planned reduction in load that lets accumulated fatigue clear so the adaptations from the preceding weeks can appear. It is standard practice across endurance sport and consistent with what is known about overreaching, although the classic "3 weeks on, 1 week off" pattern has not been tested against alternatives. The right frequency depends on how hard the loading weeks are, on age and on life stress.

## Key numbers
- Frequency: every 3rd or 4th week for most amateurs; every 3rd (or even 2 on/1 off) for masters riders, high-stress periods and very hard blocks; high-volume, low-intensity base periods can run 4–5 weeks.
- Depth: volume −30–50%, TSS about 50–65% of the previous week.
- Intensity: keep one short quality session (about half the usual work time) to stay sharp; drop the long ride to 60–70%.
- Duration: usually 5–7 days; TSB should return to around neutral or slightly positive.
- Good timing for a test: the last 2 days of the recovery week.

## Signs one is due now
Flat or falling power in key sessions for more than a week, elevated resting heart rate, suppressed HRV trend, poor sleep, irritability, loss of motivation, heavy legs on stairs, more illness.

## In practice
- Recovery weeks dictated by life (travel, work deadlines) count; move the plan around them.
- Riders on low volume (under about 5 h/week) with moderate intensity may need only occasional lighter weeks.
- Feeling slightly flat for the first days of a recovery week is common; freshness typically appears after 3–5 days.

## Limits
Individual needs vary and no trial defines optimal frequency or depth.

## Coach guidance
Protect recovery weeks: do not encourage riders to "use the freshness" for extra hard rides. Suggest an unplanned one when several fatigue signs coincide.
