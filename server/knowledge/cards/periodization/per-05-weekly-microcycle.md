## Summary
A good week has few moving parts: two quality sessions separated by easy days, one long ride, and the remaining time easy or off. Elite athletes follow the same skeleton with more volume around it. The week should fit the rider's life first; a plan that is followed at 90% beats an ideal plan followed at 60%.

## Key numbers
- Quality sessions: 2 per week (3 only for experienced riders in short blocks), at least 48 h apart.
- Long ride: 25–35% of weekly time.
- Rest: at least 1 full day off per week for most amateurs; 2 for beginners, masters and high-stress weeks.
- Typical 8 h week: Mon off · Tue VO2max or threshold 75 min · Wed easy 60 min · Thu sweet spot/threshold 75–90 min · Fri off or 45 min easy · Sat long ride 3 h · Sun easy 90 min.
- Typical 5 h week: Tue intervals 60 min · Thu intervals or tempo 60 min · Sat long ride 2–2.5 h · optional 30–45 min easy.

## In practice
- Put the most demanding session after a rest or easy day, when the rider is freshest and has time.
- Weekend warriors: Saturday hard group ride + Sunday long easy ride is a workable hard/long pair; then keep one midweek session.
- Strength training: same day as a hard ride (after it) or on an easy day, not the day before key intervals.
- A 7-day cycle is a convenience; shift workers and masters riders may do better on 9–10 day cycles.
- Repeat a stable weekly pattern for a block; change the content of sessions, not the whole layout.

## Limits
No research compares weekly layouts directly; this is practice built around recovery time courses.

## Coach guidance
Ask which days and how many hours are realistically available, then place the two key sessions and the long ride first. Treat everything else as optional volume.
