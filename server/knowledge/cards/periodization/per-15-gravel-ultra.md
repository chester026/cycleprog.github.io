## Summary
Events of 8 hours to several days are limited by durability, energy intake, contact-point tolerance and the head, not by threshold power. Intensity is low for nearly all of the time, so the engine is trained mainly with volume. What cannot be improvised on the day is eating, sitting, handling the surface and managing low points. Research specific to ultra-cycling is sparse; guidance comes from practice and from ultra-endurance studies in other sports.

## Key numbers
- Intensity: 50–65% FTP average for 8–12 h events; under 55% for 24 h+; short climbs up to 75–80%.
- Longest training ride: 60–70% of event duration for events up to about 12 h; for longer events, back-to-back days (e.g. 5 h + 5 h) rather than single extreme rides.
- Weekly volume in the peak block: ideally 1–1.5 times the event duration for single-day events.
- Fuel: 60–90 g carbohydrate per hour while intensity allows; in very long events appetite and gut limit intake to 40–70 g/h plus solid, savoury food. Total energy deficit is inevitable; the aim is to slow it.
- Fluid: 400–800 ml/h by conditions; sodium 300–600 mg per 500 ml in heat or heavy sweating.
- Gravel: rolling resistance and vibration raise energy cost; lower tyre pressure and wider tyres reduce fatigue.
- Taper: 10–14 days.

## In practice
- Keep one weekly session with some intensity (tempo, sweet spot) to hold power; otherwise prioritise hours.
- Train the gut and the routine: eat every 20–30 min from the first hour, alternate sweet and savoury.
- Rehearse night riding, navigation, repairs and the loaded bike.
- Protect contact points: bike fit, saddle choice, chamois cream, hand positions, shoes with room.
- Pace by heart rate and RPE in the first third; power targets drift downward over the day.
- Plan for low moments: they pass, usually after eating.

## Limits
Individual tolerance of sleep loss, heat and gut stress varies widely; severe symptoms (confusion, vomiting, chest pain) end the ride and need help.

## Coach guidance
Spend as much attention on logistics and nutrition as on training. Set process goals (eat on schedule, stay under a power cap) rather than time goals.
