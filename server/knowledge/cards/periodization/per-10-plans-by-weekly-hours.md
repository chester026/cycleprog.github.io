## Summary
Available time shapes the plan more than any theory. With few hours, most of the stimulus must come from intensity and consistency; with many hours, volume carries the load and intensity is a small, carefully placed share. Across bands the number of quality sessions stays about the same; what grows is easy volume and the long ride.

## Key numbers
| | 4–6 h | 7–10 h | 12+ h |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rides/week | 3–4 | 4–5 | 5–6 |
| Quality sessions | 2 (short) | 2 | 2, at times 3 |
| Long ride | 2–2.5 h | 3–4 h | 4–6 h |
| Typical quality | sweet spot, threshold, VO2max 4×4, 30/15 | threshold + VO2max | same + long tempo, back-to-back days |
| Main progression | interval time, then one longer ride | long ride, then interval time | volume, then density |
| Recovery week | every 4th, or as life dictates | every 3rd–4th | every 3rd–4th |
| Realistic CTL | 35–55 | 55–80 | 80–110+ |

## In practice
- 4–6 h: every ride has a purpose; avoid a third hard day; expect good gains for 6–12 months, then a plateau unless volume rises for a period. A few big weekends or one training week a year help.
- 7–10 h: enough for a classic LOW-based week and most amateur goals including gran fondos.
- 12+ h: protect easy days from creeping intensity; fuelling and sleep become the limiters.
- For long events on low hours: prioritise the weekend long ride above everything else in the last 8 weeks.
- Commuting counts as LOW volume; one commute a week can carry short efforts.

## Limits
Bands are practice-based. Recovery capacity (age, job, sleep, children) can make 6 h harder to absorb than 10 h for someone else.

## Coach guidance
Plan from the hours the rider has actually averaged over the last 6–8 weeks. If goals and hours do not match, say so kindly and offer the two ways out: adjust the goal or find specific extra time.
