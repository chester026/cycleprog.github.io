## Summary
Mass-start racing is stochastic: long periods in the draft at low power, punctuated by repeated efforts far above threshold, with the decisive moves often coming late. Success needs a solid threshold to sit in cheaply, the ability to repeat hard efforts with incomplete recovery, a sprint, and skill in the bunch. Training becomes more polarized and race-like in the final 6–8 weeks.

## Key numbers
- Typical amateur road race: IF 0.80–0.95, variability index 1.15–1.35, dozens of efforts above 120% FTP.
- Criterium (40–75 min): IF 0.90–1.05, a surge out of every corner, 30–50% of time coasting or soft-pedalling.
- Decisive efforts: often 30 s–5 min near maximal, then a finish sprint of 10–20 s after 1–3+ h.
- Build phase per week: 1 VO2max or short-interval session (e.g. 3 × 10 × 40/20), 1 race-simulation or anaerobic session (6–8 × 1 min, or over-unders with surges), sprints inside an endurance ride, 1 long ride.
- Final 2–3 weeks: races or fast group rides can replace one session.

## In practice
- Train repeatability: sets of short hard efforts with short recoveries, and efforts placed after 2 h of riding.
- Practise skills: cornering in a group, holding wheels, positioning, sprint timing; many races are lost on position, not watts.
- Sitting in saves 25–40% of power; the smartest watt is the one not spent.
- Analyse races by number and size of efforts above threshold and by when the rider was dropped or made the split.
- Warm up properly for criteriums: the start is the hardest part.

## Limits
Physical demands vary enormously with course, field and tactics; power data describe what happened, not what was optimal.

## Coach guidance
Pair training advice with tactical reflection on the rider's last race. Do not reduce race results to fitness alone.
