## Summary
A time trial rewards the highest steady power a rider can hold in an aerodynamic position, combined with even pacing. Two things set it apart from other events: the position, in which many riders initially lose power, and the absence of any tactical shelter. Preparation is threshold-centred and pyramidal, with as much quality work as possible done in the race position.

## Key numbers
- Sustainable power by duration (trained riders): about 105–110% FTP for 10–15 min; 100–105% for 20–30 min; 95–100% for 40–60 min.
- Position cost: 0–10% lower power in the TT position at first; narrows to 0–3% with 6–8 weeks of regular riding in position.
- Aerodynamics: at 40 km/h about 80–90% of resistance is air; a 5% reduction in CdA is worth roughly the same speed as a 5% rise in power on flat courses.
- Key sessions: 2 × 20 min at 95–100% in position; 3–4 × 10 min at 100–105%; race-pace efforts with short recoveries (5 × 5 min at 105%, 1 min rest).
- Pacing: start at target power, not above; within ±3% for the first quarter; push 5–10% harder on climbs and into headwinds, ease slightly on descents and tailwinds.
- Warm-up: 20–25 min including 2–3 short efforts near race power.

## In practice
- Ride in position at least once or twice a week in the last 8 weeks, including some easy riding to adapt neck, shoulders and hips.
- Rehearse the course or a similar profile, starts and turns.
- The first 2 min always feel easy; most blown time trials are lost there.
- Check equipment rules and practise with the race helmet and clothing.

## Limits
Aero gains need testing to confirm; an extreme position that cannot be held costs more than it saves.

## Coach guidance
Give a pacing plan in watts for each course section and a simple rule for the start.
