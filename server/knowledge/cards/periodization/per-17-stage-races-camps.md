## Summary
A training camp or multi-day event compresses a large load into consecutive days. Done well it produces a clear fitness step after recovery; done badly it produces illness or weeks of flatness. The keys are arriving rested, keeping most of the riding easy, eating and sleeping aggressively, and following with real recovery. Evidence from triathletes suggests that pushing into marked overreaching before a taper gives smaller gains than a hard but tolerable overload.

## Key numbers
- Camp load: 1.5–2.5 times normal weekly hours over 5–10 days is typical for amateurs; CTL rise of 8–15 points.
- Structure: 2–3 days on, 1 easy day; or 3 on, 1 easy, 3 on.
- Intensity: 80–90% LOW; quality limited to 2–3 sessions in a week-long camp, usually climbs at tempo/sweet spot.
- Daily carbohydrate: 6–10 g/kg; on-bike 60–90 g/h; protein 1.6–2 g/kg.
- Afterwards: 4–7 easy days; the benefit usually shows 1–3 weeks later.
- Timing: finish a camp 3–5 weeks before a key event.
- Multi-day events: pace day 1 at 5–10% under single-day targets; average power typically declines a few percent per day.

## In practice
- Lower the load in the week before the camp.
- Do not race companions on day 1; the camp is won on days 4–6.
- Sleep 8–9 h plus naps where possible; sunscreen, hydration and hygiene prevent the usual camp enders.
- Warning signs to back off: heart rate not responding, unusually high RPE, disturbed sleep, sore throat.
- Stage-race preparation: back-to-back long days and intensity on tired legs in the final 6 weeks.

## Limits
Tolerance of sudden load is highly individual; riders with little base are at most risk.

## Coach guidance
Before a camp, give three rules: easy means easy, eat more than seems necessary, and plan the recovery week now.
