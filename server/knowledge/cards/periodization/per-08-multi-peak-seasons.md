## Summary
A rider can be at a true peak only a few times a year, because each peak requires a taper that costs training and a recovery afterwards. Ranking events as A (full peak), B (short freshen-up) and C (train through) keeps the season coherent. Peaks can be held for roughly 2–3 weeks; after that fitness has to be rebuilt.

## Key numbers
- A events: 1–3 per season, at least 6–8 weeks apart for a full rebuild; or two A events 1–3 weeks apart sharing one peak.
- B events: 3–5 easy days before, no real taper; a few per season.
- C events: treated as hard training, placed where a quality session would be.
- After an A event: 3–7 easy days (longer after very long events), then 1–2 weeks of base-type riding before the next build.
- Between two peaks 8–12 weeks apart: 1 week recovery, 4–8 weeks rebuild (volume first, then intensity), 1–2 weeks taper.
- A peak maintained by racing alone erodes endurance after about 3–4 weeks.

## In practice
- Too many A events is the common planning error; riders end up always tapering or recovering and never building.
- Frequent weekend racing: use Tuesday–Thursday for one quality session and easy volume, and insert a 2–3 week race-free block mid-season to rebuild.
- After a disappointing A event, resist cramming; return to the normal cycle.
- Unplanned form (a great day in a C race) is information about readiness, not a reason to change targets.

## Limits
Entirely practice-based; individual ability to hold or repeat peaks varies.

## Coach guidance
Ask the rider to name their one or two most important events, then explain openly that the others will be raced on tired or only partly fresh legs.
