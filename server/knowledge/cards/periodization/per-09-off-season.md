## Summary
A short break after the season restores motivation and lets minor injuries and accumulated fatigue resolve. Professionals typically take 2–4 weeks with little or no structured riding. Some fitness is lost and returns quickly. For amateurs who train moderately all year a complete break is optional; a change of routine serves the same purpose. A small amount of intensity through the transition limits losses.

## Key numbers
- Break: 1–3 weeks very easy or off; up to 4 weeks after a long, heavy season.
- Expected loss after 2–3 weeks off: VO2max and threshold power about −4–8%; regained in roughly 3–6 weeks.
- In well-trained cyclists, one high-intensity session every 7–10 days during an 8-week transition preserved fitness better than low-intensity-only training and led to better early-season performance (between-group differences of roughly 10–12% in threshold power and 40-min power after the transition; small study).
- Body mass: a gain of 1–3 kg in the off-season is normal for racers.

## In practice
- Good off-season content: unstructured riding, mountain bike or gravel, running, hiking, skiing, swimming, and the start of gym strength work.
- It is the best window for heavy strength training, bike-fit changes, equipment changes and technique work.
- Review the season: what worked, limiters, next year's targets.
- Restart with 2–3 weeks of easy, gradually increasing volume before structured intervals; lower FTP about 5% for zone setting.
- Riders who feel fresh and motivated in autumn need less break; riders who dread the bike need more.

## Limits
Optimal break length is not established experimentally and is highly individual.

## Coach guidance
Give explicit permission to rest. Frame lower numbers on return as expected and temporary, with a timeline.
