## Summary
A gran fondo of 4–8 hours is decided by durability, pacing and fuelling far more than by peak power. Preparation centres on the long ride, on sustained MID efforts like the event's climbs, and on rehearsing nutrition. Riders rarely fail because their FTP was 10 W too low; they fail because they started too hard or ran out of fuel.

## Key numbers
- Timeline: 12–16 weeks is comfortable; 8 weeks is workable from a decent base.
- Long ride: build to 75–85% of expected event duration or energy (kJ) and at least 70–80% of its climbing, 2–3 times, the last one 2–3 weeks out.
- Weekly quality: 1 session of sweet spot/threshold (e.g. 3 × 15–20 min), 1 of tempo or VO2max depending on phase.
- Late-ride work: in the final 6 weeks, 2 × 20 min at 80–90% FTP in the last third of the long ride.
- Event pacing: flats and group sections under 70–75% FTP; long climbs 75–85% FTP in the first half of the event (NP for the whole event typically 0.65–0.78 IF for amateurs).
- Fuel: 60–90 g carbohydrate per hour from the start; 500–750 ml/h, more in heat; rehearse at least 3 times.
- Taper: 7–10 days.

## In practice
- Study the route: climb lengths and gradients decide gearing (aim for 70+ rpm on the steepest sustained sections) and the pacing plan.
- Practise eating while riding, descending, and riding in a group.
- Test all equipment, clothing and nutrition on long rides; nothing new on the day.
- The first hour should feel too easy.

## Limits
Guidelines are experience-based; individual durability and heat tolerance change the numbers.

## Coach guidance
Build the plan backwards from event duration and elevation. Keep returning to the three levers: long rides, pacing discipline, fuelling.
