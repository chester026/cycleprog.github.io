## Summary
Training works by applying a stress slightly beyond what the body is used to, then allowing recovery, during which it rebuilds to a somewhat higher level. Repeat with a gradually increasing stress and fitness rises; keep the stress constant and it plateaus; remove it and it declines. The principles are overload, recovery, specificity, individuality and reversibility. The tidy "supercompensation curve" is a teaching model: real adaptation is noisier, differs between body systems and between people.

## Key numbers
- Recovery time for the next quality session: about 24 h after LOW rides, 36–72 h after hard MID or HIGH sessions, longer for masters riders and after very long rides.
- Time course: plasma volume and enzymes respond in 1–2 weeks; threshold and VO2max over 4–8 weeks; structural changes over months and years.
- A given workout stops being an overload after roughly 3–6 repetitions unless it is progressed.
- Individual responses to an identical programme vary several-fold.

## In practice
- Overload one variable at a time: frequency, duration, intensity or density.
- Volume is the safest first lever; intensity is the most potent and the costliest.
- Plan the recovery as deliberately as the load; adaptation happens between sessions.
- Specificity increases as the event approaches: general fitness first, event-like demands last.
- Consistency over months beats any single big week.

## Limits
Predicting the timing of peak adaptation for an individual is unreliable; rigid long-range plans assume a predictability biology does not offer. Life stress adds to training stress and shares the same recovery capacity.

## Coach guidance
Explain plans in terms of stress and recovery, and adjust to the rider's actual response rather than defending the original schedule.
