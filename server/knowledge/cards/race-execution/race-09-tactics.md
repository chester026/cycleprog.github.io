## Summary
Mass-start races are decided less by who has the most power than by who has the most left at the decisive moment. Tactics are the management of energy and position: stay sheltered, be near the front when the race splits, and choose the few moments worth a maximal effort. The physics is on the side of patience: riding in the bunch costs a fraction of riding alone.

## Key numbers
- Drag in the bunch: behind one rider about 60–70% of solo drag; deep in a large peloton reported as low as 5–10% in simulation and wind-tunnel work, in practice roughly 30–50% of solo power at the same speed.
- Positioning zone: the front 10–25 riders in a field of 60–100: sheltered, out of the accordion effect, able to respond.
- Accordion effect at the back: every corner demands a sprint; variability index and efforts above threshold rise sharply.
- Crosswinds: shelter is diagonal; the field splits into echelons as wide as the road. Be at the front before exposed sections.
- Breakaway arithmetic: a group must be large and cooperative enough to match the bunch; solo moves succeed mostly late, on climbs, or when the bunch hesitates.
- Sprint: final effort 10–20 s; launch distance about 150–250 m depending on wind and gradient; start from a wheel, in the right gear, cadence 100–110 rpm.

## In practice
- Before the race: know the course's decisive points (climbs, narrow roads, wind, corners before the finish) and the wind direction.
- Move up before those points, when it is cheap, not during them.
- Spend "matches" deliberately: most riders have only a handful of efforts above 120% FTP before quality drops.
- In a break: short, smooth turns of 20–40 s; pull off into the wind; do not surge when coming through.
- Hold a line in corners and sprints; safety and predictability come first.
- After the race, note where energy was spent without a purpose.

## Limits
Tactics depend on field, course and team situation; rules of thumb do not replace race experience.

## Coach guidance
Review the race file together with the rider's account: when were the big efforts, and were they necessary? Encourage one tactical goal per race.
