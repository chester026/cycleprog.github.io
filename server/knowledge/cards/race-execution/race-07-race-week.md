## Summary
The final week cannot add fitness; it can only remove fatigue, keep the legs sharp and prevent avoidable mistakes. Training volume drops while short efforts at race intensity remain. Everything else in the week is about sleep, food, equipment and logistics.

## Key numbers
- Volume: about 40–60% of a normal week; frequency similar.
- Last real quality session: 4–6 days before (about half the usual work time at normal intensity).
- Day −3: easy 60–90 min. Day −2: rest or 30–45 min very easy. Day −1: openers.
- Openers (day before): 40–60 min easy including 3–5 × 30–60 s at about 110–130% FTP with full recovery, plus 2–3 short sprints. Aim: wake the legs, not train them.
- Carbohydrate: normal high-carbohydrate eating through the week; loading (10–12 g/kg/day) in the last 36–48 h for events over 90 min.
- Sleep: extra 30–60 min per night through the week; the night two days before is the important one.
- Travel: arrive at least a day early; allow 1 day per time zone where possible.

## Checklist
- Bike: service 5–7 days before, not the day before; new tyres/chain ridden at least once; check brake pads, bolts, batteries (shifting, power meter, head unit, lights).
- Kit for forecast and for 5 °C colder; spare tube/plug kit, CO2/pump, multitool.
- Nutrition purchased and tested; route loaded; start time, parking and registration known.
- Nothing new: no new saddle, shoes, position or foods.

## In practice
- Feeling flat or heavy midweek is common in a taper and unrelated to race-day form.
- Avoid long walks, heavy yard work and big sightseeing days in the last 48 h.
- Avoid large weight-loss attempts and alcohol in the final days.
- A short recon of the finish or key descent is worth more than an extra interval.

## Limits
Opener format is practice-based; some riders prefer complete rest two days out and openers the day before, others the reverse.

## Coach guidance
Provide a day-by-day plan and a short checklist. Calm pre-race doubts about lost fitness with the taper evidence.
