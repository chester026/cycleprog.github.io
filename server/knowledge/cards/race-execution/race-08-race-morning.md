## Summary
A fixed morning routine removes decisions on a day when nerves make decisions worse. Working backwards from the start time gives fixed points for waking, breakfast, travel, warm-up and the last intake. The warm-up is scaled to the event: the shorter and more intense the start, the more thorough it needs to be.

## Key numbers (T = start time)
- T −3 h to −2.5 h: breakfast, 2–3 g/kg carbohydrate, low in fibre and fat; 400–600 ml fluid.
- T −90 min: arrive; registration; bike check, tyre pressure for the day's conditions.
- T −60 to −45 min: caffeine if used (2–3 mg/kg); sip 300–500 ml with electrolytes.
- T −40 to −10 min: warm-up.
  - Time trial, criterium, hill climb, cyclocross, XCO: 20–30 min: 10–12 min building to 70–75% FTP; 3 × 1 min at 100–110% with 2 min easy; 2–3 × 10 s accelerations; 5 min easy.
  - Road race with neutral or steady start: 10–15 min easy with 2–3 short pick-ups.
  - Gran fondo and longer: 5–10 min easy or none.
- T −10 to −5 min: gel or 20–30 g carbohydrate; toilet; remove extra layers at the last moment.
- Heat: shorter warm-up in shade, cold drinks, ice in pockets or ice vest; cold: longer warm-up, stay covered until the start.

## In practice
- Lay everything out the night before: numbers pinned, bottles filled, food in pockets, head unit charged and route loaded.
- Line up early in mass starts where position matters; in large fondos a calm start further back costs little.
- Nerves and an elevated heart rate at the start are normal and fade within minutes.
- Have a plan for the first 10 minutes: power cap, wheels to follow or let go.
- Rehearse the whole routine before a hard training session or a low-priority event.

## Limits
Timings are conventions; individual gut tolerance sets the breakfast gap.

## Coach guidance
Produce a timeline anchored to the actual start time. Keep it to items the rider can tick off.
