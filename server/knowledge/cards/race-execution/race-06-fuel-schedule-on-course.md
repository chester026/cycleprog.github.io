## Summary
Knowing how many grams per hour to take is half the job; the other half is logistics: what is carried from the start, what is collected on the way, and at which points on the course eating is actually possible. Most fuelling failures in events are practical: full pockets but no hands free, a missed feed station, bottles that ran out before a long climb.

## Key numbers
- Carrying capacity: 2 bottles (1–1.5 L); pockets hold about 6–10 gels/bars (150–300 g carbohydrate). A 5 h event at 80 g/h needs 400 g: plan resupply or high-carbohydrate bottles (60–90 g each).
- Fluid between refill points: at 600 ml/h, two 750 ml bottles last about 2.5 h; less in heat.
- Timing: small intake every 15–20 min; a gel needs 150–250 ml water with it unless isotonic.
- Good moments: flats, false flats, steady early slopes of long climbs, straight descents with good visibility (drink only), the top of a climb before descending.
- Poor moments: technical descents, tight bunch riding, maximal efforts, the last steep kilometre.
- Before a long climb: eat 5–10 min before the base; the stomach handles it better at lower intensity.
- Feed stations: decide in advance which to use and for what (bottles only vs food); typical stop 1–3 min; skipping the first crowded station is often possible if carrying enough.

## In practice
- Open wrappers before the start; put items in the order they will be used.
- Use both drink mix and solid/gel sources so a dropped bottle is not a disaster.
- Practise taking food from pockets and bottles from cages at speed and in a group.
- In heat, alternate one bottle with carbohydrate and one with water/electrolyte to control concentration.
- Set a repeating alert on the head unit, or tie prompts to route sections.

## Limits
Station contents and spacing vary by event; rules on outside assistance differ.

## Coach guidance
Turn the hourly targets into a carried-items list and a per-section schedule. Ask where the feed stations are before finalising.
