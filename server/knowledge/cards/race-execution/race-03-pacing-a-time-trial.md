## Summary
A time trial is won by matching effort to where it buys the most time while never exceeding what can be sustained to the line. That means a controlled start, slightly more power where the bike is slow, slightly less where it is fast, quick re-acceleration after turns, and holding the aerodynamic position even when it hurts, since leaving it costs more than a few watts gain.

## Key numbers
- Target average: 10–15 min at 105–110% FTP; 20–30 min at 100–105%; 40–60 min at 95–100%.
- Start: reach race speed in 10–15 s, then settle at target within 30–45 s; first 2 min not above target +3%.
- Climbs and headwind sections: +5–10%; descents and tailwind: −5–10%; never coast unless spinning out.
- Turns and dead turns: brake late, accelerate 5–8 s out of the saddle or seated at 130–150% FTP, back in position.
- Final 10%: raise effort progressively; empty by the line.
- Sitting up to gain 10 W typically costs 20–40 W of aerodynamic drag at 40+ km/h.
- Out-and-back in wind: push the headwind leg harder; average power is the same but average speed rises.

## In practice
- Break the course into 3–6 sections with a power target for each; write it on the stem or set head-unit alerts.
- Display lap power and time/distance; hide speed if it tempts over-pacing with a tailwind.
- Recon corners, surface and wind exposure; know the last kilometre.
- Warm up thoroughly (see `wko-20-warm-up-cool-down`): the first minutes are aerobic sooner.
- Mental cue for the middle third, where pace usually sags: hold position, hold cadence.

## Limits
Wind shifts change the plan; riders should know the principle and adapt.

## Coach guidance
After a time trial, review power by section against plan, especially the first two minutes and the middle third.
