## Summary
In events of three hours or more the main risk is spending too much too early. Sustainable intensity falls steadily with duration, and thresholds themselves drift down as the ride goes on. A good long-event pace feels restrained for the first half, keeps power variability low, and leaves the rider able to hold power on the last climb. The plan is framed as caps, not targets.

## Key numbers
- Sustainable whole-event Intensity Factor (trained amateur, well fuelled): 2 h 0.83–0.90; 3 h 0.78–0.85; 4 h 0.73–0.80; 5–6 h 0.68–0.75; 7–9 h 0.62–0.70; 10 h+ 0.55–0.65. Less experienced riders sit 0.03–0.05 lower.
- Caps: flats and rolling terrain 65–75% FTP; long climbs 75–85% in the first two-thirds; short rises under 2 min up to 95–100%; avoid any effort above 110% in the first half.
- Variability index: aim under about 1.10–1.15 on hilly courses.
- Heart rate: keep below the top of MID on climbs in the first half; rising heart rate at falling power later signals heat, dehydration or under-fuelling.
- Expected fade: plan the last third at 5–10% lower power targets.
- TSS orientation: 3 h 150–200; 5 h 220–300; 8 h 300–400.

## In practice
- Start-line adrenaline and fast groups push riders 10–20% over plan in the first 30 min; decide in advance which wheels to let go.
- Use groups on flats to save energy, not to ride harder than planned.
- Eat and drink on schedule from the first 20 min.
- Split the event mentally into thirds: restrain, maintain, spend what is left.
- If it goes wrong (bonk, cramps): ease to LOW, eat and drink, rebuild gradually.

## Limits
Values are practice-based and assume temperate conditions, current FTP and good fuelling; heat or altitude lower them.

## Coach guidance
Derive the plan from the rider's own long-ride data where available. Give caps per terrain type and one sentence for the start.
