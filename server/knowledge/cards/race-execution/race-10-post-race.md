## Summary
What happens after the finish determines how quickly the rider can train again and how much is learned from the event. Recovery starts with food, fluid and sleep; analysis works best a day later, combining the file with the rider's own account. The emotional side matters too: a flat or low feeling in the days after a big goal is common.

## Key numbers
- First hour: 1–1.2 g/kg carbohydrate, 20–40 g protein, fluid with sodium to replace about 125–150% of the weight lost.
- Easy days before the next quality session: short race or time trial 1–2; hard road race or 3–5 h fondo 2–4; 6 h+ or mountainous event 4–7; multi-day event 7–10.
- First ride back: 30–60 min very easy within 1–2 days if desired.
- Signs recovery is incomplete: elevated resting heart rate, heavy legs on stairs, poor sleep, low motivation, heart rate slow to rise.

## What to look at in the file
- Plan versus execution: power in the first 10–20% of the event; power per climb against targets; variability index.
- Fade: last-third power against first third; heart rate against power late on.
- Fuelling: actual intake against plan; where it slipped.
- Peaks: best 1-, 5-, 20-min power and when they occurred; new bests under fatigue.
- Decisive moments: where time or position was gained or lost and why.
- Conditions: temperature, wind, altitude, group dynamics.

## Debrief questions
What went well? What would you do differently? How did the pacing feel in the first hour? What did you eat and drink? Anything that hurt or malfunctioned?

## In practice
- Record three lessons while they are fresh; use them in the next event's plan.
- A result below expectations is examined for causes (pacing, fuelling, conditions, illness, taper) before fitness is blamed.
- After an A event, take the planned easy period even when feeling good.

## Limits
Race files capture effort, not tactics or luck; one result is a single data point.

## Coach guidance
Start with what the rider did well and with their own impressions. Present numbers as dynamics (how power held up, where it changed), and end with one or two concrete takeaways.
