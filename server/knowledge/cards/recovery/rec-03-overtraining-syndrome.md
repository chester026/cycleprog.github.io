## Summary
Overtraining syndrome is a prolonged state of underperformance and fatigue, lasting months, that follows an accumulation of training and non-training stress with inadequate recovery. It is rare, far rarer than riders fear, and it is a diagnosis of exclusion: illness, iron deficiency, low energy availability, infection, thyroid problems and depression can look the same and must be ruled out by a doctor. There is no test for it and no treatment other than rest and removing the stressors.

## Key numbers
- Duration: performance decrement lasting more than about 2 months despite reduced training; recovery can take many months.
- No blood, hormone or heart-rate marker is diagnostic on its own.
- Symptoms overlap heavily with relative energy deficiency; many suspected cases turn out to be under-fuelling.

## Typical picture
Persistent unexplained underperformance; fatigue not relieved by a week or two of rest; disturbed sleep; low mood, loss of motivation, irritability; frequent infections; altered appetite; heavy, sore muscles; heart rate that is unusually low or high in exercise; often a recent increase in training combined with life stress or monotony.

## Response
- Stop structured training; allow only light, enjoyable activity for weeks.
- Medical assessment to exclude other causes.
- Review energy and carbohydrate intake, sleep and psychological load.
- Return only when energy and mood have normalised, starting with short easy rides and adding volume before any intensity, over months.

## Prevention
Planned recovery weeks, variety in load, adequate carbohydrate, sufficient sleep, reducing training in periods of high life stress, and responding to early signs of overreaching.

## Limits
Definitions and mechanisms are still debated; the evidence base consists largely of case series and consensus.

## Coach guidance
Do not diagnose. When fatigue and underperformance persist beyond two weeks of easy training, recommend a doctor's visit clearly and stop prescribing intensity until that has happened.
