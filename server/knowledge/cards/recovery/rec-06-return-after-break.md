## Summary
After weeks or months away, the cardiovascular system returns faster than muscles, tendons and contact points, and motivation usually runs ahead of both. The safe pattern is to rebuild frequency first, then duration, then intensity, starting from a deliberately reduced load. Riders with years of training behind them regain fitness much faster than they first built it.

## Key numbers
- Starting load by time off: 2–4 weeks → 60–70% of previous weekly hours; 1–3 months → about 50%; 6+ months → treat as a new rider with a head start (3–4 short easy rides per week).
- Rebuild time: roughly equal to the time off for breaks up to about 2 months; previous level after a long layoff typically takes 2–4 months.
- FTP assumption for zones: −5–8% after 2–4 weeks; −10–15% after 2–3 months; then test after 2–3 weeks of riding.
- Progression: 2–3 weeks of LOW riding only; add tempo/sweet spot; reintroduce threshold and VO2max after 4–6 weeks.
- Weekly increase: about 10–15% in hours.

## In practice
- First rides should end with the feeling that more was possible.
- Expect higher heart rate at given power and earlier fatigue for 2–3 weeks; both normalise quickly.
- Saddle, hands, neck and lower back need re-adaptation: keep early rides short even if the legs feel good.
- After injury or surgery, the clinician's or physiotherapist's clearance and limits come first; pain during or after riding means step back.
- After a crash, check bike fit, cleats and helmet before resuming.
- Compare progress with last week, not with pre-break bests.

## Limits
Practice-based; return after specific injuries needs individual medical guidance that the coach cannot give.

## Coach guidance
Be warm about the return and conservative about the load. Show the regained ground week by week. Defer to medical advice on anything injury-related.
