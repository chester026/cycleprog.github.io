## Summary
Sleep, enough food (especially carbohydrate and protein) and sensible load management deliver nearly all recovery. Add-on methods mostly change how recovered a rider feels; effects on actual performance are small and inconsistent. Some are worth using in specific situations, such as between stages or in heat; none compensates for poor sleep or under-fuelling.

## Key numbers and verdicts
- Massage: best-supported for reducing perceived soreness and fatigue; little effect on measured performance.
- Compression garments: small reductions in soreness and perceived fatigue; possible small benefit to next-day performance; harmless.
- Cold-water immersion (10–15 °C for 10–15 min): reduces soreness and perceived fatigue; useful between efforts on the same or consecutive days and in heat. Regular use after strength training blunts muscle and strength gains; effects on endurance adaptation appear small.
- Foam rolling: short-term gains in range of motion and less soreness; no performance effect.
- Stretching after riding: does not reduce soreness or speed recovery.
- Sauna/heat: not a recovery method, but post-exercise heat exposure can support heat acclimation and plasma volume.
- Pneumatic compression boots, percussion devices: mostly perceptual effects; evidence thin.
- Anti-inflammatory drugs: do not improve recovery and may impair adaptation; not for routine use.

## In practice
- Priorities after a hard ride: carbohydrate 1–1.2 g/kg within the first hour if training again within 24 h, protein 20–40 g, fluid, then sleep.
- Use cold immersion or compression for stage races, double days, tournaments and travel; skip routine cold baths after gym sessions.
- If a method makes the rider feel better and has no downside, it has value; perceived recovery influences the next session.

## Limits
Trials are small, hard to blind, and measure different outcomes; placebo effects are substantial.

## Coach guidance
Steer attention and money to sleep and nutrition first. Do not disparage methods a rider enjoys; just be accurate about what they do.
