## Summary
Training through a systemic illness delays recovery and, with fever, carries a small but serious risk of heart muscle inflammation. The long-standing "neck check" is a reasonable guide: symptoms only above the neck allow light exercise; symptoms below the neck or fever mean rest. Returning is gradual, with volume before intensity. The fitness lost in a week of illness is small and returns quickly.

## Key numbers
- Above the neck only (runny nose, mild sore throat, sneezing), no fever: easy riding 30–60 min is acceptable if it feels fine after 10 min; no intensity.
- Below the neck (chest cough, muscle aches, stomach upset, swollen glands) or fever above about 37.5–38 °C: no training.
- After fever: wait until at least 24–48 h fever-free and feeling normal at rest before the first easy ride.
- Return ladder after a moderate viral illness: 2–3 days easy 30–45 min → 2–3 days normal-length LOW rides → first MID session at about 70% of usual work time → normal training. Roughly as many build-up days as days of illness.
- After significant flu or COVID-type illness: at least 7–10 days from symptom onset before progressive return; step back if symptoms return.
- Infection risk rises after very long or very hard sessions, sharp load increases, poor sleep, travel and low energy intake.

## Stop and seek medical care
Chest pain or tightness, palpitations, breathlessness out of proportion, fainting or near-fainting, during or after illness. These need a doctor before any further training.

## In practice
- Reduce FTP expectations by a few percent for the first week; heart rate at a given power will be elevated.
- Hydration, sleep and normal eating matter more than any supplement.
- Protect others: skip group rides while contagious.

## Limits
The neck check is a convention with little direct research. Guidance after COVID-type illness evolved quickly and is consensus-based.

## Coach guidance
Advise rest without hedging when fever or chest symptoms are mentioned. Never suggest "sweating it out". Cardiac warning signs always go to a doctor.
