## Summary
A rest day is training: it is when the adaptation to the previous days is completed. Whether the day is spent completely off or with light movement makes little physiological difference; research does not show that active recovery restores performance faster than passive rest over 24 hours or more. The choice depends on what leaves the rider freshest in body and mind.

## Key numbers
- Most amateurs: 1–2 days per week without structured training.
- Beginners, masters riders and people with physically or mentally demanding jobs: usually 2.
- High-volume riders (12+ h): often 1 day off plus 1–2 very easy days.
- Active option: 20–45 min walking, easy spinning under 50% FTP, mobility work or easy swimming.
- Signs a rest day worked: lower resting heart rate, better mood, legs that feel springy in the next warm-up.

## In practice
- Place a rest day before the hardest session of the week or after the longest ride.
- Rest days are for the whole system: use them for sleep, proper meals and low stress, not for heavy yard work or long hikes.
- Riders who feel stale after a full day off often do better with a short spin and a few 10-s leg openers the day before a hard session.
- Two consecutive rest days every few weeks help riders who never quite freshen up.
- Guilt about resting is common; fitness is not lost in 1–3 days.

## Limits
Preferences and responses are individual; evidence comparing rest-day formats in cyclists is lacking.

## Coach guidance
Present rest days as planned and productive. If a rider trains every day and progress has stalled, suggest a real day off as an experiment for three weeks.
