## Summary
Young riders are not small adults. They grow at different rates, their performance is strongly influenced by maturation, and the main aims at this stage are enjoyment, skills, broad athletic development and a healthy relationship with sport. Consensus statements advise against early specialisation and against adult-style training loads; most successful adult endurance athletes had varied, moderate youth training. Training is best overseen by qualified coaches with parents involved.

## Key principles
- Variety first: several sports and disciplines (road, track, mountain bike, cyclocross, BMX, running, team sports) build skills and reduce overuse and burnout.
- Skills are the priority: bike handling, group riding, cornering, braking, gearing, race craft, safety.
- Volume and intensity grow gradually with age and maturity; structured intensity is introduced carefully in the mid-teens, with plenty of unstructured, playful riding.
- Results before and during puberty mostly reflect who matured earlier; late developers often catch up and overtake.
- Growth spurts temporarily reduce coordination and raise injury risk (knees, heels, back): reduce load during rapid growth and when pain appears; re-check bike fit often.
- Strength training with good technique and supervision is safe and beneficial for young athletes.
- Sleep needs are high (often 8–10 h).
- Food: growth plus training needs plenty of energy; no dieting, no weight targets, no body-composition talk.
- Racing regulations for youth categories (distances, gearing) exist to protect development and vary by country.

## In practice
- Keep feedback about effort, learning and enjoyment rather than outcomes.
- Watch for warning signs: loss of enjoyment, persistent fatigue, recurrent injuries, anxiety about performance, changes in eating, pressure from adults.
- Power meters and data are optional; perceived effort and fun work well.
- Medical care, not coaching, for persistent pain, delayed puberty, missed periods or signs of disordered eating.

## Limits
Training studies in youth cyclists are scarce; guidance rests on consensus and developmental research.

## Coach guidance
With a young rider, keep everything friendly, age-appropriate and focused on fun and skills; give no weight, diet or body advice; encourage involving parents and a club coach, and suggest a doctor for any health concern.
