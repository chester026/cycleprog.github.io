## Summary
Athletes arriving from running, rowing, skiing, swimming, football or the gym bring an engine or strength that cycling can use, but not all of it transfers at once. Central fitness (heart, blood) carries over fully; the muscles' cycling-specific endurance, pedalling economy and tolerance of the position must be built. These riders can usually handle more training than novices cardiovascularly, which lets them outrun what their legs, contact points and skills are ready for.

## Key numbers
- Runners and skiers: VO2max on the bike is initially about 5–10% lower than in their main sport and closes with cycling training over months.
- Heart-rate zones differ by sport: cycling maximum and threshold heart rates are typically 5–10 beats lower than running values; set cycling-specific zones.
- Local muscular fatigue limits early rides before breathing does: quadriceps burn at modest heart rates is typical for 4–8 weeks.
- Cycling allows far more volume than running because there is no impact: 6–10 h weeks are tolerable where running the same hours is not; build over 6–8 weeks anyway.
- Strength athletes: high peak power and sprint ability, limited aerobic endurance; long LOW rides are the main need.
- Rowers and skiers: usually transfer fastest because of similar leg extension patterns and large engines.

## In practice
- Start with a proper bike fit and gradual saddle time; contact-point problems, not fitness, end most early rides.
- Skills need deliberate practice: group riding, cornering, descending, gearing, clipping in.
- Use a ramp or 20-min test after 3–4 weeks to set cycling power zones; do not convert from running paces.
- Runners keeping some running retain bone loading; cyclists coming from non-impact sports should add strength and some impact work.
- Multi-sport athletes: count total load across sports; hard bike and hard run days compete for the same recovery.
- Expect quick gains for 3–6 months as specific adaptations catch up with the engine.

## Limits
Transfer varies with the sport, the individual and how long they have trained; data come mainly from triathletes.

## Coach guidance
Respect the athletic background and use it to set an ambitious but staged plan. Make skills and bike comfort explicit goals, not afterthoughts.
