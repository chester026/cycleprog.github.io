## Summary
In the first year almost any regular riding produces large gains, so the plan should optimise for one thing: still riding in twelve months. Frequency and gradual volume matter more than structure; comfort on the bike, basic skills and fuelling habits matter more than power targets. Beginners improve fastest of all riders, and are also the most likely to do too much too soon or to be put off by discomfort or comparison.

## Key numbers
- Typical first-year gains with consistent riding: FTP +10–25%, sometimes more; large falls in heart rate at a given speed within 6–8 weeks.
- Starting point: 3 rides per week, 30–60 min, mostly conversational effort.
- Progression: add about 10–15% time per week; add a fourth ride or a longer weekend ride after 4–6 weeks; an easier week every 3–4 weeks.
- First structure (after 6–8 weeks of regular riding): one session a week with short efforts, e.g. 4–6 × 3 min "comfortably hard" with equal recovery, or 3 × 8 min at tempo.
- First test: a ramp test after 4–6 weeks; retest every 6–8 weeks because zones move quickly.
- Saddle soreness and hand or neck discomfort usually settle within 2–4 weeks; if not, check fit.

## In practice
- Use RPE and the talk test before introducing zones.
- Teach early: gearing and cadence (aim to spin rather than grind), braking and cornering, eating and drinking on rides over 75–90 min, basic puncture repair, road safety.
- Quality shorts and a basic fit check are the first equipment priorities.
- Variation in individual response is normal; progress is judged against the rider's own starting point.
- Celebrate process milestones: first 50 km, first hour non-stop, first climb without stopping.
- Adults starting exercise after a long sedentary period, with cardiovascular risk factors or symptoms, should have a medical check first.

## Limits
Guidance is practice-based; training response is highly individual and partly inherited.

## Coach guidance
Keep language simple and encouraging, avoid jargon and benchmarks against others, and suggest less rather than more when unsure. Show every improvement as dynamics from their own baseline.
