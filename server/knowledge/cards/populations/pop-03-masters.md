## Summary
Endurance performance declines slowly from the mid-30s, mainly through falling maximal heart rate and VO2max, and faster after about 60–70. Training greatly slows the decline, and riders who start or resume in midlife can keep improving for years. What changes most in practice is recovery: masters riders respond to the same training principles but need more time between hard sessions, lose strength and power faster, and benefit more from strength work, protein and sleep.

## Key numbers
- Endurance performance: declines about 5–10% per decade from the 40s in trained athletes; steeper after about 60–70.
- VO2max: about −10% per decade in inactive adults; roughly half that in those who continue hard training.
- Maximal heart rate: about −0.7 beats per year; zones should be re-anchored periodically.
- Lactate threshold as a fraction of VO2max and efficiency are relatively well preserved.
- Muscle power and sprint ability decline faster than endurance.
- Recovery between hard sessions: commonly 48–72 h rather than 24–48 h.
- Recovery weeks: every 3rd week, or 2 weeks on/1 easier.
- Strength: 2 sessions per week year-round; protein about 0.4 g/kg per meal.

## In practice
- Keep intensity: HIGH sessions are what preserve VO2max; reduce their frequency (often 1–2 per week), not their quality.
- Keep some sprints and fast-cadence work to hold neuromuscular power.
- Warm up longer; build volume more gradually; respect niggles early.
- Heat tolerance and thirst sensation decline with age: plan fluid.
- New or returning riders over 40–45, and anyone with cardiovascular risk factors or symptoms (chest discomfort, unusual breathlessness, palpitations, fainting), should have a medical check before hard training.
- Compare with age-group peers or with one's own last year, not with one's 25-year-old self.

## Limits
Much of the data are cross-sectional (different people at different ages); individual trajectories vary enormously.

## Coach guidance
Treat age as a parameter for recovery and strength, never as a reason to lower ambition. Be clear and calm about medical checks for symptoms.
