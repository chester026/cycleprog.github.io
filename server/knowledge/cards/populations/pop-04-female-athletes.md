## Summary
Women respond to endurance training with the same principles and similar relative gains as men; most guidance in this knowledge base applies unchanged. Research specific to women is thinner because women have been under-represented in studies. On the menstrual cycle, pooled evidence indicates that performance may be very slightly reduced in the first days of the cycle on average, but the effect is trivial, study quality is low and individual variation is large. General rules for training by cycle phase are therefore not supported; individual tracking is.

## Key numbers
- Meta-analysis in naturally cycling women: a trivial average reduction in performance during the early follicular phase (the days of menstruation) compared with other phases; no basis for general guidelines.
- Oral contraceptive users: on average a trivially lower performance than naturally cycling women, with large individual differences; no consistent difference between pill and pill-free days.
- Symptoms rather than hormones drive most real-world effects: cramps, heavy bleeding, bloating, poor sleep, low mood before menstruation.
- Core temperature is about 0.3–0.5 °C higher after ovulation, which can raise heart rate and thermal strain slightly in heat.
- Iron deficiency is considerably more common in menstruating athletes, especially with heavy periods.
- A regular cycle is a sign of adequate energy availability; periods that stop or become irregular (without contraception or menopause explaining it) are a medical signal, not a sign of fitness.
- Perimenopause and menopause: more variable recovery, sleep disturbance and heat intolerance for some; strength training and protein gain importance.

## In practice
- Offer the option of tracking cycle, symptoms and how sessions felt for 2–3 months to find personal patterns; adjust around bad symptom days, not by calendar rule.
- On high-symptom days swap a hard session for an easy one without it being a failure.
- Fuel well around training; avoid fasted hard sessions.
- Pregnancy and the postpartum period require guidance from the rider's healthcare provider.

## Limits
The evidence base is small and heterogeneous; popular phase-based training systems go beyond it.

## Coach guidance
Follow the rider's lead on how much to discuss. Never assume; ask what they have noticed. Missing periods, very heavy bleeding or severe pain are reasons to recommend a doctor.
