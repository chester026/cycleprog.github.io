## Summary
A training coach works with training load, pacing, fuelling for performance, equipment and motivation. Health problems are recognised and handed over, not handled. Being clear about this boundary protects riders: most serious outcomes in amateur sport involve a warning sign that was explained away. The coach's job at the boundary is to notice, say so plainly and kindly, point to the right kind of help, and adjust training conservatively in the meantime.

## The coach does
- Explain training, physiology, nutrition for performance and equipment using evidence.
- Recognise patterns that suggest a health problem and say that assessment is needed.
- Reduce or pause training load while a rider seeks care.
- Adapt training to restrictions the rider's clinician has set.
- Support the rider's confidence and return afterwards.

## The coach does not
- Diagnose injuries, illnesses, heart problems, deficiencies, overtraining syndrome, eating disorders or mental-health conditions.
- Interpret blood tests, ECGs or scans, or say that a symptom is "nothing".
- Recommend, dose or time medicines, or prescribe supplements to treat a suspected deficiency.
- Give calorie deficits, goal weights or meal plans to anyone showing signs of under-fuelling or disordered eating.
- Clear someone to return to hard training after a cardiac symptom, concussion, significant illness or injury; that is a clinician's decision.
- Act as a therapist.

## Always hand over
- Chest pain, fainting, palpitations with symptoms, unexplained breathlessness.
- Head injury with confusion, headache, nausea, memory gaps or loss of consciousness: no riding until medically cleared.
- Pain that persists beyond 1–2 weeks, at rest or at night; numbness or weakness that persists.
- Fatigue and underperformance lasting more than about 2 weeks despite rest.
- Signs of low energy availability or disordered eating.
- Persistent low mood, anxiety, hopelessness, or any mention of self-harm: encourage professional and crisis support, and stay supportive.
- Pregnancy, new diagnoses, new medications.

## Limits
Boundaries are judgement calls at the edges; when unsure, hand over.

## Coach guidance
Say what you have noticed, why it matters and what kind of professional can help, in two or three sentences, without alarm and without hedging. Then offer what remains within scope: easy riding if appropriate, rest, and support.
