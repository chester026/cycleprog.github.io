## Summary
Regular endurance exercise lowers cardiovascular risk overall, but hard efforts can expose an underlying heart problem, and serious events during sport are rare but real. A small set of symptoms should never be explained away as lack of fitness, a bad day or a faulty sensor. This card exists for recognition only: the response is always to stop exercising and get medical assessment.

## Stop now and call emergency services
- Chest pain, pressure or tightness during or after exertion, especially if it spreads to the arm, jaw or back, or comes with nausea, sweating or breathlessness.
- Fainting or near-fainting during exercise.
- Sudden severe breathlessness, or breathlessness at rest.
- Palpitations with dizziness, chest discomfort or collapse.

## Stop training and see a doctor before riding hard again
- Palpitations or a racing, irregular heartbeat that starts and stops suddenly, or heart rate that jumps far above expected for the effort and stays there (after excluding an obvious sensor fault).
- Unusual breathlessness or a marked, unexplained drop in exercise capacity over days to weeks.
- Fainting immediately after stopping exercise that recurs, or any unexplained loss of consciousness.
- Chest symptoms during or after a viral illness.
- Swollen ankles, breathlessness when lying flat.
- A family history of sudden death under about 50 or of inherited heart disease, if never evaluated.

## Context
- Risk rises with age, with known risk factors (high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, family history) and when starting hard training after a sedentary period; a check-up before intense training is advised for adults over about 35–40 with risk factors or symptoms.
- Heart-rate monitors do produce artefacts (spikes, cadence lock), but symptoms always outrank the device explanation.
- A low resting heart rate is normal in trained riders when there are no symptoms.

## Limits
This list cannot be complete; any symptom that feels wrong deserves a medical opinion.

## Coach guidance
If a rider mentions any of these, respond first with a clear, calm instruction to stop and seek care, urgent where listed. Do not interpret the symptom, reassure it away or continue with training advice until they have been assessed.
