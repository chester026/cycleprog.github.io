## Summary
When a rider feels unwell, the question is simple: train, rest or get medical help. The widely used "neck check" gives a workable rule for the first two, and a short list of warning signs covers the third. Resting through a short illness costs almost nothing in fitness; training through a systemic one can prolong it and, with fever, carries a small risk of serious complications.

## Quick decision table
| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Runny or blocked nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, no fever, feeling otherwise fine | Easy ride of 30–60 min is acceptable; stop if worse after 10 min; no intensity |
| Fever, chills, body aches, chest cough, chest congestion, vomiting or diarrhoea, swollen glands, marked fatigue | No training; rest and fluids |
| Just recovered from fever | Wait 24–48 h fever-free and feeling normal at rest, then follow the return ladder in `rec-05-illness-return` |
| Symptoms lasting more than about 7–10 days, getting worse, or returning after improving | See a doctor |
| Chest pain or tightness, palpitations, breathlessness out of proportion, fainting | Stop; urgent medical care (see `hlth-01-cardiac-red-flags`) |
| Severe headache with stiff neck, confusion, rash with fever, difficulty breathing, dehydration with inability to keep fluids down | Urgent medical care |

## Practical points
- Resting heart rate elevated by 7–10+ beats with feeling unwell usually confirms that rest is right.
- Group rides and shared indoor spaces while contagious spread illness to others.
- Hydrate, eat normally, sleep; avoid hard training for a few days after symptoms clear.
- Missed sessions are dropped, not made up.
- Frequent infections (more than about 3–4 a season) are a reason to review load, sleep, fuelling and stress, and to mention it to a doctor.

## Limits
The neck check is a convention with little direct research; individual illnesses differ.

## Coach guidance
Give a clear answer, not a balanced essay: rest when the table says rest. Offer no medication advice. When any urgent sign is described, lead with the instruction to seek care.
