## Summary
Discomfort where the body meets the saddle is one of the most common reasons riders cut rides short or stop altogether, and one of the least discussed. It ranges from pressure soreness and chafing to infected follicles and genital numbness. Most of it is preventable with the right saddle, position, shorts and hygiene. Research is limited, particularly for women, so advice rests largely on practice.

## Prevention basics
- **Saddle**: width matched to sit-bone spacing; shape and cut-out matched to position and anatomy; trying several is normal. More padding is not more comfort.
- **Position**: saddle level or 1–3° nose down; correct height (too high causes rocking and friction); reach that does not roll the pelvis onto soft tissue.
- **Shorts**: good-quality padded shorts that fit closely, worn without underwear; replace when the pad compresses or seams roughen.
- **Hygiene**: clean shorts every ride; out of them and washed soon after finishing; skin dry between rides.
- **Chamois cream**: reduces friction on long or wet rides.
- **Habits**: stand briefly every 10–15 min; increase ride length gradually; rest days when skin is irritated.
- Indoor training concentrates pressure: stand more often, use a fan, consider a rocker plate.

## Numbness
Genital numbness or tingling means nerve or blood-vessel compression. It should not be ignored or ridden through: stand up, then adjust saddle tilt, type or position. Numbness that recurs despite changes, or that lasts after rides, needs medical assessment.

## See a doctor when
A sore is very painful, hot, growing, discharging or accompanied by fever; a lump persists for more than about two weeks; there are recurrent infections; numbness persists off the bike; or there are urinary or sexual symptoms.

## Limits
Individual anatomy varies widely; no saddle or rule suits everyone.

## Coach guidance
Raise the topic matter-of-factly when a rider reports discomfort or cut-short rides, and normalise it. Suggest fit and equipment checks and time off the saddle to heal; recommend a doctor on the criteria above. Give no treatment advice for infections.
