## Summary
Many cyclists train with a long-term condition or regular medication. Most can ride and improve, and exercise is often part of treatment, but medicines and conditions change how the body responds and how data should be read. Decisions about medication, doses, timing and medical safety belong to the rider's doctor. The coach's role is to know which training assumptions no longer hold and to adjust how targets are set and interpreted.

## What changes in the data (awareness only)
- **Beta-blockers**: lower heart rate at rest and in exercise, often by 20–30 beats; heart-rate zones and formulas are invalid; use power and perceived effort.
- **Other blood-pressure medicines and diuretics**: can affect hydration, blood pressure on standing and heat tolerance.
- **Asthma**: cold, dry air, pollen and pollution can trigger symptoms; a longer warm-up helps many; inhaler use follows the medical plan.
- **Diabetes treated with insulin or certain tablets**: risk of low blood sugar during and after exercise; carbohydrate and dosing strategies are set with the diabetes team. Fuelling numbers in this knowledge base are for people without diabetes.
- **Thyroid, anaemia, and hormonal treatments**: can shift heart rate, energy and recovery.
- **Anti-inflammatory painkillers**: can mask pain, upset the gut and stress the kidneys when dehydrated; routine use around hard or long events is discouraged in sports medicine guidance.
- **Some antidepressants, antihistamines and stimulants**: can alter heart rate, sweating or heat tolerance.

## Anti-doping
Riders who compete in sanctioned events are responsible for checking every medicine and supplement against the current prohibited list and for therapeutic-use exemptions; this is done through their national anti-doping organisation and doctor.

## When to send the rider back to their doctor
Any new symptom during exercise; a change in medication; planning a big step up in training or an extreme event (heat, altitude, ultra distance); pregnancy; uncertainty about whether hard efforts are safe.

## Limits
Effects vary by drug, dose and person; this list is neither complete nor individual advice.

## Coach guidance
Never suggest starting, stopping, changing or timing a medication, and never comment on doses. Adapt the training method (power and RPE instead of heart rate, conservative heat and fuelling plans) and encourage the rider to share their training plans with their doctor.
