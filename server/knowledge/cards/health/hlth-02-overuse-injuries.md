## Summary
Cycling is gentle on the body, but repeating the same movement thousands of times an hour in a fixed position produces a familiar set of complaints. In professional road cyclists, lower-back pain and knee pain were the most common overuse problems, with knee pain causing the most lost training. Most cases relate to position, sudden increases in load or intensity, gearing or equipment changes. This card helps recognise patterns and the usual non-medical checks; it does not replace assessment of pain that persists.

## Common patterns and first checks
- **Front of the knee**: often associated with a saddle that is too low or too far forward, big gears at low cadence, or a rapid rise in climbing or intensity. Check saddle height, gearing, recent load.
- **Outside of the knee**: often associated with a saddle too high, cleat rotation or stance width. Check cleats and saddle height.
- **Back of the knee**: often with a saddle too high or too far back.
- **Lower back**: long reach or low bars, limited hip mobility, long climbs in big gears, weak trunk endurance.
- **Neck and shoulders**: bars too low or far, locked elbows, heavy helmet or looking up for long periods in a low position.
- **Hand numbness or tingling**: pressure on the palm; too much weight on the hands, bar shape, long periods without changing grip.
- **Foot numbness or hot spots**: tight shoes, cleat position, swelling in heat.
- **Achilles or calf**: saddle too high, cleats too far forward, sudden increase in standing climbing.

## Sensible first response
Reduce load and intensity, avoid the provoking position or gear, check for recent equipment or position changes, and return gradually when pain-free.

## See a physiotherapist or doctor when
Pain lasts more than about 1–2 weeks despite easing off, worsens, is present at rest or at night, comes with swelling, locking, giving way, numbness or weakness that persists off the bike, or follows a crash.

## Limits
Associations between fit and injury are based largely on clinical experience and observational data.

## Coach guidance
Do not name a diagnosis. Describe the common pattern, suggest reducing load and checking fit, and recommend professional assessment on the criteria above. Never advise training through pain.
