## Summary
Energy availability is the energy left for the body's normal functions after the cost of training is subtracted from food intake. When it stays too low, the body economises: hormones, bone turnover, immunity, digestion, mood and eventually performance are affected. This syndrome is called Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). It affects men and women, at every level, and often arises unintentionally in riders who increase training without eating more. The current consensus describes a spectrum: brief, mild shortfalls are tolerated; prolonged or severe ones cause harm.

## Why cyclists are at risk
Very high energy expenditure on long rides, a culture that prizes leanness and W/kg, fasted and low-carbohydrate training fashions, appetite suppression after hard sessions, and busy lives that crowd out meals. Low carbohydrate availability appears to be harmful in its own right.

## Warning signs
- Performance: stagnation or decline despite training, poor recovery, loss of top-end power.
- Body: persistent fatigue, frequent illness, recurrent or slow-healing injuries, stress fractures, feeling cold, gut problems, dizziness.
- Hormonal: missed or irregular periods; in men, low libido and fewer morning erections.
- Mind: irritability, low mood, poor concentration, disturbed sleep, anxiety around food, rigid eating rules, guilt after eating, compulsion to train.

## What helps
Restoring adequate energy and carbohydrate intake, especially around training, and reducing load until health markers recover. This needs assessment by a doctor, ideally with a sports dietitian; psychological support is part of treatment when eating behaviour is involved.

## Limits
Energy availability cannot be measured accurately outside a laboratory, so recognition relies on signs and clinical assessment. Symptoms overlap with overtraining, illness and iron deficiency.

## Coach guidance
This is a recognition-and-referral topic. When the pattern appears, express concern plainly and kindly, recommend seeing a doctor, and stop prescribing intensity or volume increases. Give no calorie figures, weight targets or meal plans. Fuelling guidance for rides and general encouragement to eat enough remain appropriate.
