## Summary
The meal before a ride tops up liver glycogen, which falls overnight, and settles hunger. For events longer than about 90 minutes, loading muscle glycogen in the preceding 1–2 days improves performance. Modern loading needs no depletion phase: high carbohydrate intake plus reduced training is enough.

## Key numbers
- Pre-ride meal: 1–4 g/kg carbohydrate, 1–4 h before; the closer to the start, the smaller and lower in fibre, fat and protein. Typical: 2–3 g/kg about 3 h before.
- Early starts: 1 g/kg 60–90 min before, plus carbohydrate from the first minutes of riding.
- Final top-up: 20–30 g in the last 10–15 min before the start is fine.
- Carbohydrate loading: 10–12 g/kg per day for 36–48 h before events over 90 min, with light training. Expect 1–2 kg of extra body mass from stored glycogen and water.
- Events under about 90 min: normal high-carbohydrate eating the day before (7–8 g/kg) is sufficient.
- Fluid: 5–7 ml/kg in the 2–4 h before.

## In practice
- Familiar, easily digested foods: rice, white bread, oats, pancakes, bananas, jam, honey, juice; limit fibre in the last 24 h.
- Spread loading over meals, snacks and drinks; liquid carbohydrate helps reach the totals.
- A brief dip in blood sugar early in exercise after a carbohydrate snack 30–60 min before affects a minority of riders; they should eat earlier or right at the start.
- For ordinary training rides a normal meal 2–3 h before is enough; easy rides can follow a light snack or nothing.

## Limits
Loading totals are hard to reach for some riders and may cause bloating; women may need the higher end of intake to achieve the same storage.

## Coach guidance
Give the plan as meals, not only as grams. Rehearse the race breakfast before long training rides.
