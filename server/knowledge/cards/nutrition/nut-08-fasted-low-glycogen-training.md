## Summary
Training with low carbohydrate availability, whether fasted in the morning or with depleted glycogen after an earlier session, amplifies some cellular signals for mitochondrial adaptation and raises fat oxidation. Whether this translates into better performance is unresolved: one well-known trial in triathletes found a benefit of a "sleep-low" design, while studies in elite athletes found none. The costs are clear: lower session quality, higher perceived effort, greater illness risk and, if used often, low energy availability.

## Key numbers
- Fasted rides: LOW intensity only, typically 45–90 min; power at a given effort is usually a few percent lower.
- Sleep-low: evening hard session → no carbohydrate overnight → easy fasted ride next morning → normal eating afterwards. Used 1–3 times a week for 1–3 weeks in studies.
- In trained triathletes, 3 weeks of sleep-low improved 10 km running time by about 3% and cycling efficiency versus matched intake spread normally (one study).
- In elite endurance athletes, 4 weeks of periodized carbohydrate gave no advantage over high availability.
- Caffeine or a carbohydrate mouth rinse reduces the perceived effort of fasted sessions; protein before or during does not blunt the signalling.

## In practice
- If used at all: once a week, on an easy session, in base periods, by riders who are otherwise well fuelled.
- Never for HIGH or key MID sessions, long rides over 2 h, in heat, when ill or run down, or during weight-loss phases.
- Eat a proper carbohydrate-rich meal afterwards.
- An ordinary easy ride before breakfast is harmless for most people and needs no special justification.

## Limits
Protocols differ, trials are small, and "train low" often coincides with lower total energy intake, which confounds results.

## Coach guidance
Do not recommend it proactively. If a rider already trains fasted, check that quality sessions are fuelled and that there are no signs of under-eating.
