## Summary
The intestine absorbs glucose and fructose through different transporters. Glucose (including maltodextrin and starch) saturates at roughly 60 g/h; adding fructose opens a second route, so total absorption and oxidation rise and gut discomfort falls. This is why any intake above about 60 g/h should combine the two, and why modern products use ratios between 2:1 and 1:0.8 (glucose:fructose).

## Key numbers
- Peak oxidation of ingested glucose alone: about 1.0–1.1 g/min (60–66 g/h).
- With added fructose: about 1.5 g/min at 90 g/h intake, higher at 120 g/h with a ratio near 1:0.8.
- Ratios: 2:1 (classic, up to 90 g/h); 1:0.8 (for 90–120 g/h).
- Table sugar (sucrose) is 1:1 glucose:fructose and works well and cheaply.
- Home mix example for about 90 g/h: 60 g maltodextrin + 30 g fructose per hour, or about 90 g sugar, in 500–750 ml with a pinch of salt (about 1 g).
- Drink concentration: up to about 8–10% is absorbed easily; very concentrated mixes (15–18%) need extra plain water and practice.

## In practice
- Check labels: products listing only maltodextrin or glucose are fine up to 60 g/h, not above.
- Mixing forms (drink + gels + chews + solid food) reduces flavour fatigue on long rides; solids suit lower-intensity stretches.
- Fructose alone is poorly absorbed and causes gut distress; it works only in combination.
- Riders with diagnosed fructose malabsorption need individual advice from a dietitian.

## Limits
Performance benefits over glucose alone are shown mainly for efforts over 2.5 h; optimal ratio at very high intakes is still being studied.

## Coach guidance
When a rider reports stomach trouble at high intakes, check the glucose:fructose ratio and the concentration before lowering the amount.
