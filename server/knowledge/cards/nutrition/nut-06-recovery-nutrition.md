## Summary
How much post-ride nutrition matters depends on when the next demanding session is. With 24 hours or more, total daily intake does the job and timing is secondary. With less than about 8–12 hours, as in double days, stage races and camps, rapid carbohydrate intake immediately after riding makes a real difference to glycogen stores. Protein supports muscle repair and adaptation in both cases.

## Key numbers
- Rapid refuelling (next session within 8–12 h): 1.0–1.2 g/kg carbohydrate per hour for the first 4 h, starting within 30–60 min.
- If that much carbohydrate is impractical: 0.8 g/kg/h carbohydrate plus 0.3–0.4 g/kg protein achieves similar glycogen storage.
- Normal recovery (next hard session 24 h+ away): a regular meal within about 2 h with carbohydrate and 20–40 g protein; reach the day's total.
- Glycogen synthesis is fastest in the first 2 h after exercise; full restoration takes about 24 h on 7–10 g/kg.
- Fluid: replace about 125–150% of body-mass loss over the following hours, with sodium.
- Example for 72 kg after a hard 3 h ride with another tomorrow morning: 70–85 g carbohydrate per hour for 4 h plus 25–30 g protein early.

## In practice
- Convenient options: chocolate milk, rice with eggs or chicken, sandwiches, cereal with yoghurt, recovery drink followed by a meal.
- After easy rides under 90 min, the next normal meal is enough.
- High on-bike carbohydrate intake reduces the recovery burden.
- Appetite is often suppressed after hard or hot rides: start with liquids.

## Limits
The "anabolic window" for protein is wide (hours); urgency applies mainly to carbohydrate when turnaround is short.

## Coach guidance
Ask when the next key session is and scale the advice accordingly. Avoid making every ride sound like it needs a recovery shake.
