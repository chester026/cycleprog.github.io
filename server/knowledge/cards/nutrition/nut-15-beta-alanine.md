## Summary
Beta-alanine taken daily for weeks raises muscle carnosine, a buffer inside the muscle cell. This improves capacity in hard efforts lasting about 30 seconds to 10 minutes by a small amount. Unlike caffeine or bicarbonate it has no acute effect: it works by loading over time. The only common side-effect is a harmless tingling of the skin.

## Key numbers
- Dose: 3.2–6.4 g per day, split into 0.8–1.6 g doses with meals, for at least 4 weeks; 8–12 weeks for full loading.
- Muscle carnosine increases by about 40–80% depending on dose and duration; it washes out slowly over 6–15 weeks after stopping.
- Average benefit: about 2–3% in exercise capacity for efforts of 0.5–10 min; smaller and less certain in performance tests and in highly trained athletes.
- Tingling (paraesthesia): dose-related, appears 15–20 min after larger single doses, fades within an hour; sustained-release forms and split doses reduce it.
- Maintenance after loading: about 1.2 g per day.

## In practice
- Relevant for the same events as bicarbonate: pursuit, short time trials, hill climbs, criteriums, track, cyclocross, and for supporting repeated hard efforts in training blocks.
- Plan ahead: start 6–10 weeks before the target period.
- No benefit for steady endurance riding.
- Use products tested for banned-substance contamination if the rider competes under anti-doping rules.

## Limits
Effects are small relative to day-to-day variation, so an individual may not notice them. Long-term safety data beyond about 6 months are limited, though no harm has been identified.

## Coach guidance
Low priority for most amateurs. Mention only for riders focused on short, intense events who have the basics in place.
