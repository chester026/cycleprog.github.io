## Summary
Caffeine is the best-supported legal performance aid in endurance sport. It works mainly on the brain, lowering perceived effort and increasing alertness, so more power is produced at the same feeling. The average benefit is a few percent in time-trial performance. Response and side-effects vary a great deal between people, and the cost to sleep can cancel the benefit when used late in the day.

## Key numbers
- Dose: 3–6 mg/kg about 30–60 min before (72 kg: 215–430 mg). Lower doses of 1–3 mg/kg (about 100–200 mg) are often nearly as effective with fewer side-effects.
- Average effect: about 2–4% improvement in time-trial performance or power.
- Peak blood level 30–75 min after capsules or drinks; faster from gum.
- Half-life 4–6 h (range 2–10 h): a 200 mg dose at 16:00 leaves a meaningful amount at bedtime.
- Content: espresso 60–80 mg; mug of filter coffee 90–150 mg; caffeinated gel 25–100 mg; cola 30–40 mg per 330 ml.
- Long events: a moderate dose before, then 25–100 mg every 1–2 h, or a larger share saved for the final third.
- Above 6 mg/kg: no extra benefit, more side-effects (jitters, palpitations, gut upset, anxiety).

## In practice
- Test dose and timing in training.
- Habitual coffee drinkers still benefit; a withdrawal period before racing is unnecessary.
- Not a dehydrating agent at these doses.
- Avoid for evening sessions when sleep matters more than the session.
- Useful for early-morning or sleep-deprived starts and for the last part of long events.

## Limits
Genetic differences in metabolism and sensitivity are real but not practically testable. People with heart rhythm problems, high blood pressure, anxiety disorders or who are pregnant should take medical advice.

## Coach guidance
Suggest starting at the low end. Do not recommend caffeine to riders who mention palpitations or sleep problems, and never stack products without adding up the total.
