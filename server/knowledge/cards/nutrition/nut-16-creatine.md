## Summary
Creatine monohydrate increases the muscle's phosphocreatine store, improving repeated short maximal efforts and the gains from strength training. It is among the most studied and safest supplements. For cyclists its value is specific: sprint and repeated-surge ability and gym work benefit, while steady endurance performance does not, and the 1–2 kg of water weight it adds is a cost on climbs.

## Key numbers
- Dose: 3–5 g per day; muscle stores saturate in about 3–4 weeks. A loading phase (20 g/day in 4 doses for 5–7 days) is faster but not necessary.
- Muscle creatine rises about 20–40%; vegetarians start lower and respond more.
- Effects: about 5–15% more work in repeated sprints; greater strength and lean-mass gains from resistance training; small or no effect on continuous endurance performance.
- Body mass: +1–2 kg in the first weeks, mainly intracellular water.
- May enhance glycogen storage when taken with carbohydrate.
- Stores return to baseline 4–6 weeks after stopping.

## Where it fits
- Track sprint, criterium and cyclocross riders; road sprinters.
- Off-season strength blocks for any rider.
- Masters riders, for whom maintaining muscle mass and strength is a priority.
- Less suited to climbers and to riders near a weight target for mountainous events.

## In practice
- Monohydrate is the form with the evidence; other forms offer no advantage.
- Take daily at any time; timing matters little.
- Use batch-tested products for riders under anti-doping rules.

## Limits
Healthy kidneys handle these doses without harm in long-term studies; people with kidney disease should consult a doctor. Some users report mild stomach upset with large single doses.

## Coach guidance
Frame it as useful for sprinting and strength, neutral-to-negative for climbing. Leave the decision to the rider.
