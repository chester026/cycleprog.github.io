## Summary
Body mass affects cycling mainly on climbs, where speed depends on watts per kilogram; on flat roads absolute power and aerodynamics dominate. Losing fat can help climbing if power is preserved, but the pursuit of leanness is also the most common route to under-fuelling, lost power, illness and disordered eating in cyclists. For most amateurs, training consistency and fuelling well raise W/kg more reliably than dieting. Where a rider does have fat to lose and wants to, slow change in a low-stakes period is the only approach with a good track record.

## Key principles
- Timing: off-season or base period; never during hard blocks, tapers or race periods.
- Rate: slow. Research in athletes shows that slower loss preserves lean mass and performance better than rapid loss.
- Method: keep fuelling in and around training (before, during, after); make modest reductions away from training, mainly in energy-dense extras and alcohol; keep protein high (see `nut-07-protein`) and include strength training.
- Never restrict on the bike or before key sessions.
- Monitor power, mood, sleep and health, not only the scale. Falling power means the approach is wrong.
- Daily weight fluctuates 1–2 kg with glycogen, salt and hydration; only multi-week trends mean anything.
- Many riders are already at a healthy, appropriate weight; lighter is not automatically faster.

## Stop signs
Persistent fatigue, declining performance, irritability, feeling cold, poor sleep, frequent illness or injury, missed periods, low libido, preoccupation with food or weight. Any of these means end the deficit and see `nut-20-energy-availability-reds`.

## Limits
Individual healthy weight ranges differ. Body-fat measurements from scales and skinfolds carry errors of several percentage points.

## Coach guidance
Do not raise weight unless the rider does. Do not set calorie targets, deficits or goal weights; give principles and refer riders who want a structured plan to a sports dietitian. With any stop sign, or any mention of restricting, purging or bingeing, give no numbers at all and encourage professional support.
