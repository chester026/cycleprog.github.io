## Summary
Sodium bicarbonate raises the blood's capacity to buffer acid, allowing more hydrogen ions to leave working muscle during very hard efforts. It improves performance in maximal efforts lasting roughly 1–10 minutes and in repeated high-intensity bouts by a small margin. Its drawback is gastrointestinal: nausea, bloating and diarrhoea are common, and a bad reaction costs far more than the supplement gains. Newer delivery forms reduce but do not eliminate this.

## Key numbers
- Dose: 0.2–0.3 g/kg body mass (72 kg: 14–22 g), 60–150 min before the effort.
- Average benefit: about 1.5–2% in efforts of 1–10 min; possible gains for repeated sprints and finishing efforts in longer races.
- Peak blood bicarbonate: 60–180 min after ingestion, varying between people.
- To limit gut problems: lower dose (0.2 g/kg), split over 30–60 min, with a carbohydrate-rich meal and plenty of fluid; or enteric-coated capsules or hydrogel products.
- Sodium load: 0.3 g/kg contains about 6 g of sodium for a 72 kg rider.

## In practice
- Relevant for pursuits, short time trials and prologues, hill climbs, track events, criteriums and cyclocross; little use for steady endurance rides.
- Always trial at least twice in hard training with the exact protocol.
- Have access to a toilet during trials.
- Can be combined with beta-alanine and caffeine; effects are partly additive.

## Limits
Individual response and tolerance vary widely. Not appropriate for people with high blood pressure, kidney or heart conditions, or on sodium-restricted diets without medical advice.

## Coach guidance
Mention only when the target event is short and intense and the rider asks about marginal gains. Stress the trial requirement and the medical cautions.
