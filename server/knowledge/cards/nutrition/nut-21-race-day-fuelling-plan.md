## Summary
A fuelling plan turns guidelines into a schedule the rider can follow under race stress: what to eat the day before, at breakfast, and at which minute or kilometre on course. The amounts depend mainly on duration. The plan is written in advance, uses rehearsed products, and is simple enough to remember.

## Key numbers
| Event | Day before | Breakfast (3 h before) | On the bike | Fluid | Caffeine (optional) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 60 min (TT, hill climb, crit) | normal high-carb | 1–2 g/kg | nothing or mouth rinse; gel before start | sips | 2–3 mg/kg 45 min before |
| 1–2.5 h (road race, short fondo) | 7–8 g/kg | 2–3 g/kg | 60 g/h | 500 ml/h | 2–3 mg/kg before |
| 2.5–5 h (gran fondo) | 10–12 g/kg (loading) | 2–3 g/kg | 80–90 g/h (up to 100–120 if trained) | 500–750 ml/h | 2–3 mg/kg before + 50–100 mg in final third |
| 5 h+ (long fondo, ultra) | 10–12 g/kg | 2–4 g/kg | 60–90 g/h, mixed forms incl. solids | 400–800 ml/h + sodium 300–700 mg/h | small doses every 1–2 h |

## Building the schedule
- Convert g/h into items: e.g. 90 g/h = one 500 ml bottle of 40 g mix + two 25 g gels per hour.
- Set a timer every 15–20 min; first intake within 20–30 min of the start.
- Tie eating to the course: before climbs and on easy sections, not on hard efforts or technical descents.
- List what to carry and what to take at each feed station; assume stations may have run out.
- Heat: raise fluid and sodium, keep carbohydrate concentration manageable (see `nut-10-hydration`).
- Have a fallback if the stomach rebels: slow slightly, small sips, resume gradually.

## In practice
- Under-eating in the first two hours is the most frequent error; appetite then disappears when it is needed most.
- Rehearse the full plan on at least two long rides.
- After the finish: carbohydrate, protein and fluid within the first hour.

## Limits
Plans are starting points; body size, intensity and tolerance shift the numbers.

## Coach guidance
Deliver the plan as a timeline with concrete items, linked to route points when a route file is available, and keep each instruction short enough to read on a head unit.
