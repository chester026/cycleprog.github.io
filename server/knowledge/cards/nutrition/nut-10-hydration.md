## Summary
Sweat losses during cycling range from negligible to more than two litres per hour depending on heat, intensity, body size and the individual. Fluid loss raises heart rate and core temperature and, beyond a certain point, reduces performance, particularly in heat. Two schools exist: plan intake to keep body-mass loss under about 2%, or simply drink to thirst. In cool conditions and rides under 2 hours thirst is sufficient; in heat and long events a plan based on a measured sweat rate is safer. Over-drinking is also harmful.

## Key numbers
- Sweat rate: typically 0.5–1.0 L/h in cool conditions, 1.0–2.0+ L/h in heat or at high intensity.
- Measuring it: weigh (minimal clothing) before and after a 60–90 min ride; loss in kg + fluid drunk in L − urine = sweat in L; divide by hours.
- Guideline: limit body-mass loss to about 2–3% in heat; larger losses are tolerated in cool conditions and in outdoor cycling with good airflow.
- Practical intake: 400–800 ml/h; up to 1 L/h in heat (gastric emptying limits about 1–1.2 L/h).
- Before: 5–7 ml/kg 2–4 h before; pale-yellow urine.
- After: 1.25–1.5 L per kg lost, with sodium.
- Each 1% body-mass loss raises heart rate by roughly 3–5 beats/min in heat.

## In practice
- Indoors, sweat rate is high because airflow is low: use fans and drink.
- Cold weather blunts thirst but losses continue under clothing.
- Carry enough for the gap between refill points; plan refills on long routes.
- Never drink far beyond thirst for hours: weight gain during an event is a warning sign of over-hydration (see `nut-11-sodium`).

## Limits
Lab studies in still air overstate the effect of dehydration on outdoor cycling; the 2% threshold is debated.

## Coach guidance
Recommend a sweat test before hot events. Give a range per hour and tie it to conditions.
