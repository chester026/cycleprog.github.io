## Summary
A racing heart, a tight stomach, frequent toilet visits and racing thoughts before an event are the body preparing to perform. Research on competitive anxiety shows that the intensity of these symptoms matters less than how the athlete interprets them: read as readiness, they help; read as a sign of impending failure, they hurt. Experiments in other performance settings found that relabelling anxiety as excitement improved performance more than trying to calm down. Practical tools are reappraisal, a fixed routine, controlled breathing and a focus on process.

## Key points
- Some arousal improves performance; the useful level differs between people and events (higher for a short hill climb than for a six-hour fondo).
- Physical symptoms peak before the start and usually fade within the first minutes of riding.
- Trying to suppress nerves tends to amplify them; naming and relabelling ("I'm keyed up, I'm ready") works better.
- Worry feeds on uncontrollables (results, rivals, weather); process goals and checklists bring attention back to controllables.
- Slow breathing with long exhalations (e.g. in for 4 s, out for 6–8 s, for 2–3 min) lowers physiological arousal.
- Routines reduce the number of decisions and signal familiarity.
- Sleep the night before is often poor and matters little if the previous nights were good.

## In practice
- Write the race-morning timeline and the first-ten-minutes plan in advance (see `race-08-race-morning`).
- Prepare answers to "what if" worries: puncture, dropped from the group, bad legs early. A plan dissolves most of them.
- Use the warm-up as a transition: once the legs move, nerves have somewhere to go.
- Keep caffeine moderate if prone to jitters.
- Enter low-stakes events to practise the whole experience.
- After the event, note what the nerves actually did compared with what was feared.

## Limits
Evidence for reappraisal comes largely from non-sport tasks; individual responses vary. Anxiety that is persistent, overwhelming, causes panic attacks or spreads into daily life is a matter for a psychologist or doctor.

## Coach guidance
Normalise nerves, offer one or two tools, and keep the focus on the rider's process plan. Suggest professional support when anxiety is severe or not limited to events.
