## Summary
Prolonged demanding mental work, concentration, decisions, screens, emotional control, makes subsequent endurance exercise feel harder and shortens how long people can sustain it, even though heart, lungs and muscles respond normally. In the key experiment, 90 minutes of a demanding cognitive task before cycling to exhaustion reduced endurance time by about 15% through higher perceived effort. A later systematic review confirmed the effect for endurance performance; maximal strength, power and short sprints are largely unaffected.

## Key numbers
- Design of the original study: 16 subjects, randomized crossover; cycling at 80% of peak power to exhaustion after 90 min of a demanding cognitive task or 90 min of neutral documentaries.
- Time to exhaustion: about 640 s when mentally fatigued versus 754 s in the control condition.
- Heart rate, oxygen uptake, lactate and cardiac output responses were essentially the same; perceived effort was higher from the start.
- Motivation for the physical task was not lower.
- Self-paced efforts show lower power at the same perceived effort.

## In practice
- After a draining work day, expect interval power to be down or RPE up by a point or two; this is not lost fitness.
- Schedule key sessions for mentally fresher times: mornings, days off, before rather than after long meetings where possible.
- When that is not possible: a 15–20 min mental break without screens, a short walk, caffeine, music and a longer warm-up reduce the effect; then judge by the first interval (see `rec-04-training-when-tired`).
- Easy rides after hard mental days are usually fine and often restore mood.
- Before races: avoid heavy cognitive work, long drives in traffic and stressful logistics on the day.
- Some researchers propose that training under mild mental load could build resistance; evidence is preliminary.

## Limits
Lab tasks differ from real work; effects vary between people and may be smaller in well-trained athletes. Mental fatigue is distinct from sleepiness and from low mood, though they often coincide.

## Coach guidance
Ask about the day when a session goes unexpectedly badly. Treat "tired from work" as a legitimate training factor and adjust without implying weakness.
