## Summary
Where a rider directs attention changes both how an effort feels and how well it is executed. Distraction makes easy and moderate riding pass more pleasantly; at high intensity, attention is pulled toward the body whether the rider wants it or not, and the useful skill becomes steering it toward things that help: pacing information, technique cues, relaxation and the immediate task, rather than toward how much it hurts or how far is left. Experienced endurance athletes switch flexibly between these modes.

## Key points
- **Outward, distracting focus** (scenery, music, conversation, podcasts): lowers perceived effort at low to moderate intensity; suits endurance and recovery rides and long indoor sessions. Impairs pacing in maximal efforts.
- **Task-relevant monitoring** (power, cadence, breathing rhythm, gear choice, line, the wheel ahead): supports pacing and efficiency in hard efforts and races.
- **Active self-regulation** (relaxing face, hands and shoulders; smooth pedalling; rhythmic breathing; cue words): associated with better economy and performance.
- **Unhelpful internal focus**: dwelling on pain, catastrophising about remaining distance, monitoring breathing anxiously; raises effort.
- Chunking: dividing an effort into short segments (to the next bend, next minute, next kilometre) keeps attention in the present.
- Checking the head unit constantly can raise anxiety; glancing at set intervals works better.

## In practice
- Decide a focus plan for key efforts: e.g. first third: hold back, check power every 30 s; middle: rhythm and relaxed upper body; final third: count down segments, technique cues.
- Use a body scan every few minutes: jaw, shoulders, grip, breathing; release tension.
- In races, attention outward on tactics and safety; inward only for brief checks.
- Practise in intervals; attention control improves with deliberate use.
- Music: useful for training mood and effort at sub-maximal intensity; not available or allowed in most events, so rehearse without it too.
- Safety first on open roads: never so absorbed in data or audio that traffic awareness drops.

## Limits
Research uses varied definitions; what works is individual and depends on intensity and experience.

## Coach guidance
Offer one focus cue per phase of an effort and ask the rider what they noticed. Keep road-safety awareness above any focusing technique.
