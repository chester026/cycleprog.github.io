## Summary
Self-efficacy, the belief that one can carry out a specific task, predicts sport performance in meta-analysis and shapes how people respond to difficulty: those with higher self-efficacy try harder and persist longer. It is built mainly from experience of succeeding at things that were genuinely challenging, and it can be rebuilt after a bad race, an abandoned session, illness or injury. How a setback is explained matters: causes that are specific, temporary and controllable preserve confidence; explanations that are global and fixed ("I'm just not good enough") erode it.

## Sources of confidence, strongest first
1. **Own accomplishments**: completed sessions, progress in data, events finished.
2. **Seeing similar others succeed**: riders of comparable age, level or circumstances.
3. **Credible encouragement**: specific, honest feedback from someone trusted.
4. **Interpreting body signals**: reading nerves and effort as normal rather than as weakness.

## After a setback
- Separate the facts from the story: what happened, in numbers and events, before any conclusion.
- Look for causes in order: conditions, pacing, fuelling, sleep and stress, illness, plan errors, then fitness.
- Identify what went well alongside what did not.
- Choose one or two changes that are within the rider's control.
- Set a near-term, achievable challenge to bank a success quickly: a session that is demanding but very likely to be completed.
- Keep a record of evidence: a short log of sessions completed and bests achieved is a resource on low days.

## In practice
- One bad session is noise; patterns over weeks are information (see `load-15-progress-vs-noise`).
- After illness or a break, use "comeback" benchmarks rather than previous peaks.
- Confidence for an event is built by rehearsing its hardest parts in training: the long climb, the distance, the fuelling.
- Comparing with others on social platforms undermines confidence more often than it builds it.

## Limits
Confidence without preparation does not produce performance; the relationship runs both ways. Persistent self-criticism, hopelessness or low mood is beyond coaching and merits professional support.

## Coach guidance
Give specific, truthful evidence of capability from the rider's own data. Never inflate. Guide the explanation of setbacks toward causes that are temporary and actionable.
