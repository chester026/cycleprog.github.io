## Summary
Mentally rehearsing an event, running through the course, the efforts, the decisions and the difficult moments in vivid detail, prepares the rider to act without hesitation when those moments arrive. Imagery is a long-established tool in sport psychology; it is most effective when it resembles the real situation closely and when it includes how to respond to problems, not only a perfect performance. Simple "if–then" plans add a strong, well-evidenced element: deciding in advance what to do when a specific situation occurs.

## Key points
- Effective imagery is realistic: same perspective as riding, real-time speed, including sensations (effort, breathing, sound, temperature), emotions and the actual course.
- Rehearse process and responses: pacing the first climb, eating on schedule, staying calm when dropped, the final effort.
- Include difficulties and a successful response to each; imagining only success leaves the rider unprepared.
- If–then plans link a cue to an action: "If the group surges on the first climb, then I hold my power cap and let them go." "If I feel flat at halfway, then I eat, drink and reassess in ten minutes." "If I puncture, then I stop safely, fix it and ride my own pace."
- Short and frequent beats long and rare: 5–10 minutes, several times in the final week.
- Combining imagery with the physical setting (on the trainer, in kit, looking at the route profile or video) strengthens it.

## In practice
- Use the route profile to walk through the event section by section with the planned power, gears and feeding points.
- Rehearse the start routine and the first ten minutes most carefully.
- Rehearse the hardest moment and the cue word or action planned for it.
- Course reconnaissance, real or by video and map, feeds accurate imagery.
- Review the if–then list the evening before; three to five items is enough.
- Imagery also helps during injury or time off to keep skills and confidence alive.

## Limits
Imagery ability varies between people; effects on endurance performance specifically are less studied than on skills. It supplements training, it does not replace it.

## Coach guidance
For events with a route file, offer a short written walkthrough and help the rider write three if–then plans for likely problems.
