## Summary
Goals direct attention and effort, and decades of research show that specific, challenging goals improve performance more than vague intentions. In sport the type of goal matters. Outcome goals (a placing, beating someone) motivate but depend on others. Performance goals (a time, a power number) depend on the rider's own level. Process goals (what to do: pace the first climb at 220 W, eat every 20 minutes) are fully controllable. Reviews in sport suggest process goals have the most reliable benefit for both performance and confidence; using all three in layers works best.

## Key points
- Good goals are specific, challenging but realistic, time-bound and written down.
- Layering: one outcome or event goal for direction → two or three performance goals that would make it likely → process goals for training weeks and for event day.
- Process goals keep attention on the present task and protect against anxiety and against giving up when the outcome slips out of reach.
- Open goals ("see how well I can ride this climb") can work better than fixed targets for beginners and after setbacks.
- Goals need feedback: regular checks against data.
- Commitment is higher when the rider sets or co-sets the goal.
- Too many goals, or goals far beyond current ability, reduce motivation.

## Examples
- Outcome: finish the gran fondo in the first half of the field.
- Performance: hold 3.2 W/kg for the 40-min climb; raise 20-min power from 240 to 255 W by June.
- Process: complete 3 rides a week for 10 weeks; long ride every weekend; cap the first climb at 215 W; 80 g carbohydrate per hour.

## In practice
- Review goals every 4–8 weeks and after key events; adjust without drama when life or data change.
- Have a "B goal" for event day in case conditions make the main one impossible.
- Celebrate process goals achieved even when the outcome falls short.

## Limits
Individual preference varies; some riders are demotivated by numerical targets and do better with themes and open goals.

## Coach guidance
Help turn every big goal into controllable process goals, and refer back to those when reviewing sessions and events.
