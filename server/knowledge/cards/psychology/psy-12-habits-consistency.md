## Summary
Fitness is the product of what a rider does most weeks for months, so consistency outweighs any detail of programming. Consistency depends less on willpower than on habits: behaviours tied to stable cues and repeated until they need little deliberation. Field research found that everyday habits took a median of about two months to become automatic, with wide variation, and that missing an occasional day did not derail the process. Specific plans of when and where to act roughly double follow-through in pooled studies.

## Key numbers
- Habit formation: median about 66 days to reach automaticity, ranging from under 3 weeks to over 8 months depending on person and behaviour.
- A single missed occasion had no meaningful effect on habit formation.
- Implementation intentions ("On Tuesday at 6:15, I ride the trainer for 60 min before work"): medium-to-large effect on goal achievement in meta-analysis.
- Realistic adherence for a good training year: about 80–90% of planned sessions.

## Building the habit
- **Cue**: fixed days and times; attach to an existing routine (after the school run, before breakfast).
- **Low friction**: kit laid out, bike ready, bottles filled, session loaded the night before; trainer permanently set up.
- **Small start**: commit to the first 10 minutes; a shortened session keeps the habit alive.
- **Planned obstacles**: "If I miss the morning slot, then I do 30 minutes at lunch."
- **Reward**: something enjoyable attached to the ride: a podcast, coffee after, a favourite route.
- **Social commitment**: a riding partner or group fixes the appointment.
- **Identity**: "I'm someone who rides on Tuesdays" outlasts "I must train".

## In practice
- Schedule the week on a fixed day; protect two or three anchor sessions.
- Track completion visibly, but avoid streaks that punish rest, illness or life.
- After a break in routine (holiday, illness), restart with the easiest version of the habit, not the full plan.
- When adherence falls, change the plan to fit life before questioning commitment.
- "Never miss twice" is a useful rule; "never miss" is not.

## Limits
Habit research is mostly on simple daily behaviours; complex training routines take longer and remain partly deliberate.

## Coach guidance
Ask when and where the rider will do each session and help remove friction. Treat missed sessions as planning information, never as a failing.
