## Summary
Burnout in sport is a lasting state of emotional and physical exhaustion, a reduced sense of accomplishment and a loss of caring about something that used to matter. It differs from ordinary tiredness in that rest alone does not restore the desire to ride. It develops when demands stay high while enjoyment, control and reward fall, and it is more likely when cycling has become an obligation, when identity rests entirely on performance, or when training is squeezed into an already overloaded life. For amateurs it often shows as dread of the bike and guilt in equal measure.

## Signs
- Rides feel like chores; relief when a session is cancelled.
- Persistent tiredness with normal training load; flat mood around cycling.
- Cynicism about goals that used to excite; "what's the point".
- Feeling that no result is good enough.
- Irritability, withdrawal from riding friends.
- Riding out of guilt, fear of losing fitness or to maintain a streak.

## Contributing factors
Monotonous training, constant measurement and comparison, perfectionism, goals set by others, too little recovery, life stress, insufficient sleep and under-fuelling, lack of choice, loss of social riding.

## What helps
- A real break from structure: one to several weeks of no plan, riding only when and how the rider wants, or not at all.
- Removing or hiding data for a while; riding by feel.
- Restoring choice and variety: new routes, other disciplines, social rides, other sports.
- Revisiting why the rider rides and resetting or dropping goals.
- Reducing total load, in training and where possible outside it.
- Reconnecting with people.

## Boundary with health
Overtraining, under-fuelling and medical causes can look similar (see `rec-03-overtraining-syndrome`, `nut-20-energy-availability-reds`). Low mood, loss of interest in most things, sleep and appetite changes or hopelessness lasting more than about two weeks point to depression rather than sport burnout and need a doctor or psychologist; any thought of self-harm needs immediate support.

## Limits
Burnout research uses differing definitions and is mostly observational.

## Coach guidance
Take loss of enjoyment seriously and give permission to stop or change. Do not try to re-motivate with new targets. Encourage professional help when low mood extends beyond cycling.
