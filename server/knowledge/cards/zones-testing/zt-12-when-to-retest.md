## Summary
Zones need updating when fitness has moved by more than the test's own noise, roughly 3%. That happens at predictable points: after a training block, after a break, at the start of a new phase. Formal tests are one way; reading recent hard efforts is another and avoids spending a hard day on testing. Updating too rarely leaves training mis-targeted; testing too often produces noise and anxiety.

## Key numbers
- Typical rhythm: every 6–8 weeks during building phases; at the start of base, after the first build, before the race period.
- After a break: 2+ weeks off usually costs 3–7% of threshold power; retest or reduce FTP by about 5% and adjust from sessions.
- Smallest meaningful change: about 3% (8–10 W for most amateurs).
- New riders improve fast: check every 4–6 weeks in the first year.
- Test in a recovery week's second half: rested but not detrained.

## Signs zones are too low
- Threshold intervals completed at RPE 5–6 with heart rate well under threshold HR.
- New 20–60 min bests in rides or races above current FTP.
- VO2max reps all completed above range with stable power.

## Signs zones are too high
- Sweet-spot or threshold sessions repeatedly cut short or fading more than 5–8%.
- Heart rate reaching VO2max levels during "threshold" work.
- Endurance rides at the usual percentage feel hard and show high drift.

## In practice
- Without a test, move FTP in 2–3% steps and confirm with the next two key sessions.
- Update body mass at the same time if W/kg is used.
- Keep the same protocol and conditions between tests.
- Before an important event, test no later than 10–14 days out.

## Limits
A single bad test should not lower zones if recent sessions contradict it. Seasonal heat lowers achievable power without loss of fitness.

## Coach guidance
Propose an update when session data show a consistent pattern, with the evidence. Present a lower FTP after a break neutrally, as a temporary recalibration.
