## Summary
Functional Threshold Power is a field performance measure: the highest power a rider can hold in a quasi-steady state for roughly an hour, usually estimated as 95% of a 20-minute best. It is practical, repeatable and good enough for setting zones and tracking change. It is not a direct measurement of a physiological threshold: on average it lands near the second threshold, but for an individual it can be several percent off in either direction.

## Key numbers
- Convention: FTP = 0.95 × 20-min mean maximal power.
- Against maximal lactate steady state in trained cyclists: average bias around 1%, but 95% limits of agreement of about ±9%, i.e. ±20–25 W for a 270 W rider.
- A lab study in trained cyclists found steady state lactate at about 91% of 20-min power rather than 95%.
- Time to exhaustion at FTP: typically 30–70 min, mean around 50 min; many riders cannot hold it for 60.
- Repeatability of the 20-min test is high in experienced riders (about ±2–3%).

## In practice
- Treat FTP as "about what I can hold for 40–60 min" and as an anchor for zones.
- Riders with large anaerobic capacity tend to get an inflated FTP from 20-min or ramp tests; signs: threshold intervals at 100% feel like VO2max work and cannot be repeated.
- Diesel-type riders may be underestimated by short tests.
- Verify with a long effort: 2 × 20 min at 95–100% should be hard but repeatable.

## Limits
The original FTP definition (hour power) and the 20-min estimate are not interchangeable individually. Studies disagree on validity largely because warm-ups, protocols and reference thresholds differ.

## Coach guidance
Use FTP confidently for load and zones, but when session data contradict it, trust the sessions and suggest adjusting FTP by 2–5% or retesting.
