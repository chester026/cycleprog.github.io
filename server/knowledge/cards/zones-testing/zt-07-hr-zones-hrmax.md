## Summary
Heart-rate zones are only as good as their anchor. Age formulas predict the average maximum heart rate of a population and can be wrong for an individual by 10–20 beats, enough to shift every zone by one. Zones should be anchored to a measured maximum or, better, to heart rate at threshold. Maximum heart rate is largely genetic, falls slowly with age and does not indicate fitness.

## Key numbers
- Formulas: 220 − age; 208 − 0.7 × age (better fit in adults). Individual error (1 SD) about ±10–12 beats/min for both.
- Cycling HRmax is typically 5–10 beats lower than running HRmax.
- Field HRmax: after a thorough warm-up, 3–4 min hard uphill effort finishing with a 30–60 s all-out sprint; take the highest value seen in such efforts or races over recent months.
- Threshold heart rate (LTHR): average heart rate over the last 20 min of a 30-min solo maximal effort, or average of a 20-min test minus about 3–5%.
- LTHR is usually 85–92% of HRmax in trained riders.
- Zones from LTHR (Coggan): Z1 <68%, Z2 69–83%, Z3 84–94%, Z4 95–105%, Z5 >105%.

## In practice
- Replace device default zones (usually age-based) with zones from measured values.
- The highest heart rate recorded in the last season's hard races is often the best HRmax estimate.
- Fatigue, heat and dehydration move heart rate at a given power by 5–15 beats; a "wrong zone" day is often a conditions day.
- Maximum tests are for healthy, trained riders; anyone with cardiac symptoms or risk factors needs medical clearance first.

## Limits
Beta-blockers and some other medications lower heart rate and invalidate standard zones. Optical sensors add error.

## Coach guidance
If a rider's zones are age-derived, say so and suggest anchoring them to real data. Never comment on a low or high HRmax as good or bad.
