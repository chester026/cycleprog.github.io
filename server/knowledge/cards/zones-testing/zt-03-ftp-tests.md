## Summary
Three field protocols dominate. The 20-minute test is the reference; the ramp test is the easiest to execute; the 8-minute test sits between. Each multiplies a short maximal effort by a fixed factor, and that factor is the weak point: it is an average across riders. Pick one protocol, keep conditions constant and compare like with like.

## Key numbers
- 20-min test (full protocol): warm-up, 5 min all-out, 10 min easy, then 20 min maximal evenly paced; FTP = 95% of the 20-min average. Without the 5-min effort the result runs higher.
- Ramp test: steps of about 20 W per minute to exhaustion; FTP = 75% of the best 1-min power. Takes 15–25 min; pacing skill not needed.
- 8-min test: 2 × 8 min maximal with 10 min recovery; FTP = 90% of the average.
- Test-retest variation of the 20-min test in competitive cyclists: about 2–3%; smallest change detectable about 9 W in one lab study.
- Individual correction factors for the 20-min test range roughly 88–95%.

## In practice
- 20-min pacing: start at the expected average, hold through minute 15, raise in the last 5. A fast first 3 min costs more than it gains.
- Ramp tests overestimate riders with strong anaerobic capacity and underestimate steady "diesel" riders; the 20-min test is more robust for training zones.
- Standardize: same bike and power meter, indoors with strong cooling or the same climb, rested, fuelled, similar time of day.
- Alternatives without a formal test: best recent 20–60 min race or climb efforts; modelled FTP from the power curve if it contains recent maximal efforts.

## Limits
A test measures the day as well as the rider. Beginners improve test results through pacing practice alone.

## Coach guidance
Recommend the ramp test to newcomers and anxious testers, the 20-min test to riders targeting sustained efforts. State which protocol an FTP came from when comparing values.
