## Summary
Critical power (CP) is derived from two or more maximal efforts of different durations. It yields two numbers: CP, the boundary above which no physiological steady state is possible, and W′, the fixed amount of work available above it. Compared with FTP it rests on a physiological model, needs no arbitrary percentage and also quantifies the anaerobic reserve. The result depends on which durations are used.

## Key numbers
- Protocol: 2–3 maximal efforts between 2 and 15 min (commonly 3 and 12 min, or 3, 7 and 12 min), fully rested or at least 30 min apart; ideally within one week.
- Work–time model: Work = CP × time + W′.
  Example: 3 min at 400 W (72 kJ) and 12 min at 320 W (230.4 kJ) → CP = (230.4 − 72) / (720 − 180) = 293 W; W′ = 72 − 293 × 0.18 = 19 kJ.
- Typical W′: 10–25 kJ. CP in trained riders sits at about 75–90% of VO2max.
- Predicted time to exhaustion above CP: t = W′ / (P − CP). At 340 W in the example: about 400 s.
- CP usually falls slightly above lactate steady state and a few percent above 20-min-derived FTP on average, with wide individual scatter.

## In practice
- Efforts must be truly maximal and evenly paced; a soft long effort lowers CP and inflates W′.
- Including efforts shorter than 2 min raises CP and lowers W′; longer than 15–20 min does the opposite.
- CP can be modelled from the rider's best recent efforts in ride files if they were all-out.
- Use CP for pacing efforts of 3–30 min and for W′ balance; use FTP conventions for TSS-based load unless the platform supports CP.

## Limits
Sustainable time at CP is only about 20–40 min, so it is not "hour power". Estimates carry an error of roughly ±3–5%.

## Coach guidance
Offer CP testing to riders who race or want precise pacing. Report CP and W′ together and round them.
