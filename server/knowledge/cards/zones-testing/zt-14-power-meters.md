## Summary
A power meter measures torque with strain gauges and multiplies by angular velocity. Where it sits in the drivetrain determines what it sees and what can go wrong. For training, consistency matters more than absolute accuracy: one well-maintained meter used for everything gives reliable trends, while switching between devices adds a few percent of noise that can mask or mimic real change.

## Key numbers
- Claimed accuracy: ±1–2% for most current crank, spider and pedal meters; ±1–3% for direct-drive smart trainers; ±3–5% for wheel-on trainers.
- Independent testing of many units found average error near 1% but individual units off by several percent.
- Location: pedals and cranks read highest; hub and trainer read 2–4% lower because of chain and drivetrain losses.
- Single-sided (left only): doubles the left leg; typical asymmetry of 2–4% between legs becomes a 4–8% swing in extreme cases, and asymmetry changes with intensity and fatigue.
- Temperature: strain gauges drift with temperature change; most units compensate automatically, but a zero offset after the bike has adapted to outside temperature (10–15 min) is good practice.
- Zero offset: before every ride, cranks vertical and unloaded for crank types; pedals unloaded for pedal types. Trainers: spin-down or factory auto-calibration as specified.

## In practice
- Check for a firmware update and battery level when values look odd; low batteries cause dropouts.
- After moving pedals or cranks to another bike, re-zero and tighten to the specified torque; installation torque affects pedal meters.
- To compare two devices, record both simultaneously over a steady 10–20 min effort.
- Oval chainrings can inflate readings by 1–4% on meters that assume constant angular velocity within a pedal stroke.

## Limits
Published accuracy tests cover specific models and conditions. Rough surfaces and very low cadences increase error.

## Coach guidance
When a rider changes power meter, treat previous bests as a separate series for a few weeks and suggest a fresh test.
