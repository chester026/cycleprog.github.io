## Summary
Five durations summarise a rider's power profile: 5 seconds, 1 minute, 5 minutes, 20 minutes and 60 minutes. Each maps onto a different physical quality, so together they show where a rider is strong and what a given event will demand. Benchmarks in watts per kilogram give orientation; they are approximate bands drawn from published profiling tables and field data, not standards to judge a rider by.

## Key numbers (W/kg, approximate; men / women)
| Level | 5 s | 1 min | 5 min | 20 min | 60 min (FTP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New rider | 9–12 / 7–10 | 4.5–6 / 3.5–5 | 2.5–3.3 / 2–2.8 | 2.2–2.9 / 1.8–2.5 | 2.0–2.7 / 1.6–2.3 |
| Regular amateur | 12–16 / 10–13 | 6–8 / 5–6.5 | 3.3–4.3 / 2.8–3.7 | 3.0–3.8 / 2.5–3.2 | 2.8–3.6 / 2.3–3.0 |
| Strong amateur racer | 16–19 / 13–16 | 8–9.5 / 6.5–8 | 4.3–5.3 / 3.7–4.6 | 3.9–4.8 / 3.3–4.1 | 3.7–4.5 / 3.1–3.9 |
| Elite / professional | 19–24+ / 16–19+ | 9.5–11.5 / 8–9.3 | 5.5–7.5 / 4.8–6.6 | 5.0–6.6 / 4.3–5.8 | 4.8–6.4 / 4.1–5.7 |

- What each reflects: 5 s neuromuscular power; 1 min anaerobic capacity; 5 min VO2max; 20 and 60 min threshold.
- Age: expect roughly 5–10% lower values per decade after 40 at the same training level, more for 5 s and 1 min.

## In practice
- Compare a rider mainly with their own history; use bands only for orientation and event planning.
- On flat courses absolute watts matter more than W/kg; heavier riders are not disadvantaged there.
- Show dynamics: change over 6 weeks, season best versus all-time best, trend direction.
- Fatigued values (after 2000+ kJ) are the relevant ones for long events.

## Limits
Bands are compiled from tables built mostly on male racers; data for women, masters and recreational riders are thinner. Body mass used for W/kg is often outdated.

## Coach guidance
Never rank or grade a rider by a table. Use benchmarks to answer "what does this event need?" and present each number with its trend.
