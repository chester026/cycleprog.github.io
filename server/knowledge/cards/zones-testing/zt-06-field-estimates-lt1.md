## Summary
The first threshold (LT1/VT1) is the upper limit of easy training, and FTP does not locate it. Several field methods estimate it: the talk test, heart-rate drift during steady rides, breathing pattern, and a heart-rate-variability index (DFA alpha-1). None is precise; used together and repeated they place the boundary within roughly ±10–15 W, which is enough for training.

## Key numbers
- Typical position: 65–80% of FTP; 70–80% of HRmax; about 75–85% of threshold heart rate. Higher fractions in well-trained riders.
- Talk test: last intensity at which several full sentences can be spoken comfortably is at or just below VT1; when speech becomes clipped, VT1 has been passed.
- Drift test: after warm-up, ride 45–60 min at constant power indoors with cooling. Heart-rate rise under about 3–5% between the first and second half suggests the power is below LT1; above about 5% suggests it is over.
- Breathing: nasal breathing remains possible and breathing rate is steady below VT1 (rough guide).
- DFA alpha-1: a value of about 0.75 during a slow ramp has been proposed as VT1. Needs a chest strap with beat-to-beat recording and artefact-free data; validation results are mixed, with individual errors of ±10% or more.
- Lactate with a portable meter: the power at which lactate first rises about 0.3–0.5 mmol/L above baseline.

## In practice
- Start with talk test plus one drift test at 70% FTP; adjust the test power by 10 W and repeat on another day.
- Re-check after long breaks and after big fitness changes.
- When in doubt, set the easy-ride ceiling lower: riding 10 W under LT1 loses nothing.

## Limits
Heat, dehydration, caffeine and fatigue distort drift and HRV methods. Wrist optical sensors are unsuitable for DFA alpha-1.

## Coach guidance
Give the estimate as a range and say it is an estimate. Encourage the rider to trust the talk test on the road.
