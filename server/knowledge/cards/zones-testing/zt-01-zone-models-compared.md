## Summary
All zone systems slice the same physiology. The body has two real boundaries, the first and second thresholds, which give three domains (LOW, MID, HIGH). Five-, six- and seven-zone systems subdivide those domains for prescription. Zones anchored to a measured threshold (FTP, CP, lactate) are more individual than zones anchored to a maximum (HRmax, VO2max). Zone numbers from different systems are not interchangeable.

## Key numbers
- Coggan power zones (% FTP): Z1 recovery <55; Z2 endurance 56–75; Z3 tempo 76–90; Z4 threshold 91–105; Z5 VO2max 106–120; Z6 anaerobic 121–150; Z7 neuromuscular (maximal).
- Coggan heart-rate zones (% threshold HR): Z1 <68; Z2 69–83; Z3 84–94; Z4 95–105; Z5 >105.
- Typical 5-zone HR model (% HRmax): Z1 50–60; Z2 60–70; Z3 70–80; Z4 80–90; Z5 90–100. Brand defaults (Garmin, Polar, Strava) differ by several percent.
- Mapping: LOW = Coggan Z1–Z2; MID = Z3–Z4; HIGH = Z5–Z7. Sweet spot (88–94% FTP) straddles Z3/Z4.
- The second boundary sits at about 75–90% of VO2max in trained people, which is why %VO2max or %HRmax zones fit some riders poorly.

## In practice
- Identify which system the rider's device or app uses before discussing a zone number.
- For prescription use power zones from a current FTP or CP; use heart rate as the check for long steady efforts.
- Zone edges are conventions: 75% and 77% FTP are the same training.
- The LOW/MID edge is the least certain in FTP-based models, because LT1 is not a fixed fraction of FTP.

## Limits
Zones assume a fresh, cool rider. Thresholds fall during long rides and in heat, so fixed zones overstate what is sustainable late in a ride.

## Coach guidance
Speak in the rider's own zone system and add the physiological meaning ("upper endurance, below your first threshold"). Never assume another app's "Zone 3" equals Coggan Z3.
