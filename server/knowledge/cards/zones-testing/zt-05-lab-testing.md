## Summary
A lab test measures what field tests infer. A stepped test with blood lactate sampling locates the first and second lactate thresholds; a ramp with gas analysis gives VO2max and the ventilatory thresholds. The main practical gain over field testing is a measured first threshold, which sets the ceiling for easy training. Results depend on protocol and on how thresholds are computed, so repeat tests at the same lab.

## Key numbers
- Lactate step test: stages of 3–5 min (longer stages give truer steady-state values), increments 20–40 W, sample at the end of each stage; 6–9 stages.
- LT1: first rise of about 0.3–0.5 mmol/L above baseline (often 1.5–2.2 mmol/L). LT2: by method, e.g. baseline + 1.5 mmol/L, modified Dmax, or fixed 4 mmol/L (the fixed value misplaces many individuals).
- VO2max ramp: 20–30 W/min, 8–12 min to exhaustion. Criteria: plateau in VO2, RER above 1.10, HR near maximum.
- Different threshold methods applied to the same data can differ by 20–40 W.
- Typical trained cyclist: LT1 at 60–75% and LT2 at 78–90% of VO2max power.
- Retest interval: 2–3 times per season is enough for most riders.

## In practice
- Arrive rested, fed normally, no hard training for 48 h, same pre-test routine each time.
- Ask for the raw lactate-power curve; a rightward shift of the whole curve is the clearest sign of improved endurance.
- Lab powers come from the lab's ergometer; compare with the rider's own power meter before transferring zones (differences of 2–5% are common).
- Portable lactate meters allow field step tests; technique errors (sweat contamination) are the main risk.

## Limits
Short stages overestimate threshold power. VO2max alone predicts performance poorly within a group of similar riders. Lab conditions lack wind and terrain.

## Coach guidance
Use lab values, when available, in preference to estimates, especially LT1. Do not push riders toward lab testing; field data are adequate for most.
