## Summary
Power is the external load: what the rider did, instantly and independent of conditions. Heart rate is the internal load: what it cost the body, delayed and sensitive to heat, fatigue, hydration, caffeine and stress. Neither replaces the other. Power is the better target for intervals; heart rate is the better limiter on long easy rides and the better window on how the day is going; the relationship between them is the most informative signal of all.

## Key numbers
- Lag: heart rate needs 1–3 min to reflect a change in power; half-time of the response about 20–40 s. Useless for efforts under about 2 min.
- Day-to-day variation of heart rate at a fixed submaximal power: about 2–6 beats/min.
- Heat: +5–15 beats at the same power. Dehydration: several beats per 1% body-mass loss. Altitude raises submaximal heart rate. Caffeine +2–5 beats.
- Fatigue patterns: acute fatigue or glycogen depletion often lowers heart rate at high power (cannot get it up) with high RPE; illness onset and heat raise it.
- Drift on long steady rides: 3–10% is common (see `load-06-hr-drift-decoupling`).

## In practice
- Intervals: pace by power, read heart rate afterwards as the response.
- Easy rides: a heart-rate cap protects against going too hard when hot or tired; a power cap protects on fresh days. Use whichever is reached first.
- Without a power meter: heart rate plus RPE is adequate for LOW and MID work; use RPE for short HIGH efforts.
- Reading combinations: power up, HR same → fitter. Power same, HR up, RPE up → heat, illness, dehydration or fatigue. Power down, HR down, RPE high → deep fatigue, rest.

## Limits
Individual heart-rate behaviour varies widely; trends within one rider matter, absolute values do not. Medications can blunt the response.

## Coach guidance
When power and heart rate disagree, do not pick a winner; explain what the gap suggests and ask about conditions and feeling.
