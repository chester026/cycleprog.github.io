## Summary
The power–duration curve plots the best power a rider has produced for every duration from one second to several hours. Its shape is hyperbolic: very high power is available briefly, then the curve flattens toward the sustainable asymptote. It summarises a rider's whole capacity in one picture, shows relative strengths, and reveals change over time. It is only as good as the maximal efforts it contains.

## Key numbers
- Regions: 1–15 s neuromuscular power; 30 s–2 min anaerobic capacity; 3–8 min VO2max; 20–60 min threshold; 2 h+ endurance and durability.
- Typical ratios: 5-min power is about 115–125% of FTP; 1-min about 150–200%; 5-s about 250–400%+. 60-min power is about 93–97% of 20-min power.
- Profile shapes: sprinter (5 s and 1 min far above the rest), pursuiter/puncheur (1–5 min peak), time-trialist/climber (20–60 min relatively strongest, per kg for climbers), all-rounder (flat).
- Sustainable power is only about 25–35% of maximal sprint power.
- Use a rolling window (6–12 weeks) for current ability; all-time bests show potential.

## In practice
- A dip at some duration usually means the rider has not attempted a maximal effort there recently.
- Compare the current-window curve with last season's to see where the rider gained or lost.
- Choose training emphasis by event demands versus profile: a strong 20-min rider preparing for criteriums needs 1-min and repeatability work.
- Express in W/kg for climbing, in absolute watts (or W/CdA) for flat riding.

## Limits
Fresh bests overstate what is available late in a race (see `load-12-durability-metrics`). Group-ride and race efforts are rarely paced maximally at every duration. Rider typing is descriptive, not destiny.

## Coach guidance
Show changes along the curve as the main picture of progress, and tie strengths to event choices. Avoid labelling a rider permanently as one type.
