## Summary
Distribution is not a fixed setting for the year. Athletes commonly move from a pyramidal pattern in the preparation phase toward a more polarized one as competition approaches, and limited trial evidence suggests this sequence works better than holding either pattern throughout. The second principle is specificity: in the final 6–8 weeks the hard work should increasingly resemble the demands of the target event.

## Key numbers
- Well-trained runners, 16 weeks, four groups (about 15 per group): 8 weeks pyramidal followed by 8 weeks polarized gave the largest gains in VO2peak and 5 km performance, ahead of polarized-only, pyramidal-only and the reverse order (60 runners; one study).
- Trained road cyclists: across 7 controlled studies of 8–12 weeks (7.5–11.7 h/week), block and traditional periodization both improved VO2max, peak power and thresholds; no model was shown to be superior.
- Typical shift by time in zone: base about 85–90% LOW with mostly MID quality; pre-competition about 80% LOW with more HIGH; race season: races supply much of the intensity, training becomes easier.

## Why it works
Early MID work builds the capacity to sustain sub-threshold power and tolerates higher volume; later HIGH work raises the ceiling when freshness and specificity matter most. Changing the stimulus also counters stagnation after 6–8 weeks of the same emphasis.

## In practice — emphasis by event
- **Gran fondo / long sportive (4–8 h)**: LOW volume and long rides first; MID (tempo, sweet spot, long climbs at 80–90% FTP); small dose of HIGH.
- **Long climbs / hill-climb events (20–60 min efforts)**: threshold and sweet-spot blocks; VO2max work 6–8 weeks out.
- **Time trial (20–60 min)**: threshold-specific intervals in position; pyramidal throughout.
- **Road race / criterium**: repeated HIGH efforts, short intervals and sprints on a LOW base; more polarized.
- **Ultra / multi-day**: LOW volume and durability; back-to-back long days; minimal HIGH.

## Limits
Sequencing evidence rests largely on one running study. Event-specific emphases are coaching practice supported by event-demand analyses, not by comparative trials.

## Coach guidance
Ask for the target event and date before recommending a distribution. Plan in 3–8 week blocks with a stated emphasis, and explain the shift in emphasis when it changes.
