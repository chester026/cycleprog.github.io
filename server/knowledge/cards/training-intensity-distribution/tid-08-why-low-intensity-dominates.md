## Summary
Easy riding dominates because it is the only intensity at which large training volume can be accumulated without recovery cost outrunning adaptation. Below the first threshold the body is disturbed little, so a rider can train again the next day and can hit the hard sessions properly. Low-intensity volume and high-intensity work appear to be complementary: they drive overlapping adaptations through partly different signals, at very different costs.

## Key numbers
- In highly trained athletes, heart-rate variability returned to baseline within minutes after sessions below VT1, whether 60 or 120 min long; after sessions above VT1 (threshold or intervals) recovery was clearly delayed, on the order of an hour or more.
- So the first threshold behaves like a switch for acute autonomic stress; duration below it adds comparatively little.
- Hard sessions tolerated long term: about 2 per week, sometimes 3, across sports and levels.

## Mechanisms
- **Signal by volume**: prolonged contraction activates pathways for mitochondrial biogenesis and capillary growth through sustained energy turnover and calcium signalling; the stimulus scales with duration.
- **Fibre recruitment over time**: as slow fibres fatigue during long rides, additional motor units are recruited at the same low power, extending the stimulus to more of the muscle.
- **Central adaptations**: long LOW work expands plasma volume and supports stroke volume.
- **Low cost**: little glycogen use per minute, low catecholamine and cortisol response, low muscle damage.
- **Protecting quality**: fresh legs allow HIGH sessions at target power; proponents describe easy days as preparation for hard days and as a way to keep training monotony low.

## In practice
- The value of LOW training comes from its total, so it pays off mainly when there are enough hours. With few hours, the same principle holds but the absolute stimulus is small (see `tid-12-tid-by-weekly-volume`).
- Easy means below LT1 for the whole ride, including climbs where possible; a ride that averages LOW but spends 30% above LT1 has a different recovery cost.

## Limits
The autonomic-recovery data come from small studies of well-trained athletes; less-trained riders recover more slowly from all intensities. The claim that LOW work is uniquely effective for mitochondria is not supported (see `tid-09-zone-2-training`); its advantage is cost, not potency per minute.

## Coach guidance
Explain easy riding as "cheap volume that lets you train more and hit the hard days", not as a magic zone. Never label an easy ride as a weak or failed session.
