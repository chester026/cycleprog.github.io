## Summary
Popular "Zone 2" is steady riding in the upper part of LOW: just below the first threshold, zone 2 of a 5–7 zone model. For athletes with many training hours it is the backbone of the programme and that role is well supported. The stronger popular claims, that it is the *best* intensity for mitochondria, fat oxidation or health and that going harder spoils the effect, are not supported: per minute, higher intensities stimulate these adaptations at least as much. Zone 2 is valuable because a lot of it can be done, not because it is special.

## Key numbers
- Intensity: below LT1; typically about 55–75% FTP, 65–75% of HRmax, lactate under about 2 mmol/L, conversation possible. LT1 varies widely between individuals, so fixed percentages can miss in either direction.
- Expert consensus description for athletes: continuous sessions, commonly 1–5 h, repeated several times per week.
- Evidence review for the general population: no substantive evidence that Zone 2 is superior to higher intensities for mitochondrial or fat-oxidation capacity; earlier meta-analytic work suggests training below roughly 60% of maximal work rate produces little mitochondrial gain in untrained people; higher intensities matter most when weekly volume is low.

## Why the confusion
The claim was inferred from elite athletes, who do huge volumes of this riding and have exceptional mitochondrial capacity. Their capacity reflects total volume plus hard training plus selection, not a property of the zone.

## In practice
- 10+ h/week: Zone 2 volume is the main progression lever; extend the long ride, add hours before adding hard sessions.
- 6–10 h/week: 2 quality sessions, the rest Zone 2; longer single rides (2.5–4 h) give more than several short ones.
- Under 5–6 h/week: an all-Zone-2 plan under-uses limited time; keep 2 harder sessions and use Zone 2 for the remainder.
- Harder minutes inside a ride do not cancel the benefit of the easy minutes; they add fatigue.

## Limits
The critical review addressed the general population and specific cellular outcomes, not athlete performance; it is a narrative review. Direct trials of lactate-verified Zone 2 against other intensities at matched volume are scarce.

## Coach guidance
Support riders who enjoy Zone 2 riding; it is a legitimate choice and good training. Correct only two errors: expecting large gains from 3–4 h/week of easy riding alone, and fearing that any hard effort ruins an endurance ride.
