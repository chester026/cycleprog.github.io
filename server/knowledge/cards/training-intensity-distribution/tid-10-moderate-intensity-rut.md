## Summary
The most common distribution problem in self-coached riders is unintended convergence: easy rides creep above the first threshold, fatigue builds, and interval days are then ridden below target. The week ends up mostly MID without anyone planning it. Trials in which athletes shifted this unplanned MID time back to LOW, with hard work unchanged, showed better performance gains. The remedy is intensity discipline, not the removal of planned tempo or threshold sessions.

## Key numbers
- Sub-elite runners, 5 months, equal load and equal HIGH work: the group with about 80% LOW / 12% MID improved 10 km-type performance more than the group with about 67% LOW / 25% MID (time gains of 157 s versus 122 s; small groups).
- Recreational runners, 10 weeks: both polarized and MID-emphasis groups improved 10 km time (about 5% vs 3.6%, not significantly different); among those who actually held the prescribed distribution the gap was larger. Many recreational athletes drifted toward MID despite an easy prescription.
- Typical signature in ride data: under 60–65% of time in LOW over several weeks with no structured MID sessions on the calendar; "endurance" rides with intensity factor 0.75–0.85.

## Why it happens
Group rides and riding partners, hills on every route, short available time ("make it count"), the feeling that easy is wasted, and chasing average speed or segment times.

## In practice
- Distinguish intended from unintended MID: 3 × 15 min sweet spot on plan is training; a 2 h "easy" ride at 80% FTP is drift.
- Tools for easy days: a power or heart-rate cap rather than a target, flatter routes or lower gears on climbs, solo rides, the talk test.
- Check the other side too: are HIGH sessions reaching target power and duration? If not, fatigue from drift is a likely cause.

## Limits
Evidence comes mainly from runners; in cycling, terrain makes some MID time unavoidable and less costly (no impact loading). A rider who simply enjoys riding steadily hard and is not chasing performance is not making an error.

## Coach guidance
Raise this only when the data show the pattern *and* the rider wants to improve or reports stagnation or fatigue. Frame it as "making easy days easier so hard days can be harder". Riding easy is always the rider's choice and is never flagged negatively.
