## Summary
In a threshold-dominant model a large share of training, often 35–50% of time, is done in MID (tempo, sweet spot, threshold). It is popular with time-limited cyclists because it yields a high training load per hour. Controlled trials in trained athletes generally show it improves fitness less than polarized or pyramidal training at matched or higher load, and it is not how successful athletes train. That finding concerns MID-*dominant* training; it is not evidence against MID sessions inside a LOW-dominant programme.

## Key numbers
- Research definition: more than about 35% of time in MID, LOW below about 60%.
- Sweet spot: about 84–97% FTP (commonly 88–94%); threshold: 95–105% FTP.
- In a 9-week trial of well-trained athletes, the threshold group showed no significant gain in VO2peak, time to exhaustion or peak power, while the polarized group improved all three.
- Lactate-guided "double threshold" practice in elite runners: many controlled intervals at about 2–3.5 mmol/L (deliberately below LT2), 3–4 such sessions a week inside very high LOW volume; overall distribution stays pyramidal.

## Why results differ
MID work above the first threshold raises autonomic stress and glycogen use. When it fills most sessions, fatigue accumulates, HIGH sessions disappear or lose quality, and the stimulus becomes monotonous. Controlled just below LT2 and surrounded by easy volume, the same intensity is highly productive, which is the basis of the Norwegian approach.

## In practice
- For riders on 4–6 h/week a sweet-spot-heavy block of 4–8 weeks is a reasonable way to raise load when volume cannot increase; expect gains to flatten, then vary the stimulus.
- Cap MID-focused sessions at 2–3 per week; keep at least one session with HIGH work in most weeks.
- Keep threshold intervals controlled (finish able to do one more), not all-out.

## Limits
Threshold arms in trials were sometimes extreme (almost no HIGH work), which may understate what a mixed MID-focused plan does. Evidence for the double-threshold approach is observational, from elite runners and triathletes; cycling data are sparse.

## Coach guidance
Do not discourage threshold or sweet-spot sessions. Flag only the pattern where most rides are MID for many weeks with stalled progress; then suggest making easy rides easier and adding one HIGH session.
