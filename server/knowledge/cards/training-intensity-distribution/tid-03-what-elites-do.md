## Summary
Across rowing, cross-country skiing, running, swimming and cycling, descriptive studies of national- and international-level athletes converge on one pattern: a large training volume, most of it below the first threshold, with a small, deliberately placed share of hard work. This is an observation of what successful athletes do, not proof that it is optimal, but it has been stable across sports, decades and countries. Within that pattern, most datasets, including professional road cyclists, look pyramidal rather than strictly polarized.

## Key numbers
- About 80% of sessions easy, about 20% containing hard work (session-goal method), in athletes training 10–13 times per week.
- Elite volumes: roughly 500–1200 h/year depending on sport; professional road cyclists about 800–1100 h/year (20–30 h in heavy weeks).
- Hard sessions: typically 2, occasionally 3 per week, even at 25 h/week.
- Pro cyclists by power time in zone: the majority LOW, MID clearly larger than HIGH; races add much of the MID and HIGH load.

## Why it looks this way
Volume is the main lever available once intensity is capped by recovery. Athletes who tried replacing easy volume with more hard sessions generally did not sustain it. Reviews of intensification studies in already well-trained athletes found no convincing long-term gain from shifting the balance toward more high-intensity work.

## In practice
- The transferable principle is the ratio of hard to easy sessions and keeping easy sessions truly easy, not the absolute hours.
- Road racing itself is a large source of MID and HIGH work, so in-season training for racing cyclists is often easier than the distribution of the whole week suggests.

## Limits
Survivorship: we see athletes who tolerated this training. Elite distributions arise at 15–30 h/week; whether the same ratio is best at 5–8 h/week is a separate question (see `tid-12-tid-by-weekly-volume`). Descriptive data cannot separate the effect of distribution from talent and total volume.

## Coach guidance
Use elite data to support "most riding should be easy, and 2 quality sessions are enough", not to prescribe elite volumes or to claim that a polarized split is what professionals do.
