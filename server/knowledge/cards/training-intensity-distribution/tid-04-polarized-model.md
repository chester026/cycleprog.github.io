## Summary
A polarized distribution puts most training in LOW, very little in MID, and more in HIGH than in MID (LOW > HIGH > MID). The idea is to spend the hard-training budget well above the second threshold and keep everything else clearly easy. Short controlled trials in trained athletes show it works well, particularly for VO2max and peak power. Whether it beats a pyramidal distribution is not established (see `tid-07-pol-vs-pyr-vs-thr`).

## Key numbers
- Typical target by time: about 75–80% LOW, about 5% MID, 15–20% HIGH. By sessions: 4 easy to 1 hard.
- Polarization index: PI = log10( (LOW / MID) × HIGH × 100 ), with zones as fractions of total time. PI > 2.0 = polarized. If MID = 0, use 0.01; if HIGH = 0, PI = 0.
  Example: 0.80 / 0.05 / 0.15 → PI = 2.38 (polarized). 0.75 / 0.20 / 0.05 → PI = 1.27 (pyramidal).
- Stöggl & Sperlich 2014: 48 well-trained athletes (VO2peak ~63 mL/kg/min), 9 weeks, four groups. Polarized group: VO2peak +11.7%, time to exhaustion +17.4%, peak power/velocity +5.1%; threshold and high-volume groups showed no further gains.
- Neal 2013: 12 trained cyclists, crossover, 6 weeks per arm; polarized (about 80/0/20) improved peak power and lactate-threshold power more than threshold-focused (about 57/43/0).

## Why it works
HIGH work provides the strongest stimulus for stroke volume and maximal oxygen uptake per minute of training; LOW work adds volume at low autonomic and glycogen cost, so the athlete arrives fresh enough to execute the hard sessions at target quality.

## In practice
A polarized week for an 8–10 h rider: 2 HIGH sessions (e.g. 4–6 × 4–8 min at 105–115% FTP, or 3 sets of 30/15 s), everything else below LT1 including one long ride. No tempo "filler".

## Limits
Trials are short (6–16 weeks) and small. Comparison groups often did unusual training (threshold only, or no intensity). Achieving a true polarized split by power outdoors is hard, since terrain adds MID time. Very little MID work may under-prepare riders for events raced at MID intensity (long climbs, time trials, gran fondos).

## Coach guidance
Present polarized training as one effective option with good short-term evidence, strongest for raising VO2max in trained riders. Do not present it as proven superior.
