## Summary
Research on training intensity distribution uses three zones anchored to two physiological thresholds, not to percentages of a maximum. LOW is below the first threshold (LT1/VT1), where lactate stays near baseline and effort is sustainable for hours. MID lies between the first and second threshold (LT2/VT2, close to critical power or MLSS). HIGH is above the second threshold, where no metabolic steady state is reached. The thresholds are real boundaries in the body's response; the 5- and 7-zone systems riders see in apps are finer subdivisions of the same three domains.

## Key numbers
- LT1: first sustained rise in blood lactate above baseline, often near 2 mmol/L (individual range roughly 1.2–2.5 mmol/L).
- LT2: often near 4 mmol/L, but individual values range widely (about 2.5–6 mmol/L); fixed 2 and 4 mmol/L cut-offs are approximations.
- Approximate mapping to Coggan power zones: LOW ≈ Z1–Z2 (up to ~75% FTP), MID ≈ Z3–Z4 (~76–100% FTP), HIGH ≈ Z5 and above (>105% FTP). FTP sits near, usually slightly below or at, LT2.
- LT1 as a fraction of FTP is not fixed: commonly 65–80%, higher in well-trained riders (practice-level estimate; test individually).

## Why it works
Each boundary marks a change in physiological behaviour: below LT1 stress hormones and lactate remain stable; between thresholds lactate is elevated but stable; above LT2 lactate and oxygen uptake drift upward until exhaustion. Training stress and recovery cost change at these boundaries, which is why distribution is described relative to them.

## In practice
- When a rider says "zone 2", establish which model they mean. In a 5–7 zone model it is the upper part of LOW; in the research literature it is MID.
- Percent-of-FTP zones place the LOW/MID boundary by assumption. For a rider whose LT1 is at 68% of FTP, "Z2 at 75%" is already MID.
- Field checks for LT1: can speak full sentences; nasal breathing possible; heart-rate drift under ~5% over 60+ min at steady power.

## Limits
Thresholds shift with fatigue, heat and glycogen depletion within one ride, so a fixed power boundary overstates what is LOW late in a long ride. Different threshold methods give different values for the same rider.

## Coach guidance
Translate every distribution statement into the rider's own zone model before giving advice. If only FTP is known, treat the LOW/MID boundary as uncertain (about 65–80% FTP) and suggest a field check before prescribing large LOW volumes near the top of the range.
