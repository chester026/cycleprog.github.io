## Summary
The same training week produces very different percentages depending on how it is counted. The famous "80/20" comes from the **session-goal** method: each session is classified by its main purpose, so an interval day counts fully as hard even though most of its minutes are easy. Counted by **time in zone**, the same training looks closer to 90% LOW or more. Comparing a rider's time-in-zone chart with an 80/20 target is therefore a category error.

## Key numbers
- Session-goal: roughly 75–80% easy sessions, 15–20% hard sessions in elite endurance athletes.
- The same athletes by heart-rate time in zone: roughly 88–93% LOW, a few percent MID, a few percent HIGH (approximate; exact values vary by sport and study).
- A 90-min session with 4×8 min hard contains ~32 min at target, i.e. about 35% of the session; heart rate will register even less in the HIGH zone because of lag.
- For 2 hard sessions in a 10 h week with ~35 min at intensity each: about 12% of time is hard, about 88% easy.

## Why it matters
- Heart rate lags behind power by 1–3 min, so HR time in zone under-counts short intervals; power time in zone counts every second above threshold, including surges and standing starts, and over-counts HIGH on variable terrain.
- Coasting and stops inflate LOW share on outdoor rides.
- Meta-analyses note that conclusions about which model is best depend on the quantification method used.

## In practice
- For "am I following 80/20?" use session counts: out of every 5 rides, about 1 is a deliberate hard session.
- For load control use time in zone by power, and judge it against time-in-zone norms: LOW 80–90%+, MID+HIGH together typically 10–20%.
- Report both when analysing a period: "9 sessions, 2 hard (22% by session); 91% of time below LT1".

## Limits
Session-goal classification is subjective for mixed rides such as group rides or hilly endurance rides. No method captures how hard a HIGH minute was (110% vs 150% FTP).

## Coach guidance
Always state which method a percentage refers to. Never tell a rider their distribution is wrong by comparing time-in-zone numbers with session-goal targets.
