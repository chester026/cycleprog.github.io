## Summary
The 80/20 pattern was observed in athletes training 10–25+ hours a week. The robust part that transfers to any volume is the count of hard sessions: about 2 per week, occasionally 3. Because that count stays roughly constant, the *percentage* of hard training necessarily rises as weekly hours fall. A rider on 5 hours with 2 quality sessions is training sensibly at around 70/30 by time, not violating a rule. These rules are practice-level synthesis: trials have not compared distributions across volume bands.

## Key numbers (by time in zone, power)
| Weekly hours | Hard sessions | LOW share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–5 h | 2 (3 if each is short) | 60–75% | 1 HIGH + 1 MID session; remaining time easy; one ride 90+ min if possible |
| 6–9 h | 2 | 75–85% | 1 HIGH + 1 MID, or 2 HIGH in a VO2max block; one long ride 2.5–4 h |
| 10–14 h | 2, sometimes 3 | 82–90% | add volume before adding a third hard day |
| 15+ h | 2–3 | 88–93% | classic elite pattern; races count as hard days |

- Quality minutes per hard session: HIGH about 15–30 min at target (e.g. 5 × 4–5 min); MID about 30–60 min at target (e.g. 3 × 15–20 min).
- At least 1 easy or rest day between hard sessions for most riders; masters riders often need 2.

## Why
Supporting evidence is indirect: recreational athletes responded at least as well to pyramidal as to polarized distributions; reviews of low-volume exercisers indicate intensity matters more when volume is small; and elite data show that hard-session frequency, not percentage, is what stays constant.

## In practice
- First lever for improvement is usually total hours and consistency; second is execution of the 2 quality sessions; the exact split comes third.
- Adding a third or fourth hard session is the usual error at low volume: load rises for 2–3 weeks, then quality drops.
- When hours increase, add them as LOW.

## Limits
The table is a starting point. Individual recovery (age, sleep, work stress, training history) shifts it more than any model does. Riders below about 3 h/week are outside what any of this literature covers.

## Coach guidance
Use the rider's actual average weekly hours from their data, not their intended hours. State the recommendation as sessions per week first, percentages second. Mark it as coaching practice rather than trial-proven when asked how certain it is.
