## Summary
A pyramidal distribution keeps the same large LOW base as polarized training but orders the rest the other way: more MID than HIGH (LOW > MID > HIGH). It is the pattern most often found when the training of successful endurance athletes is actually measured, and the usual result of normal road cycling, where climbs, group riding and races add tempo and threshold time. In controlled comparisons its results are, on average, not distinguishable from polarized training.

## Key numbers
- Typical target by time: about 70–80% LOW, 15–20% MID, 5–10% HIGH.
- Network meta-analysis of individual data (13 studies, 348 athletes): no difference between pyramidal and polarized for VO2max (standardized difference about −0.06) or time-trial performance (about −0.05).
- Same analysis, subgroup: recreational athletes tended to gain more VO2max with pyramidal, competitive athletes with polarized.
- In trials on trained road cyclists (8–12 weeks, 7.5–11.7 h/week), both pyramidal and polarized distributions were used and no periodization model was clearly better.

## Why it works
MID work (tempo, sweet spot, threshold) accumulates a large amount of time at high oxygen uptake and high muscle fibre recruitment with less strain per minute than HIGH work, so more total quality minutes fit into a week. It is also specific: many cycling events are decided at MID intensity.

## In practice
A pyramidal week for an 8–10 h rider: 1 threshold or sweet-spot session (e.g. 3 × 15 min at 88–95% FTP), 1 VO2max session or hard group ride, the rest LOW. MID time is planned, not accumulated by riding every easy ride slightly too hard.

## Limits
"Pyramidal" by time in zone can hide two very different riders: one doing structured MID sessions on a big LOW base, and one whose easy rides drift upward (see `tid-10-moderate-intensity-rut`). The first is the evidence-backed version.

## Coach guidance
Treat pyramidal as the default description of sound road-cycling training and as fully legitimate. For recreational riders it is at least as well supported as polarized. Check that the MID share comes from intended sessions.
