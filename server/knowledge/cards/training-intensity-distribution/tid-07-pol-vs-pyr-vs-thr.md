## Summary
The consistent finding is that programmes built on a large LOW base outperform MID-dominant programmes. Between the two LOW-based options, polarized and pyramidal, pooled trial data show no reliable difference in VO2max or time-trial performance. Polarized training may have a small edge for VO2max in competitive athletes and over short blocks; pyramidal may suit recreational athletes at least as well. The practical message: total volume, enough easy riding and 2 well-executed quality sessions matter more than the exact split of the hard 20%.

## Key numbers
- Individual-participant network meta-analysis: 13 studies, 348 athletes (150 recreational, 198 competitive; 52 women), VO2max 47–68 mL/kg/min. Polarized vs pyramidal: VO2max and time trial both about zero difference (standardized differences −0.06 and −0.05, not significant). No significant difference between polarized and any other model.
- Subgroup signal: response differed by level; competitive athletes tended to improve VO2max more with polarized, recreational with pyramidal.
- An earlier meta-analysis reported a small advantage of polarized training for VO2peak, mainly in interventions shorter than 12 weeks and in highly trained athletes, with no advantage for other outcomes (details cited from memory; check before quoting).
- Polarized vs threshold-dominant: earlier meta-analysis of randomized trials favoured polarized for time-trial performance (moderate effect).

## The expert debate
- Against "polarized is optimal": measured elite training is mostly pyramidal; no trial shows a specifically polarized split is superior; MID work has strategic value.
- For: athletes need high LOW volume plus measured HIGH work; in their closing statement proponents acknowledged that pyramidal, its near relative, may be as effective and that the best split may depend on the event.
- Both sides agree on a LOW-dominant programme and on rejecting MID-dominant training.

## In practice
Choose by rider and event rather than by doctrine: more HIGH emphasis when the limiter is VO2max or the event has short decisive efforts; more MID emphasis for long climbs, time trials and gran fondos, and for riders who recover poorly from HIGH sessions. Alternate emphases across blocks (see `tid-11-tid-across-season`).

## Limits
Trials last 6–16 weeks with 10–20 athletes per group; year-long effects are untested. Results depend on how zones and distribution were quantified. Few women and few cyclists-only studies.

## Coach guidance
When asked "is polarized better?", answer: not shown to be better than pyramidal; both beat threshold-heavy training. Then individualize.
