## Summary
Thresholds, efficiency and maximal power measured in a fresh state are not constants: they deteriorate during prolonged exercise. Durability is the resistance to that deterioration. After a few hours, the power at the first and second thresholds can be meaningfully lower, oxygen cost higher and sprint power reduced. Riders differ widely in how fast this happens, independently of their fresh values, which makes durability a fourth dimension of endurance fitness.

## Key numbers
- After about 2–3 h of moderate riding, studies report threshold or critical power lowered by roughly 5–10% on average, with individual changes from near 0 to 15%+.
- Efficiency falls and heart rate at fixed power rises 5–10%.
- The decline is larger when prior work includes efforts above threshold and when carbohydrate intake is low.
- In professionals, the size of the decline, not the fresh level, separated more and less successful riders.

## Mechanisms
Glycogen depletion in individual fibres, forcing recruitment of less efficient fibres; impaired calcium handling and contractile function; rising core temperature and dehydration; central fatigue; reduced fat-oxidation capacity relative to demand.

## What improves it
- Higher training volume and regular long rides.
- Hard efforts performed late in long rides.
- Carbohydrate intake of 60–90+ g/h during long sessions and races.
- Heavy strength training (supported by studies showing better late-ride power).
- Heat acclimation and good hydration in warm conditions.

## In practice
- Zones are valid for a fresh rider; in hour 4 the same wattage sits in a higher physiological domain.
- For long events, plan pacing with a durability discount (see `load-12-durability-metrics`).

## Limits
A young research area: definitions, protocols and norms are not standardised; most data come from male professionals. Training studies that target durability directly are few.

## Coach guidance
When long-event performance disappoints despite good test numbers, discuss durability and fuelling before changing threshold training.
