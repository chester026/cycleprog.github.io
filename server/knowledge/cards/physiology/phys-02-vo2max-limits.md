## Summary
VO2max is the highest rate at which the body can take up and use oxygen: the ceiling of aerobic power. In healthy people exercising with large muscle mass it is limited mainly by oxygen delivery, that is, by how much blood the heart pumps and how much oxygen that blood carries, rather than by the muscles' ability to use it. It sets the upper bound of endurance performance; how much of it can be sustained (thresholds) and how much power each litre yields (efficiency) decide the rest.

## Key numbers
- VO2 = cardiac output × arteriovenous oxygen difference.
- Typical values (mL/kg/min): untrained men 35–45, women 30–40; trained amateur cyclists 50–65; elite men 70–85+, elite women 60–75.
- Maximal cardiac output: about 20 L/min untrained, 30–40 L/min in elite endurance athletes; the difference is almost entirely stroke volume.
- Training response: average +15–20% in previously sedentary people over 20 weeks, with individual responses ranging from near 0 to 40%+; about half of that variation is heritable.
- Trained riders: gains of 3–8% from a focused block are realistic; it plateaus after several years while performance keeps improving.
- Ageing: about −10% per decade in inactive adults, roughly half that in people who keep training hard.
- 5-min maximal power is the closest field proxy.

## In practice
- Raise it with HIGH-intensity intervals on top of adequate volume; increase total volume for long-term development.
- Per-kg values rise with fat loss without any change in the engine; absolute L/min matters on the flat.
- Watch-estimated VO2max is a trend indicator with errors of ±5–10%.

## Limits
Among riders of similar level VO2max predicts results poorly. Whether a true plateau is reached in a test depends on motivation and protocol.

## Coach guidance
Present VO2max as the ceiling, not the score. Emphasise threshold, efficiency and durability for riders fixated on a watch estimate.
