## Summary
Muscles burn a mixture of fat and carbohydrate. Fat stores are effectively unlimited but supply energy slowly; carbohydrate stores are small but support high power. As intensity rises the mix shifts toward carbohydrate, and above the second threshold it is almost all carbohydrate. Running out of glycogen ("bonking") forces power down to what fat can sustain. Endurance training raises fat oxidation at every submaximal power, sparing glycogen.

## Key numbers
- Stores: muscle glycogen about 300–500 g (up to 600–700 g trained and loaded); liver 80–120 g; total about 1600–2800 kcal. Body fat: tens of thousands of kcal even in lean riders.
- Maximal fat oxidation ("fatmax"): average about 0.4–0.6 g/min at 45–65% of VO2max in a large sample; trained cyclists 0.5–0.9 g/min; very high values above 1 g/min occur in elite and in fat-adapted athletes.
- Fat oxidation falls toward zero above roughly 80–90% of VO2max.
- Carbohydrate oxidation at threshold and above: 3–5 g/min in trained riders.
- Glycogen lasts roughly 90–120 min at high intensity without intake; longer at lower intensity.
- 1 g carbohydrate = 4 kcal; 1 g fat = 9 kcal. Carbohydrate yields about 5–7% more energy per litre of oxygen.

## In practice
- Any ride with long MID or HIGH content is carbohydrate-limited: fuel it.
- Better fat oxidation comes mainly from training volume; it shows as lower heart rate and lactate at endurance power.
- Fasted or low-carbohydrate training raises fat oxidation markers further but has not been shown to improve performance and costs session quality.
- Early signs of glycogen depletion: rising RPE at constant power, falling power on climbs, irritability, hunger; then a sudden drop.

## Limits
Fatmax values vary several-fold between individuals and day to day with diet. Device estimates of "fat burned" are rough.

## Coach guidance
Link fuelling advice to the carbohydrate cost of the specific ride. Do not promote fat-burning zones as a weight-loss method; energy balance over days decides that.
