## Summary
Fitness declines when training stops, in a predictable order: blood volume within days, VO2max and threshold over weeks, mitochondrial enzymes over weeks, with long-standing structural adaptations lasting longest. A few days off costs nothing measurable and often helps. Losses from 2–4 weeks off are real but return faster than they were first built. Fitness can be held for weeks on much less training if some intensity is kept.

## Key numbers
- Up to about 5–7 days off: no meaningful loss; often better performance through recovery.
- 2 weeks: plasma volume −5–12%; VO2max −4–7%; heart rate at fixed power +5–10 beats; threshold power −3–7%.
- 3–4 weeks: VO2max −7–10% or more in highly trained athletes; mitochondrial enzyme activity −25–40%.
- 8–12 weeks: VO2max down about 15–20% in highly trained athletes, stabilising above untrained levels; capillary density largely retained.
- Maintenance: cutting volume by up to about 60–70% while keeping intensity and at least 2 sessions a week preserved VO2max for up to 15 weeks in classic studies; cutting intensity instead lost fitness quickly.
- Retraining: commonly takes roughly as long as the break, up to twice as long after extended breaks; faster in riders with years of history.

## In practice
- Illness, travel or a busy week: nothing to make up; resume the plan.
- 2–4 weeks off: lower FTP about 5–8%, restart at 50–70% of previous load, rebuild over 2–4 weeks.
- Forced low-time periods: 2 short sessions with intensity per week (e.g. 45–60 min with threshold or VO2max work) hold most fitness.
- An off-season break of 1–3 weeks is normal practice and does not harm the next season.

## Limits
Classic studies were small. Individual rates of loss vary; older athletes may lose and regain more slowly.

## Coach guidance
Reassure after short breaks with these numbers. Present reduced zones after a longer break as temporary, with a concrete rebuild timeline.
