## Summary
Critical power (CP) is the asymptote of the hyperbolic relationship between power and how long it can be held. Physiologically it marks the highest metabolic rate at which oxygen uptake, lactate, phosphocreatine and muscle acidity can still stabilise. Below CP a steady state is reached; above it they drift continuously, oxygen uptake climbs to VO2max, and exhaustion follows once a fixed amount of work, W′, has been spent. This makes CP a true fatigue threshold and W′ a finite battery.

## Key numbers
- Time to exhaustion above CP: t = W′ / (P − CP).
- CP in trained cyclists: about 75–90% of VO2max power. W′: 10–25 kJ.
- Just below CP: tolerable for roughly 30–60 min with stable responses. Just above (even 10–15 W): VO2 rises to maximum and exhaustion typically occurs within about 10–25 min.
- W′ recovery is exponential and depends on how far below CP the rider drops.
- Endurance training raises CP; W′ changes little or may fall slightly; sprint and high-intensity training can raise W′.

## Why W′ is finite
It is not a simple "anaerobic tank". Its depletion coincides with falling phosphocreatine, accumulating phosphate and hydrogen ions, potassium shifts and progressive loss of muscle efficiency (the VO2 slow component) reaching limits the muscle or the brain tolerates.

## In practice
- Small pacing errors above CP are expensive: 5% too hard early in a 20-min climb can halve the time before blowing up.
- Tactics as energy accounting: attacks, gap closings and steep ramps all draw on the same W′.
- Riders with equal CP but different W′ suit different events.

## Limits
The two-parameter model overestimates power for very short (<2 min) and very long (>30 min) durations. CP is not sustainable "indefinitely". Fatigue lowers both CP and W′ during long rides.
