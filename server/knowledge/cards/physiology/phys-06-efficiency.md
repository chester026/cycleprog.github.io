## Summary
Gross efficiency is the share of metabolic energy that reaches the pedals as mechanical work. It is the third determinant of endurance performance alongside VO2max and threshold: two riders with the same oxygen uptake can differ by tens of watts. Efficiency is higher in riders with more slow-twitch fibre, improves modestly with years of training, and declines within a long ride as fatigue sets in.

## Key numbers
- Gross efficiency in cyclists: about 18–25%; most trained riders 20–23%.
- A 1 percentage-point difference (e.g. 21% vs 22%) is worth roughly 4–5% more power at the same oxygen uptake: about 10–15 W at threshold for a typical trained rider.
- Rises with power output (resting metabolism becomes a smaller share), so compare at the same power.
- Seasonal change in trained cyclists: about +1 percentage point from off-season to race season in some studies; larger over many years.
- Falls by about 0.5–1.5 points over 2+ h of hard riding.
- Cadence: metabolically cheapest at 60–80 rpm for moderate power; riders freely choose higher (85–100 rpm), trading some efficiency for lower muscular force.

## What influences it
Share of type I fibres, mitochondrial efficiency, muscle recruitment pattern, cadence, position (aggressive aero positions can reduce it slightly), temperature, and fatigue. Heavy strength training and high training volume are the interventions with the best support.

## In practice
- Efficiency explains why power at threshold can improve when VO2max no longer does.
- It cannot be measured without gas analysis; power at a fixed heart rate is only a loose proxy.
- Calorie estimates from kJ assume about 22–24%; individual error is ±5–10%.

## Limits
Measurement error of gas analysis is of the same size as the training effects, so individual changes are hard to prove. Whether specific pedalling technique training improves efficiency is not supported.

## Coach guidance
Mention efficiency when explaining long-term improvement and durability; avoid promising efficiency gains from drills or gadgets.
