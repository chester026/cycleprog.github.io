## Summary
Intervals of roughly 3–8 minutes at an intensity sustainable for only that long are the classic way to raise maximal oxygen uptake. The aim is to accumulate time near VO2max (commonly defined as above 90%), which takes 1–2 minutes to reach in each repetition. Many controlled trials show clear gains in VO2max and performance within 4–8 weeks.

## Key numbers
- Power: 106–120% FTP (about 85–95% of ramp-test peak power); shorter reps at the higher end. Heart rate reaching 90–95% HRmax in later reps; RPE 8–9.
- Formats: 5–6 × 3 min, 4–6 × 4 min, 5 × 5 min, 4 × 6–8 min (at about 105–108%).
- Recovery: 50–100% of work time, easy spinning.
- Session total: 15–25 min at target; up to 30–32 min for 4 × 8.
- In moderately trained subjects, 4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax three times a week for 8 weeks raised VO2max by about 7%, while matched low-intensity and threshold work did not.
- Frequency: 1–2 per week in blocks of 3–6 weeks.

## Why it works
Maximal stroke-volume demand and high oxygen extraction provide the strongest per-minute stimulus for central cardiovascular adaptation.

## In practice
- Pace by the hardest power that can be held evenly for all reps ("isoeffort"); the last rep should be very hard but completed.
- Slightly longer reps at slightly lower power (4 × 8) give more total time at high heart rate and suit many amateurs better than all-out 3-min efforts.
- Higher cadence (95–105 rpm) helps reach high oxygen uptake.
- Start rested and fuelled; follow with an easy day.

## Limits
Heart rate lags, so judge short reps by power and effort. Gains plateau after about 6–8 weeks of the same stimulus. Highly trained riders respond less in VO2max and more in performance.

## Coach guidance
Prescribe by rider level: begin with 4 × 4 or 4 × 5 and build total time before intensity.
