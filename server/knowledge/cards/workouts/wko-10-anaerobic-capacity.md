## Summary
Efforts of 30 seconds to 2 minutes well above VO2max power, with long recoveries, train the ability to produce and tolerate very high power for short periods: attacks, short climbs, bridging gaps. They enlarge and speed the use of the work capacity above critical power. The adaptations come quickly and fade quickly, so this work belongs close to the events that need it.

## Key numbers
- Power: 130–170% FTP for 1–2 min efforts; near-maximal for 30–45 s; RPE 9–10.
- Formats: 6–8 × 1 min with 3–4 min recovery; 4–6 × 2 min with 4–5 min; 8–10 × 30–40 s with 2–3 min.
- Session total: 6–12 min of work. Quality drops sharply beyond that.
- Timing: 4–6 weeks of 1 session per week gives most of the benefit; maintain with races or one session every 7–10 days.

## Why it works
Maximal glycolytic flux, high lactate and hydrogen-ion accumulation, and full fibre recruitment improve buffering, enzyme activity and the neural ability to repeat hard efforts.

## In practice
- Full recoveries protect quality: if power drops more than about 8–10% from the first rep, stop.
- Good for road racers, criterium riders, mountain bikers, cyclocross; low priority for long steady events.
- Place after a rest or easy day, never after a long depleting ride.

## Limits
High neuromuscular and metabolic cost; riders with small training volume or masters riders need 48–72 h before the next hard day. Heart rate is uninformative for these durations.

## Coach guidance
Recommend only when the target event contains such efforts. For gran fondo and long-climb goals, direct the effort budget to threshold and VO2max work instead.
