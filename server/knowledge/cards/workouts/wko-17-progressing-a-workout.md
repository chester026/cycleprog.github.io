## Summary
A workout type should get slightly harder over a block in one dimension at a time. The usual order is: more total time at intensity, then longer continuous blocks, then shorter recoveries, and only last higher power. Progress is earned by completing the current step well, not by the calendar.

## Key numbers
- Step size: +10–20% work time per week (one extra rep, or 2–5 min per block).
- Block length: 3–6 weeks on one workout type, with an easier week every 3rd or 4th week (reduce work time by 30–50%, keep intensity).
- Power increases: 2–3% (about 5–10 W) after the upper time range is reached or FTP is retested.
- Example, threshold: 3 × 10 → 3 × 12 → 2 × 20 → (easy week 2 × 12) → 3 × 15 → 2 × 25.
- Example, VO2max: 4 × 4 → 5 × 4 → 5 × 5 → (easy week 3 × 4) → 6 × 5 or 4 × 8.

## In practice
- Advance when the last session was completed on target with a stable last rep and RPE no higher than expected.
- Repeat the same step if the last rep faded or RPE was maximal; step back after illness or a missed week.
- After a rest week, resume at the last completed step, not beyond it.
- Change workout type after 4–8 weeks or when gains stall; return to it later.

## Limits
Linear progression cannot continue indefinitely; life stress and cumulative fatigue interrupt it. Rules here are practice-based.

## Coach guidance
Base the next session on how the previous one actually went. State the single change ("one more rep at the same power") and why.
