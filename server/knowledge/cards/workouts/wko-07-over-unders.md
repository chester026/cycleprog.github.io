## Summary
Over-unders alternate short periods slightly above threshold with periods slightly below it, without full recovery. They imitate what racing and hard climbing demand: surge, then keep working while the body clears the disturbance. The rationale is sound in terms of the critical power model; direct training studies of the format are lacking, so it rests on coaching practice.

## Key numbers
- Over: 105–110% FTP for 1–2 min. Under: 88–95% FTP for 2–4 min.
- Blocks: 3–4 cycles = 9–16 min per block; 2–4 blocks; 4–6 min easy between blocks.
- Session total at intensity: 25–45 min.
- Variants: climbing-style (2 min under / 30 s over at 120%); race-style (3 min at 100% with a 15-s surge each minute).

## Why it works
Each "over" draws on W′ and raises lactate; the "under" forces partial recovery at a high metabolic rate rather than at rest, training lactate clearance and tolerance of power fluctuations near threshold.

## In practice
- Introduce after a rider handles 3 × 12–15 min steady threshold.
- The "under" is the key part: if power there drops below about 85% FTP, the overs are too hard.
- Useful in the 6–8 weeks before road races, punchy gran fondos and group-ride goals.
- At most once a week; counts as a hard day.

## Limits
Highly sensitive to FTP accuracy: a 3% error turns the under into threshold and the session becomes unsustainable.

## Coach guidance
Offer over-unders to riders who say they "can't recover after surges". Check FTP is current before prescribing.
