## Summary
One ride per week that is clearly longer than the others is the main tool for building durability: holding power, efficiency and fuelling late into an effort. It is also the rehearsal for long events: position, nutrition, pacing and equipment are tested here.

## Key numbers
- Duration: about 25–35% of weekly hours; 2.5–3 h for a 7–8 h rider, 4–6 h for a 12–15 h rider.
- Event preparation: reach about 75–85% of expected event duration (or event kJ) at least 2–3 times, the last one 2–3 weeks before.
- Intensity: LOW throughout, or LOW with planned MID segments late in the ride.
- Progression: add 20–30 min per week for 2–3 weeks, then an easier week.
- Fuel: 60–90 g carbohydrate per hour from the first hour for rides over 3 h; 500–750 ml fluid per hour, more in heat.

## Why it works
The later hours add stimulus the first hours cannot: recruitment of additional motor units as fibres fatigue, glycogen depletion signalling, practice of fat oxidation and gut tolerance, and resilience of posture and contact points.

## In practice
- Variants: steady LOW; "fast finish" with the last 30–45 min at tempo or sweet spot; 2–3 × 15–20 min sweet spot in the final third to train fatigued power.
- Judge success by the last hour: stable power, drift under about 5–8%, no bonk.
- Back-to-back long days (e.g. 3 h + 3 h) are an alternative when a single very long ride is impractical.

## Limits
Under-fuelled long rides raise illness risk and blunt the following days' training. Above about 5–6 h the recovery cost rises faster than the benefit for most amateurs.

## Coach guidance
Ask about fuelling whenever late-ride power fades. Recommend extending duration gradually and celebrate new longest rides.
