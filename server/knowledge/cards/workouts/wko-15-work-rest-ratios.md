## Summary
Recovery duration decides what an interval session trains. Short recoveries keep oxygen uptake high and make the session more aerobic; long recoveries restore the anaerobic reserve and protect power on the next effort. The recovery is chosen for the goal, and it does not need to feel complete.

## Key numbers
- Threshold and sweet spot: recovery 20–35% of work time (2–5 min after 10–20 min).
- VO2max reps of 3–8 min: 50–100% of work time. In well-trained runners doing 6 × 4 min, lengthening rest from 2 to 4 min did not allow meaningfully higher work intensity; self-selected rest was about 2 min.
- Short intervals: 2:1 work:rest within sets (30/15, 40/20); 3–5 min between sets.
- Anaerobic capacity (30 s–2 min): 1:2 to 1:4.
- Sprints (5–15 s): 3–5 min, i.e. 1:20 or more.
- Recovery intensity: 40–55% FTP. Active recovery clears lactate faster; completely passive rest restores phosphocreatine slightly better for maximal sprints.

## In practice
- If later reps fade more than about 5–8%, lengthen recovery before lowering the target.
- If reps feel easy and heart rate falls far between reps, shorten recovery by 30–60 s as a progression.
- Fixed recoveries make sessions comparable over weeks.
- Indoors, keep pedalling in recoveries; outdoors, descending counts as passive rest.

## Limits
Optimal recovery differs with fitness: fitter riders recover faster. Data on recovery duration come largely from runners.

## Coach guidance
When analysing a session, check recoveries as well as work blocks: skipped or stretched recoveries explain many deviations from plan.
