## Summary
Sweet spot is the upper part of MID, just under threshold. The name reflects a practical trade-off: nearly the stimulus of threshold work with noticeably less strain, so more minutes can be accumulated and repeated during the week. It is a coaching construct rather than a physiological boundary, widely used and effective for raising sustainable power, especially on limited time.

## Key numbers
- Power: 88–94% FTP (range in use 84–97%); RPE 5–6.
- Formats: 3 × 12–15 min, 2 × 20, 3 × 20, 2 × 30, up to 60–90 min total for advanced riders; recoveries 3–5 min.
- Progression: +5 min per block or one extra block per week; raise power only after reaching about 60 min total.
- Frequency: 1–2 sessions a week; up to 3 in a short dedicated block.

## Why it works
High oxygen uptake and lactate turnover just below the point where fatigue accumulates rapidly; long time under load for fast-oxidative fibres.

## In practice
- Good first structured workout for newer riders: demanding but controllable.
- Hold even power; finishing each block feeling one more would be possible is right.
- Indoors works particularly well; outdoors needs a steady road or climb.
- Pair with at least one session containing HIGH work in most weeks.

## Limits
Programmes made mostly of sweet spot did worse than LOW-based programmes in trials of trained athletes; gains tend to flatten after 6–8 weeks. An inflated FTP turns sweet spot into threshold.

## Coach guidance
Endorse sweet spot as a tool. Watch for it replacing all other intensities for months, and for sessions repeatedly failed (FTP likely set too high).
