## Summary
A warm-up raises muscle temperature and speeds oxygen-uptake kinetics so that the first hard effort is aerobic sooner and feels easier. It matters most before short, intense efforts and matters little before long steady rides. Long, hard traditional warm-ups can cause fatigue; shorter ones with a few brief efforts work as well or better. Cool-downs are optional: reviews find little effect on recovery, soreness or next-day performance.

## Key numbers
- Before intervals or races under about 60 min: 15–25 min total: 10–15 min easy building to about 70% FTP, then 2–3 × 1 min at about 100–110% FTP or 3 × 10-s accelerations, with 1–2 min easy between, finishing 5–10 min before the start.
- Priming: one 3–5 min effort at about 90–105% FTP, 6–15 min before the event, improves oxygen-uptake kinetics; too hard or too close impairs performance.
- Before long events (3 h+): 5–10 min easy or none; the first kilometres serve as warm-up.
- In track cyclists, a shorter, lower-intensity warm-up (about 15 min) gave higher subsequent peak power than a traditional 50-min warm-up.
- Cool-down: 5–10 min easy spinning if desired.
- Heat: shorten the warm-up and use cooling (ice vest, cold drinks); cold: lengthen and keep clothing on.

## In practice
- Before training intervals, 10–15 min easy plus 2–3 short pick-ups is enough; the first interval may be slightly below target.
- Masters riders and morning sessions benefit from a few extra minutes.
- Rehearse the race warm-up in training.

## Limits
Optimal priming intensity and timing are individual. Evidence for cool-down effects on adaptation is thin.

## Coach guidance
Exclude warm-up and cool-down segments when judging a session's quality. Do not treat a skipped cool-down as a fault.
