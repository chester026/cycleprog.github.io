## Summary
A recovery ride is very easy, short riding on a day after hard training or racing. Evidence that it speeds physiological recovery compared with complete rest is weak; its real value is keeping routine, loosening stiff legs, adding a little volume and, for many riders, mental refreshment. It works only if it is truly easy. A day off is an equally valid choice.

## Key numbers
- Power: under 55% FTP (Coggan Z1); heart rate under about 65% HRmax; RPE 1–2.
- Duration: 30–60 min; up to 90 min for high-volume riders.
- Cadence: comfortable, light gear, flat route.
- TSS: usually 15–35.

## In practice
- Flat route or indoor trainer; small chainring on any rise.
- If a rider cannot keep it this easy outdoors (hills, company), rest is the better option.
- Riders training 3–5 times a week generally gain more from a rest day; recovery rides fit riders on 6+ sessions.
- Signs to take full rest instead: elevated resting heart rate, heavy fatigue, illness symptoms, poor sleep.

## Limits
No good trials show performance benefits of recovery rides over rest in cyclists. Individual preference matters more than any rule.

## Coach guidance
Never push intensity on a recovery day and never describe a recovery ride's low numbers as poor. Offer the choice between an easy spin and rest without ranking them.
