## Summary
Threshold intervals are sustained efforts at or just below the second threshold. They raise the power a rider can hold for 20–60 min, the quantity most cycling events reward. The most productive version is controlled, slightly under the limit, so that total time at intensity is high and the session can be repeated.

## Key numbers
- Power: 95–100% FTP (controlled version 90–98%); heart rate reaching about 95–100% of threshold HR by the end of blocks; RPE 6–7.
- Formats: 4 × 8 min, 3 × 10–12, 2 × 15–20, 3 × 15, 2 × 20–25; recoveries about 25–50% of work time.
- Session total: 20–30 min for newer riders, 30–45 min typical, up to 60 min advanced.
- Trial in recreational cyclists (7 weeks, 2 sessions/week): 4 × 8 min at about 90% HRmax gave larger overall gains in VO2peak and threshold power (about 11%) than 4 × 16 min at lower or 4 × 4 min at higher intensity (about 5–6%).

## Why it works
Near-maximal sustainable oxygen uptake and lactate clearance, large stroke-volume demand, and long time under high tension for the fibres that decide sustainable power.

## In practice
- Even pacing; start at the low end of the range for the first block.
- Shorter recoveries (2–3 min) keep oxygen uptake elevated; they do not need to be full.
- Use event-like terrain and position for time-trial and climbing goals.
- 1–2 sessions per week; rarely on consecutive days.

## Limits
Riding above the true threshold turns the session into a much harder one with less total time. FTP from a 20-min test overestimates threshold in some riders.

## Coach guidance
Value completed minutes at target over heroic first blocks. If the last block fades by more than about 5%, lower the target 2–3% next time.
