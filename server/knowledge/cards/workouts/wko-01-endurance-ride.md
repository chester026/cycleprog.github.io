## Summary
The endurance ride is steady riding below the first threshold and makes up most of a cyclist's training time. Its job is to add aerobic volume at low recovery cost. Duration is the main dose variable: the ride gets more valuable as it gets longer, not as it gets harder.

## Key numbers
- Power: about 55–75% FTP (Coggan Z2); heart rate about 65–75% HRmax; RPE 2–3; conversation possible.
- Duration: 60–90 min is a useful minimum; 2–4 h is the typical working range; several per week.
- Cadence: self-selected, usually 80–95 rpm.
- Fuel: water only is fine under 90 min after a normal meal; 30–60 g carbohydrate per hour beyond that.

## Why it works
Sustained low-intensity work drives mitochondrial and capillary adaptations through duration, expands plasma volume, and progressively recruits more muscle fibres as the ride goes on, all with little autonomic stress.

## In practice
- Set a ceiling, not a target: stay under the top of the range on climbs by using easier gears.
- Riding the lower half of the range (55–65% FTP) is still effective and is preferable on days between hard sessions.
- Progress by adding 15–30 min to one or two rides per week before adding intensity.
- Indoors, 60–90 min is often enough; add cooling and a slightly lower power target if heart rate drifts.

## Limits
With under about 5 h a week, endurance rides alone give limited stimulus. The LOW ceiling as a percentage of FTP is individual (see `tid-01-three-zone-model`).

## Coach guidance
Treat every endurance ride as valid training. Suggest capping rather than chasing power. Offer duration as the first progression.
