## Summary
Tempo is sustained riding in the lower part of MID: clearly above the first threshold, comfortably below the second. It is the intensity of long climbs in a gran fondo, breakaways and strong group rides. As planned work it builds the ability to hold moderately high power for a long time; as an accident, it is what easy rides drift into.

## Key numbers
- Power: 76–87% FTP (Coggan Z3); heart rate about 80–88% of threshold HR; RPE 4–5; speaking in short sentences.
- Formats: 2–3 × 20–30 min with 5 min easy; continuous 40–90 min; tempo finish in a long ride.
- Weekly dose when used: 60–150 min at intensity.
- Carbohydrate use is high: 60–90 g/h for sessions over 75 min.

## Why it works
Large accumulated time at high oxygen uptake and substantial recruitment of fast-oxidative fibres, at a strain that allows long blocks. Trains fuelling and pacing at event intensity.

## In practice
- Best uses: base and early build for riders targeting long events; riders who cannot yet tolerate threshold work; muscular endurance on long climbs.
- Progress by extending block length before raising power.
- Low-cadence tempo (60–70 rpm) on climbs is a common variant (see `wko-12-low-cadence`).
- Keep the surrounding days easy; tempo is not a recovery intensity.

## Limits
No trials isolate tempo against sweet spot or threshold at matched load. Recovery cost per minute is lower than threshold but far above LOW.

## Coach guidance
Recommend tempo deliberately for event-specific endurance. When it appears unplanned on every ride, address it as drift, kindly and only if the rider wants to improve.
