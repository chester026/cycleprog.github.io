## Summary
For each intensity there is a range of accumulated work time per session that gives a solid stimulus without disproportionate fatigue. Intensity and duration trade off: slightly lower intensity allows much more accumulated time, and the product of the two, not maximal intensity, drives adaptation. Riders should work in these ranges and progress within them.

## Key numbers (work time per session)
| Type | Intensity | Starter | Typical | Upper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tempo | 76–87% FTP | 30 min | 45–90 min | 120+ min |
| Sweet spot | 88–94% | 24–30 min | 40–60 min | 90 min |
| Threshold | 95–100% | 16–20 min | 30–45 min | 60 min |
| VO2max | 106–120% | 12 min | 16–25 min | 30–32 min |
| Short intervals | 115–130% | 8–10 min | 15–20 min | 25 min |
| Anaerobic | 130%+ | 4–6 min | 6–10 min | 12 min |

- Per week: MID + HIGH work time combined usually 40–120 min for amateurs, in 2 (sometimes 3) sessions.
- In recreational cyclists, 4 × 8 min (32 min at about 90% HRmax) produced larger gains than 4 × 4 min at higher intensity or 4 × 16 min at lower intensity over 7 weeks (about 11% versus 5–6% overall gain).
- Goal for VO2max sessions: about 10+ min above 90% of VO2max.

## In practice
- New to structure: start at "starter", add one rep or 2–5 min per week.
- When a rider reaches the upper range comfortably, raise power 2–3% and drop back to typical.
- Two different types in one session (e.g. threshold then short sprints) are fine; reduce each.

## Limits
Ranges are syntheses of trials and practice; individual tolerance varies with volume, age and history.

## Coach guidance
Use the table to sanity-check planned and completed sessions. A session far above the upper range is flagged as a recovery consideration, not as an error.
