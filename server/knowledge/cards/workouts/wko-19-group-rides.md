## Summary
Group rides produce a stochastic load: long stretches of low power in the draft, repeated surges above threshold, and much time in the middle. They build race skills, pack confidence and motivation better than any solo workout and can deliver a strong high-intensity stimulus. Their weakness is that the intensity is set by the group, so they are neither a reliable easy day nor a controlled interval session.

## Key numbers
- Drafting saves about 25–40% of power at the same speed behind one rider, more inside a bunch.
- Typical fast group ride: IF 0.75–0.90, variability index 1.15–1.35, 15–30% of time coasting or under 50% FTP, 5–15% above FTP.
- Count a hard group ride as one of the week's 2 quality sessions.
- A hard 2–3 h group ride commonly needs 24–48 h before the next quality session.

## In practice
- Decide the ride's purpose in advance: hard day (take pulls, contest hills) or easy day (sit in, skip the surges, or choose a slower group).
- Analyse by time above threshold, number of efforts above 120% FTP, and kJ above FTP, not by average power.
- Useful targets inside a group ride: longer turns at threshold on the front; sprinting for signs; staying seated on climbs at tempo.
- If the weekly group ride is non-negotiable, build the rest of the week around it: one structured session plus easy riding.

## Limits
Average power and TSS under-describe the neuromuscular cost of repeated accelerations. Safety and etiquette constrain what can be practised.

## Coach guidance
Respect the social value of group rides; never frame them as wasted training. Help the rider place them in the week and read them with the right metrics.
