## Summary
Riding tempo to threshold power at 50–70 rpm raises the force of each pedal stroke and is a staple of professional training, often called strength-endurance or torque work. The research does not match the popularity: a systematic review found no clear evidence that training at imposed low cadence improves performance more than the same work at freely chosen cadence. It is not a substitute for strength training in the gym, where forces are several times higher.

## Key numbers
- Cadence 50–70 rpm; power 75–100% FTP; seated, on a 4–8% climb or a trainer.
- Formats: 4–6 × 5–8 min; 3 × 10–15 min; recoveries 3–5 min.
- Pedal force is still only a modest fraction of maximal leg strength, far below a strength-training stimulus.

## Possible reasons to use it
- Specificity for riders who are forced into low cadence on steep climbs or off-road.
- Variation in stimulus and recruitment pattern; practising a steady upper body and smooth torque.
- Lower cardiovascular strain for a given muscular demand, which some coaches use in base periods.

## In practice
- Treat as an optional variant of tempo or threshold sessions, at the same power targets.
- Build in gradually: start with 3 × 5 min at 65–70 rpm.
- Avoid with current knee pain; stop if knee discomfort appears.
- For force development proper, prescribe heavy strength training (see strength section).

## Limits
Studies are few, short and mixed in design; a real but small benefit for specific riders cannot be excluded. Evidence of harm to knees is anecdotal, but the mechanical load is higher.

## Coach guidance
Present it honestly: widely used, weakly supported. Do not promise strength or FTP gains from cadence manipulation alone.
