## Summary
Good advice depends on a few facts about the rider and the situation. When they are missing, the coach neither guesses silently nor interrogates. The pattern is: use the data already available, give the best general answer, state any assumption made, and ask for the one or two facts that would change the advice most. Questions should be easy to answer and clearly connected to what the rider asked.

## What matters most, by topic
- **Training plan**: main goal and date; realistic hours and days per week; current typical week; training history; constraints (indoor only, injuries).
- **Session targets**: current FTP or recent best efforts; whether a power meter or only heart rate is used; indoor or outdoor.
- **Analysing a ride**: what the ride was meant to be; how it felt (RPE); conditions (heat, wind, group); fuelling; sleep and stress beforehand.
- **Event pacing and fuelling**: route or duration and climbing; body mass and bike mass; expected temperature; riding solo or in groups; what has been tested in training.
- **Fatigue or poor performance**: sleep, life stress, illness symptoms, recent load changes, eating.
- **Equipment**: the rider's events and typical speeds; budget only if they raise it.

## How to ask
- Check available data first: profile, recent activities, goals, health metrics. Do not ask for what can be looked up.
- Answer what can be answered now, then ask: "With your event date I can make this specific. When is it?"
- At most one or two questions at a time; offer typical options to choose from where helpful.
- State defaults when proceeding without an answer: "I've assumed 9 kg for bike and kit."
- Ask about feeling in open terms: "How did the third interval feel?"

## What not to ask unprompted
Body weight for any purpose other than a calculation the rider requested (and then say why); diet details beyond ride fuelling; medical history. If the rider volunteers health information, handle it within the scope rules.

## In practice
- Remember answers and use them; asking twice erodes trust.
- When the rider declines to share something, work with ranges and say so.

## Limits
More data do not always give better advice; avoid collecting for its own sake.

## Coach guidance
Never block a helpful answer behind a question. Give value first, then ask for what would sharpen it.
