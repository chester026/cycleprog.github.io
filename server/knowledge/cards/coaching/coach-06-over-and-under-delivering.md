## Summary
A target range is a guide to the intended stimulus, not a ceiling to be punished for crossing. In cycling, a rider who holds power above the range with good form has shown capacity and earned a higher target. Riding above the range is therefore acknowledged positively. Only falling short is flagged, and it is flagged as something to understand, with likely causes, never as a failing of the rider.

## How to read each case
- **Within range, even across blocks**: on plan. Confirm and progress as scheduled.
- **Above range, stable or rising across blocks, form intact**: positive. Say so, and raise the next target 2–3% or add work time. Mark as exceeded (a distinct positive colour, not a warning).
- **Above range early, then fading below**: a pacing lesson, framed constructively: the capacity is there, the distribution cost the later blocks. Suggest starting at the middle of the range.
- **Slightly below range (within about 3–5%) but completed**: normal variation; note conditions; no change needed unless repeated.
- **Clearly below range or cut short**: explain, do not judge. Check in order: fatigue from recent days, sleep and stress, fuelling, heat or indoor cooling, illness onset, FTP set too high, session too ambitious. Adjust the next target or the surrounding days.
- **Repeatedly below across sessions**: zones probably need lowering (see `zt-12-when-to-retest`) or recovery needs raising.

## One caution about going over
Exceeding targets is good in quality sessions. On days meant to be easy, the purpose is recovery, so there is no target to beat; and if every session is ridden far above plan, later sessions in the week may suffer. Mention this only when the data show the following sessions declining.

## In practice
- Report per block, with numbers, and lead with the strongest block.
- For exceeded targets, name the size ("8 W above the top of the range in all four") and the consequence ("next week 3 × 12 at 260–270").
- For shortfalls, give one probable cause and one adjustment.

## Limits
Outdoor terrain and traffic produce apparent over- and under-shoots that are not about the rider.

## Coach guidance
Reward over-delivery explicitly. Treat under-delivery as a puzzle to solve together. Never use red-flag language for either.
