## Summary
Riding easy is what most good training consists of, and it is the rider's decision. An analysis that compares an easy spin with the rider's harder efforts and marks it as below standard is wrong on the physiology and discouraging in effect. The coach reads every ride against its own purpose. A low-intensity ride is assessed for what it contributes, aerobic volume, recovery, durability, enjoyment, and never flagged for not being hard.

## Rules for analysing easy rides
- Do not compare power on an easy ride with the rider's bests, with FTP or with interval targets as if they were expected.
- No negative labels, warning colours or "below target" markers for low power, low heart rate, low intensity factor or low TSS on a ride that was easy by choice or by plan.
- Positive framing that is true: time accumulated in the endurance range, steadiness, low heart-rate drift, a new longest ride, good fuelling, a recovery day respected.
- If the ride was planned as hard and came out easy, ask what happened before interpreting; fatigue, weather, company and mood are all legitimate.
- If no plan exists, assume the ride was what the rider wanted.
- Short hard moments inside an easy ride are noted neutrally or not at all.

## When to say something
Only two patterns merit a gentle comment, and only if the rider wants to improve:
- Easy rides that are consistently not easy (drifting into MID), together with fatigue or stalled hard sessions (see `tid-10-moderate-intensity-rut`).
- Weeks made up only of easy riding at low volume when the rider has stated a performance goal; then offer, not prescribe, one or two harder sessions.

## Why it matters
Easy volume is the base of adaptation at low recovery cost; riders who feel judged for it either stop logging rides or turn every ride into a moderate effort, which is worse for progress and for enjoyment.

## Limits
Without a stated plan or goal, intent has to be inferred; when unsure, ask.

## Coach guidance
Describe what the easy ride added and, if useful, one optional idea for next time. The rider chooses how hard to ride; the coach's analysis respects that choice.
