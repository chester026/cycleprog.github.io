## Summary
A number alone tells a rider little: "5-minute power 310 W" has no meaning until it is placed in motion. The same value becomes informative and motivating as "up 12 W in six weeks, your best since last June, and held after two hours of riding for the first time". Feedback research shows that feedback directed at the task and at progress helps, while feedback that reads as a verdict on the person can reduce performance. The coach therefore presents every key figure with its direction, its context and what it makes possible next.

## The pattern for any metric
1. **Value**: what it is now, rounded sensibly.
2. **Direction**: change over a meaningful window (4–8 weeks; season; year on year), with a plain word for confidence: clear, probable, too early to tell.
3. **Reference**: against the rider's own history (recent best, season best, all-time), not against other people.
4. **Cause**: what in their training plausibly produced it.
5. **Meaning**: what it changes in real riding: a climb time, a group they can stay with, a pace for their event.
6. **Next**: the one thing that would move it further.

## Applying it
- Standard durations (5 s, 1, 5, 20, 60 min): show each with trend and reference, and highlight the one that moved most.
- Endurance: "at 140 bpm you now ride 185 W, 13 W more than in March".
- Durability: "your last-hour power fell 4% instead of 9%".
- Consistency: "ten weeks without a missed long ride" is a result.
- Flat periods: describe neutrally with what changed (load, sleep, heat, a break) and the plan.
- Declines: state plainly, give the likely temporary causes and the expected timeline back.

## In practice
- Lead with the most encouraging true finding.
- Use small wins: bests at any duration, bests under fatigue, smoother pacing, better fuelling.
- Avoid dumping tables; two or three figures with meaning beat twenty without.
- Keep honesty intact: never present noise as progress (see `load-15-progress-vs-noise`).

## Limits
Trends need comparable conditions and enough data; early-season or sparse data limit what can be said.

## Coach guidance
Never give a bare figure when a trend is available. The aim is that the rider finishes reading wanting to ride again, with an accurate picture of where they stand.
