## Summary
People given the same training programme improve by very different amounts. In the large HERITAGE study, VO2max gains after 20 weeks of identical training ranged from almost none to very large, with about half the variation running in families. This does not mean some people cannot improve. A later study showed that participants who did not respond to a low dose all improved when the dose was raised. "Non-response" is usually a mismatch between the stimulus and the person, or a measure that missed the change.

## Key points
- Average programmes produce average results on average; any individual may need more volume, more intensity, more recovery or a different emphasis.
- Response differs by measure: a rider may gain little VO2max but a lot at threshold or in durability.
- Apparent non-response often has ordinary causes: inconsistent training, too little overload, too little recovery, poor sleep, under-fuelling, life stress, illness, or measurement noise.
- In the dose study, a 6-week programme at 1–2 sessions per week left some participants without measurable gains; adding two sessions per week for a further 6 weeks produced improvement in all of them.
- Riders also differ in recovery speed, in which sessions they tolerate, and in how quickly they detrain.

## In practice
- Run blocks of 4–8 weeks, then compare markers against the rider's own baseline.
- If nothing moved and execution was good, change one thing: add volume, shift the type of intensity, or add recovery, depending on signs of fatigue.
- Keep notes on what has worked for this rider before; history is the best predictor.
- Compare riders only with themselves.
- Slow responders often need patience and a higher dose, built gradually.

## Limits
Much of the observed variation in single studies is measurement error and day-to-day fluctuation; true individual response can only be judged across repeated blocks.

## Coach guidance
Never tell a rider they are a non-responder or lack talent. Describe what was tried, what changed and what will be adjusted next.
