## Summary
The best plan is the one the rider actually follows. Adherence is mostly a property of the fit between plan and life, and of how the rider experiences the coaching relationship: research on exercise behaviour consistently links long-term adherence to feeling in control, capable and supported. A coach raises adherence by making the plan realistic, giving choice, explaining purpose, noticing what was done and responding to gaps as information.

## What raises adherence
- **Fit**: sessions sized to the time and energy the rider has on that day of the week; the plan built from their real schedule.
- **Purpose**: one sentence on why each session exists.
- **Choice**: alternatives of equal value (indoor or outdoor, intervals or a hard group ride, today or tomorrow).
- **Early wins**: targets in the first weeks that are very likely to be met.
- **Feedback that is specific and prompt**: what went well, in the rider's own numbers.
- **Flexibility rules agreed in advance**: what to do when time is short, when tired, when a day is missed.
- **Enjoyment**: at least one ride a week that the rider would choose anyway.
- **Connection**: acknowledging life events, remembering goals, a consistent tone.

## What lowers it
Plans that assume ideal weeks; too many hard sessions; unexplained prescriptions; criticism or silence after missed sessions; constant changes; goals the rider did not choose; comparison with others.

## Reading adherence data
- 80–90% of planned sessions over a month is excellent.
- Repeatedly missed same-day sessions point to a scheduling problem.
- Sessions started but cut short point to targets set too high or fatigue.
- Easy rides ridden too hard point to unclear purpose or social riding; ask before assuming.
- A sudden drop after good consistency usually has a life cause.

## In practice
- Ask "what got in the way?" and "what would make this week workable?" rather than "why didn't you?".
- Shrink the plan before it breaks; rebuild from what is being done.

## Limits
Evidence comes mainly from general exercise settings; coaching-specific trials are few.

## Coach guidance
Treat every gap between plan and execution as feedback about the plan. Reinforce what the rider did do.
