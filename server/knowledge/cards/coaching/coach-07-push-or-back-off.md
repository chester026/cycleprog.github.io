## Summary
Coaching decisions come down to a repeated question: add load, hold, or reduce. No single metric answers it. The decision combines three kinds of evidence: how the rider is performing in key sessions, how they say they feel, and the context of load and life. Subjective wellbeing has been shown to track training response at least as well as objective markers, so the rider's report carries real weight. When signals conflict, the conservative choice costs little; the aggressive one can cost weeks.

## Signals to add load
- Key sessions completed at or above target with stable final blocks and normal RPE.
- Heart rate responding normally; power at a given heart rate steady or improving.
- Good sleep, stable mood, appetite normal, motivation high.
- At least 2–3 weeks at the current load without warning signs.
- Life stress manageable for the coming weeks.

## Signals to hold
- Sessions completed but with rising RPE or fading last blocks.
- A busy or stressful life period.
- Recent load increase not yet absorbed (under 2 weeks).
- Mixed signals.

## Signals to reduce
- Power down in key sessions for more than a week despite effort; heart rate suppressed or unusually high.
- Two or more of: poor sleep, elevated resting heart rate, suppressed HRV trend, irritability, loss of motivation, heavy legs, frequent colds.
- Illness, pain, or any health red flag.
- Rider says they feel worn down, regardless of what the numbers show.

## How to adjust
- Add: one variable, 5–15% (time before intensity).
- Hold: repeat the week.
- Reduce: 30–50% less volume for 3–7 days, keep one short quality touch if the rider feels up to it; reassess.
- Before a key event, err toward less.

## In practice
- State the reasoning: "two strong sessions and good sleep, so we add one interval".
- After reducing, look for the rebound; if absent after a week, extend and consider health causes.
- Let the rider overrule toward rest at any time.

## Limits
Thresholds are judgement, not rules; individuals differ in how they show fatigue.

## Coach guidance
Make a clear recommendation and explain it in one or two sentences. When uncertain, choose the option that keeps the rider healthy and willing to ride tomorrow.
