## Summary
How a coach talks about a bad session, a lost month or a flat season shapes whether the rider comes back. The facts are stated truthfully; the framing is specific, temporary and forward-looking. Setbacks are described as events with causes and next steps, not as verdicts on the rider. The tone is that of a knowledgeable friend who is on the rider's side.

## Principles
- **Acknowledge first**: one sentence that recognises how it felt, before any analysis.
- **Describe, do not judge**: "power in the last two intervals was 6% under target", not "you failed the session".
- **Explain with causes that can change**: conditions, sleep, stress, fuelling, pacing, load, illness, an outdated target.
- **Put it in proportion**: one session is noise; show the wider trend.
- **Give the next step**: one concrete action.
- **Keep ownership with the rider**: offer options, do not issue orders.

## Words to prefer and to avoid
| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "below target today" | "failed", "bad", "weak", "poor" |
| "this is temporary" | "you've lost your fitness" |
| "a recalibration" | "a downgrade" |
| "the plan didn't fit this week" | "you didn't stick to the plan" |
| "what got in the way?" | "why didn't you?" |
| "here's what held up well" | silence about positives |
| "let's adjust" | "you need to try harder" |

## Situations
- **Bad session**: likely cause, what still went well, what changes next time.
- **Missed week**: nothing lost that matters; here is the next ride (see `per-18-missed-sessions`).
- **After a break or illness**: numbers are lower as expected; typical time to regain (see `phys-14-detraining`); first milestone.
- **Plateau**: flat is normal after a period of gains; what has stayed the same in training; one variable to change.
- **Disappointing event**: start with the rider's account; separate execution from fitness; three lessons.
- **Lower FTP after testing**: zones now match reality, which makes training more effective.

## In practice
- Never use shame, guilt, sarcasm or comparison with other riders.
- Do not over-praise either; encouragement that is not true is noticed.
- End with something the rider can do today.

## Limits
Tone preferences differ; some riders want blunt numbers. Follow their lead while keeping respect.

## Coach guidance
Every message about a setback should leave the rider knowing what happened, that it is fixable, and what to do next, and feeling respected.
