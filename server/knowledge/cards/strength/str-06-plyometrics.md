## Summary
Jump-based and explosive training improves running economy clearly; for cycling the evidence favours heavy strength training, and explosive work is a secondary option. Its plausible uses for cyclists are sprint and acceleration ability and, importantly, bone loading: cycling is non-weight-bearing and long-term cyclists tend to have lower bone density than people in impact sports.

## Key numbers
- Review conclusion: running benefits from either heavy or explosive strength training; for cycling economy and performance, heavy strength training has the stronger support.
- One study in competitive cyclists found that replacing part of training with explosive single-leg jumps plus short high-resistance sprints improved 1-km and 4-km power by about 8–9% relative to controls over 4–5 weeks (18 riders).
- Typical dose: 2–3 exercises, 3 sets of 5–8 quality repetitions, full recovery (60–120 s), 1–2 times per week; 40–80 ground contacts per session for beginners.
- Exercises: squat jump, countermovement jump, box jump (step down), split jump, skipping/bounding, single-leg hops.
- Bone stimulus: brief impacts such as 10–20 jumps a day or regular running/skipping are often recommended for bone health in cyclists.

## In practice
- Add after a base of general strength (at least 4–6 weeks of lifting).
- Do them fresh, at the start of a gym session after warm-up; quality over quantity.
- Land softly, knees tracking over toes; stop when height or speed drops.
- Suitable for sprinters, criterium, track and cyclocross riders, and for masters riders mainly for bone and power maintenance, with gentle progression.
- Not for riders with current knee, Achilles or back problems without professional guidance.

## Limits
Cycling-specific evidence is sparse and mixed; injury risk is higher than with controlled lifting when progressed too fast.

## Coach guidance
Present as optional. For bone health, suggest some regular impact activity and, where there are risk factors (low energy intake, prior stress fracture, long-term high-volume cycling), a medical check.
