## Summary
Strength training is built in the off-season and base period, when bike intensity is low, and maintained with a small dose through the rest of the year. Heavy lifting and hard intervals compete for recovery, so their placement in the week matters. The "interference effect", endurance training blunting strength gains, is real for maximal strength and muscle growth but is of little concern for cyclists, whose aim is enough strength, not maximal.

## Key numbers
- Annual plan: introduction 2–4 weeks (late off-season) → build 8–12 weeks at 2 sessions/week (base period) → maintenance 1 session/week (build and race season) → stop 7–10 days before A events.
- Separation: at least 6 h between a lifting session and a bike session where either needs quality; ideally lifting later the same day as a hard ride, or on an easy day.
- Avoid: heavy legs session within about 24–36 h before key intervals or a long hard ride.
- Soreness in the first 2–3 weeks reduces bike power; schedule the introduction when bike training is easy.
- Strength gains are lost within about 8 weeks without maintenance.

## Weekly examples
- Base, 8 h rider: Mon gym · Tue intervals · Wed easy · Thu gym + easy spin · Sat long ride · Sun easy.
- Hard-day stacking: Tue morning intervals, Tue evening gym; Wed easy. Keeps easy days truly easy.
- Time-limited: one 30-min session per week still maintains most benefit.

## In practice
- In build and race phases, reduce gym volume (sets) and keep the load.
- If total fatigue is high, drop a set before dropping the session.
- Eat protein and carbohydrate after combined days.
- Masters riders may keep 2 sessions per week year-round and trim bike intensity instead.

## Limits
Optimal ordering (bike first or gym first) has little direct evidence in cyclists; recommendations combine molecular studies with practice.

## Coach guidance
Place strength sessions in the weekly plan explicitly so they do not collide with key rides. When a rider reports dead legs in intervals, check where the gym session sits.
