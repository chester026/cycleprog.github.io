## Summary
Strength built in winter fades over a racing season if lifting stops, and the performance benefits fade with it. A single short heavy session per week is enough to hold the gains. In a study of well-trained cyclists, those who continued one session weekly through the competition period kept their strength and showed better power development than those who stopped.

## Key numbers
- Dose: 1 session per week (every 7–10 days at minimum).
- Content: 2–3 leg exercises, 2 sets of 4–6 reps at about 80–85% of 1RM, stopping 1–2 reps short of failure; 20–30 min total.
- In the maintenance study (13 weeks of competition season after a 12-week build): the lifting group retained strength and thigh muscle gains and improved 40-min time-trial power and peak power more than the endurance-only group (from memory).
- Without maintenance: strength returns toward baseline within roughly 8–12 weeks.
- Before key events: last session 7–10 days out for A events, 3–5 days for lesser ones.

## In practice
- Keep the load heavy and cut the volume; light "toning" sessions do not maintain maximal strength.
- Place the session early in the week after a weekend race, or on the day of a hard ride.
- During stage races, camps and peak weeks, skip without guilt and resume afterwards.
- Riders who race every weekend: Tuesday lifting plus one bike quality day works for most.
- If only bodyweight options are available while travelling, single-leg squats, step-ups and jumps bridge 1–2 weeks.

## Limits
Evidence comes from few small studies; the minimal dose for masters riders is probably higher (they lose strength faster).

## Coach guidance
Keep one strength session in the plan through the season unless the rider objects. Frame it as protecting winter work.
