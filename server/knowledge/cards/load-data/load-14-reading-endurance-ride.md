## Summary
An endurance ride has a different success definition from an interval session: accumulate time at LOW intensity, stay there, and finish without excessive strain. The analysis looks at duration, how much of the time was truly LOW, how smooth the effort was, heart-rate drift and fuelling. An easy ride is a deliberate training choice and is assessed against its own purpose.

## Key numbers
- Intensity: most of the moving time below LT1 (about 55–75% FTP); IF typically 0.55–0.72.
- Time above LT1: under about 10% keeps the ride easy in recovery terms; hilly routes may reach 15–25% without changing its character much.
- Variability index 1.00–1.08 on flat or indoor rides; up to 1.15 on rolling terrain. Higher values mean surging.
- Decoupling under about 5% in cool conditions: duration well tolerated (see `load-06-hr-drift-decoupling`).
- Coasting above about 15% of ride time reduces the effective training time; note it for descents and group rides.
- Carbohydrate: 30–60 g/h for rides over 90 min; more for 3 h+.

## In practice
- Report: duration and kJ, share of time in LOW, drift, and whether the ride extended the rider's recent longest duration.
- Progress markers over weeks: longer duration at the same drift, more power at the same heart rate, less fade in the last hour.
- Short hard efforts inside the ride (town signs, short hills) do not spoil it; note them only if they are numerous enough to change the recovery cost.
- Late-ride power fade with rising heart rate usually points to fuelling or heat before fitness.

## Limits
Without a tested LT1 the LOW boundary is an estimate. Group rides and mountainous routes cannot be judged by the same thresholds.

## Coach guidance
Never mark an easy ride as weak, lazy or below potential. Describe what it contributed (aerobic volume, recovery, durability) and, where relevant, one thing to try next time (longer, steadier, better fuelled).
