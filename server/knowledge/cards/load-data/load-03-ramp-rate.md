## Summary
Load should rise gradually enough that adaptation keeps up. In cycling this is usually tracked as the weekly change in CTL or in hours. There are no trial-validated thresholds; the numbers below are coaching conventions that work as guard rails. Cycling tolerates faster volume progression than running because there is no impact loading, so the main risks are accumulated fatigue and illness rather than injury.

## Key numbers
- CTL ramp: +3 to +5 per week sustainable for most amateurs; +5 to +8 aggressive, for a few weeks or a camp; above +8 only short blocks followed by recovery.
- Hours: increase weekly volume by roughly 5–15%; larger jumps are tolerable if the added time is strictly LOW.
- Structure: 2–3 building weeks followed by 1 easier week at 50–70% of the previous load (see periodization cards).
- After a break of 2+ weeks: restart at about 50–70% of the previous load and rebuild over a similar number of weeks.

## In practice
- Add one variable at a time: hours first, then intensity. Raising both together is the common route to overreaching.
- Beginners and masters riders sit at the low end of the ranges; riders returning to a previously held load can ramp faster.
- Watch the response, not only the plan: falling power at usual heart rate, poor sleep, unusual soreness or mood change mean the ramp is too steep regardless of the numbers.

## Limits
The classic "10% rule" has no direct experimental support. Individual tolerance varies several-fold. Training camps deliberately break these limits and work when followed by adequate recovery.

## Coach guidance
Present ramp limits as guard rails, not targets. When a rider's load has jumped sharply, mention it once with a concrete suggestion (an easier 3–4 days), without alarm.
