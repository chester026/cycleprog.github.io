## Summary
Durability is the ability to keep power, thresholds and efficiency from deteriorating during prolonged exercise. Standard tests measure a fresh rider; long events are decided in a fatigued state. In professional cycling, fresh power profiles barely separate more and less successful riders, whereas the drop in 5- and 20-min power after large amounts of work does. Durability is trainable and largely independent of fresh FTP.

## Key numbers
- Common field measure: best 1-, 5-, 20-min power after 1500, 2000, 2500, 3000 kJ (or after 20–50 kJ/kg) compared with fresh bests.
- Professionals: after about 50 kJ/kg, successful climbers lost clearly less 5- and 20-min power than less successful ones; fresh values did not differ.
- Amateurs: declines of 5–15% in 20-min power after 2000–3000 kJ are common; under 5% indicates strong durability.
- The intensity of prior work matters: the same kJ with more time above threshold causes larger declines.

## Why it varies
Glycogen availability, fat oxidation capacity, muscle fibre fatigue resistance, heat and hydration, and fuelling during the ride. Higher carbohydrate intake reduces the decline.

## In practice
- Build "fatigued bests" from long rides and races: 5- and 20-min efforts placed late (after 2+ h).
- Training that targets it: long rides, quality efforts at the end of long rides, back-to-back long days, strength training, sufficient weekly volume.
- For event planning, pace from fatigued power, not fresh FTP: late-climb targets 5–10% below fresh values are realistic for most amateurs.

## Limits
Field measures depend on motivation and fuelling on the day; few riders have true maximal efforts late in rides. No standard protocol or norms exist yet. Evidence in amateurs and women is limited.

## Coach guidance
When a rider's long-event results lag behind their FTP, look at fatigued power before prescribing more threshold work. Always check fuelling on the rides being compared.
