## Summary
A change is real when it exceeds the normal variation of the measurement and repeats. Maximal cycling efforts vary by a few percent from day to day even with no change in fitness, and power meters add their own error. So a single 2% improvement or decline means little; a 3–5% shift confirmed on more than one occasion, or supported by several independent markers, means something.

## Key numbers
- Day-to-day variation of time-trial-type power in trained cyclists: about 1.5–3%; larger in less experienced riders and for sprints.
- Power meter accuracy: typically ±1–2% for the same device; differences between devices can reach 3–5%.
- Practical threshold: treat changes under about 2–3% as noise; 3–5% as probable; above 5% as clear, provided conditions were comparable.
- Typical rates: beginners can gain 10–20% FTP in the first year; trained riders 2–5% per season; highly trained riders 1–2%.

## In practice
- Triangulate: best power at 5 s, 1, 5, 20, 60 min over rolling 6-week windows; power at a fixed submaximal heart rate; durability; RPE at standard sessions.
- A new best is only informative if the rider attempted a maximal effort of that duration. Missing bests usually mean no attempt, not lost fitness.
- Compare the same season phase year on year as well as month on month.
- Body mass changes alter W/kg without any change in watts; state which moved.
- Signs FTP is outdated: threshold intervals completed with low RPE and low heart rate, repeated bests at 20–60 min above current FTP, or sweet-spot sessions repeatedly failed.

## Limits
Outdoor conditions (wind, temperature, drafting, altitude) can shift results by more than a season's training gain.

## Coach guidance
Show progress as a direction with dynamics over time, and state confidence in plain words ("clear", "probable", "too early to tell"). A plateau is described neutrally with what changed in training.
