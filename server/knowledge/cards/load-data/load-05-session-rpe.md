## Summary
Session RPE asks one question about 30 minutes after training: how hard was the whole session, on a 0–10 scale? Multiplying by duration in minutes gives a load value. It correlates well with heart-rate and power-based load, costs nothing, and captures what objective metrics miss: heat, poor sleep, stress, under-fuelling. The gap between RPE and power is itself useful information.

## Key numbers
- Scale (CR-10): 1–2 very easy, 3 moderate, 4 somewhat hard, 5–6 hard, 7–8 very hard, 9–10 maximal.
- Load = RPE × minutes (e.g. 90 min at RPE 6 = 540 units).
- Rough correspondence: LOW rides RPE 2–3; MID sessions 4–6; HIGH sessions 7–9.
- Monotony = weekly mean daily load / standard deviation; values above about 2 with high load have been linked to illness and overreaching in observational work.

## In practice
- Same session, same power, RPE up by 2 points or more than usual: fatigue, illness onset, heat or low glycogen. Repeated over several days, that warrants an easier period.
- Same RPE at higher power over weeks: real progress.
- For rides without power or with unreliable heart rate (mountain bike, commuting, other sports), sRPE is the best available load estimate.

## Limits
Ratings are affected by the last part of the session, by mood and by experience with the scale. Riders tend to rate easy sessions harder and hard sessions easier than the coach intended.

## Coach guidance
Ask how a session felt whenever data look ambiguous. Treat a rider's perceived effort as valid data, not as something the numbers overrule.
