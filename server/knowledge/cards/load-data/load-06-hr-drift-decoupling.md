## Summary
During a long steady ride heart rate rises gradually at constant power (cardiovascular drift). Decoupling expresses this as the percentage change in the power-to-heart-rate ratio between the first and second half of a steady effort. Low drift at a given intensity and duration indicates good aerobic endurance for that duration; it is a practical field marker, not a validated test.

## Key numbers
- Decoupling (Pw:HR) = (ratio first half − ratio second half) / ratio first half × 100.
- Under about 5% on a steady LOW ride: aerobically well coped with at that duration (coaching convention).
- 5–10%: at the edge; above 10%: the duration or intensity exceeded current endurance, or conditions interfered.
- Heat can add 5–15 beats/min of drift on its own; dehydration of 2% body mass adds several beats.

## Why it happens
Plasma volume falls with sweating, core temperature rises, stroke volume drops and heart rate compensates. Glycogen depletion and recruitment of less efficient fibres raise oxygen cost at the same power.

## In practice
- Valid only for steady efforts of 60+ min with little coasting: flat or indoor rides, long climbs. Exclude warm-up.
- Compare like with like: same power range, similar temperature, similar fuelling.
- Use to extend long rides: when a 2.5 h ride shows under 5%, extend duration before raising intensity.
- If drift exceeds about 5% at a power assumed to be LOW in cool conditions, the intensity may be above LT1.

## Limits
Caffeine, stress, altitude, illness and poor sleep all shift heart rate. Optical wrist sensors are unreliable on the bike. The 5% threshold has no experimental validation.

## Coach guidance
Check temperature and fuelling before drawing conclusions. Report drift as a trend over several comparable rides, not from one ride.
