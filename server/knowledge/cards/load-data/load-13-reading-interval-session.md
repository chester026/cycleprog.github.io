## Summary
An interval session is judged per work block: was the target power reached, was it held evenly within and across blocks, and how did heart rate and effort respond. Whole-ride averages hide all of this. The useful output is a short verdict per block plus one conclusion about the session and one about the next target.

## Key numbers
- On target: average block power within about ±3% of the prescribed range for threshold and sweet spot, ±5% for VO2max and shorter efforts.
- Within-block pacing: first-third vs last-third power within about 5%. A start 10%+ above target followed by fade is a pacing error.
- Across blocks: last block within about 3–5% of the first indicates the target was right. All blocks above range with stable or rising power: target too low, raise 2–3% next time.
- Fade of more than 5–8% across blocks, or blocks cut short: target too high, fatigue, heat or under-fuelling.
- Heart rate: in HIGH intervals of 3+ min, reaching about 90%+ of HRmax by the later blocks confirms the stimulus. Heart rate failing to rise with high RPE suggests fatigue.
- Recovery valleys: 40–55% FTP is normal; shortened or skipped recoveries change the session.

## In practice
- Detect blocks from the data (sustained power well above surrounding riding), then compare with the planned structure if there is one.
- Exceeding the target range with good form is a positive result and a reason to raise the target. Only falling short is flagged.
- Distinguish indoor from outdoor: outdoor blocks on rolling terrain show more variability at the same quality.
- Cadence drop of 10+ rpm in later blocks is an early fatigue sign.

## Limits
Targets depend on a current FTP. Short intervals (under 60 s) need power, since heart rate lags. Terrain, traffic and wind explain many "failed" outdoor blocks.

## Coach guidance
Lead with what went well, give numbers per block, then one adjustment. Over-delivering is praised; under-delivering is explained with likely causes, never as a failure of the rider.
