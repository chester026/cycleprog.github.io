## Summary
In the critical power model a rider has a sustainable ceiling (critical power, CP) and a fixed amount of work available above it (W′, in kJ). W′ balance models how this reserve drains whenever power exceeds CP and refills below it. It explains why repeated surges exhaust a rider even when average power looks modest, and it is useful for analysing races and designing interval sessions.

## Key numbers
- W′ in trained cyclists: typically 10–25 kJ (sprinters and pursuiters higher, pure climbers lower).
- Draining: every second above CP costs (P − CP) joules. 60 s at CP + 150 W uses 9 kJ.
- Refilling is exponential and faster the further power drops below CP: roughly half restored in 2–4 min of easy pedalling, near-full in 8–15 min; slower when recovering just below CP.
- Recovery slows with repeated efforts and accumulated fatigue; models tend to be optimistic late in long rides.

## In practice
- Reaching W′bal near zero means the effort was truly maximal; a rider who "blew up" on a climb usually shows it.
- Negative W′bal in the data means CP or W′ is set too low; a hard race where W′bal never drops below 50% suggests they are set too high.
- Interval design: 5 × 3 min at 120% CP with 3 min recovery drains W′ progressively; shortening recovery raises the strain far more than adding 10 W.
- Race pacing: spend W′ where it buys time (steep sections, closing gaps), not on the flat lead-in.

## Limits
Requires accurate CP and W′ from recent maximal efforts of about 3–20 min; FTP is only a rough substitute for CP (usually a few percent lower). Different reconstitution models give different balances. Individual recovery kinetics vary.

## Coach guidance
Use W′bal to explain what happened in a ride, with rounded numbers. Avoid presenting it as a precise fuel gauge.
