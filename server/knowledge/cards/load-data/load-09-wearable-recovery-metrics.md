## Summary
Rings and watches report resting heart rate, sleep duration and stages, and composite scores (Oura Readiness, Garmin Body Battery and Stress, and similar). The raw signals, night-time resting heart rate and total sleep time, are reasonably accurate and useful. Sleep-stage breakdowns are approximate, and composite scores are proprietary blends without published validation against training outcomes. They are best used as prompts to check in, not as decisions.

## Key numbers
- Resting HR: normal day-to-day variation 2–4 beats/min. A rise of 5+ beats above the personal baseline for 2+ mornings commonly accompanies illness onset, heavy fatigue, dehydration, heat or alcohol.
- Sleep: most adults training regularly need 7–9 h; under 6 h for several nights measurably worsens perceived effort and recovery.
- Wearables estimate total sleep time within roughly 10–30 min on average; staging (deep/REM) agrees only moderately with lab polysomnography.
- Garmin Body Battery (0–100) and Stress are derived mainly from HRV and heart rate; Oura Readiness combines resting HR, HRV, temperature deviation, sleep and prior activity.

## In practice
- Read trends over 3–7 days and deviations from the rider's own baseline.
- Strongest practical signal: elevated resting HR + suppressed HRV + short sleep together. Then swap a HIGH session for LOW or rest.
- Body-temperature deviation of +0.5 °C or more with elevated resting HR often precedes illness by a day.
- A good score does not license extra intensity beyond the plan.

## Limits
Scores from different brands are not comparable. Algorithms change with firmware. A low score after a hard, well-executed training day is expected, not a problem.

## Coach guidance
Quote the underlying signal ("resting HR is 6 beats above your norm for three days") rather than the composite score. Health interpretation beyond training readiness is out of scope; persistent abnormalities go to a doctor.
